# ASSESSMENT OF TWO APPLICATIONS BY DEFENDANT NO. 5 (MUKESH GUPTA)
## CS(OS) 545/2026 — Rahul Gupta v. Kamal Gupta & Ors.

---

# APPLICATION 1: ORDER VII RULE 10 — RETURN OF PLAINT (Jurisdiction)
### Filed by: Defendant No. 5 (Mukesh Gupta) through Shreya Singh

---

## WHAT IT SAYS

**Two Grounds:**

**Ground I — Artificial Valuation:**
- The Rs. 2,00,00,000 valuation under "compensation / possession by allotment of alternate properties" (Para 64 of plaint) is artificial
- No prayer in the plaint corresponds to this valuation head
- This figure was inserted solely to cross the Rs. 2 Cr pecuniary threshold for DHC Original Side
- Without this artificial head, suit falls below DHC jurisdiction

**Ground II — Commercial Courts Act:**
- Properties like 2129-2130 KBG, Nai Sarak, Gurugram showroom are commercial properties used exclusively in trade/commerce
- Disputes relating to such properties = "commercial dispute" under S.2(1)(c)(vii) Commercial Courts Act, 2015
- Suit should have been filed as a commercial suit before the Commercial Division, not as ordinary civil suit

---

## WHY IT MUST FAIL

### A. On Pecuniary Jurisdiction — The Valuation is NOT Artificial

**1.** The plaint values the suit at Rs. 2,00,01,300/- (not just Rs. 2 Cr). The valuation includes MULTIPLE heads — partition of the entire PPJ Corpus, compensation for alienated properties, mesne profits, rendition of accounts, injunction. The Rs. 2 Cr "alternate properties" head is ONE of several.

**2.** Even if this ONE head is excluded, the suit clearly exceeds Rs. 2 Cr:
- The plaint itself discloses properties worth Rs. 81 Cr under Prayer C alone
- The PPJ Corpus is valued at over Rs. 1,327 Cr; 23% = ~Rs. 305 Cr
- Rs. 60 Cr worth of properties have been alienated (compensation claim)
- The valuation is for COURT FEE purposes (fixed court fee for partition), not for quantifying the actual claim

**3.** The Plaintiff has the RIGHT to determine the valuation of his suit. The Defendant cannot dictate valuation at this stage.

**Case Law:**
- **Khatri Hotels Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India, (2011) 9 SCC 126:** The plaintiff has the right to value the suit for purposes of jurisdiction and court fee. The court cannot reject the valuation at threshold unless it is patently absurd.
- **Sunit Gupta v. Deepika Singhania, 2024 SCC OnLine Del 4268:** Plaintiff is dominus litis and has the prerogative to determine the forum by appropriate valuation of the suit.

**4.** The plaint DOES disclose the basis for the Rs. 2 Cr head — Para 606 states that properties allocated to the Plaintiff have been alienated, and the Plaintiff reserves the right to seek compensation/allotment of equivalent properties. The valuation is NOT "artificial" — it represents a genuine relief head.

### B. On Commercial Courts Act — A Partition Suit is NOT a "Commercial Dispute"

**5.** This is the WEAKEST argument in the entire application. A suit for PARTITION between family members is NOT a "commercial dispute" under the Commercial Courts Act, 2015. The Act applies to disputes arising from COMMERCIAL TRANSACTIONS, not to family partition merely because some properties happen to be commercial.

**6.** Section 2(1)(c)(vii) applies to: *"agreements relating to immovable property used exclusively in trade or commerce."* The present suit is NOT based on a "commercial agreement" — it is based on an **Oral Family Settlement** for partition of family assets. The nature of the DISPUTE (family partition), not the nature of the PROPERTY (commercial), determines whether the Commercial Courts Act applies.

**Case Law:**
- **Ambalal Sarabhai Enterprise v. K.S. Infraspace LLP, (2020) 15 SCC 585:** It is the nature of the dispute and its connection with trade/commerce that determines whether it qualifies as a "commercial dispute." A dispute between family members regarding partition of assets does not become a "commercial dispute" merely because the assets include commercial properties.
- **Vasu Healthcare Pvt. Ltd. v. Gujarat Akruti TCG Biotech Ltd., (2017) 4 SCC 668:** The Commercial Courts Act is designed for disputes arising out of ordinary commercial transactions between parties engaged in trade and commerce, not for disputes of a personal/family nature.

**7.** CONTRADICTORY POSITIONS BY DEFENDANTS:
- **Kamal Gupta's O7R11 (court fee)** treats the suit as a PARTITION suit (argues it should attract ad valorem fee because Plaintiff seeks possession in a partition)
- **Mukesh Gupta's O7R10** treats the suit as a COMMERCIAL suit (argues it should go to Commercial Division)
- **Mukesh Gupta's O7R11** treats it as enforcement of ORAL AGREEMENT (argues it's time-barred)

**The Defendants cannot simultaneously argue:** (a) it's a partition suit where wrong court fee was paid, (b) it's a commercial suit that must go to Commercial Division, AND (c) it's an enforcement suit that's time-barred. These are mutually contradictory positions.

**8.** Even under the Commercial Courts Act, the pecuniary threshold for commercial suits on the Original Side is the SAME as for ordinary civil suits. Even if the suit were somehow commercial in nature, it would still be within DHC jurisdiction — the plaint would simply need to be re-registered as a commercial suit under CS(COMM) instead of CS(OS). Return of plaint is NOT warranted — at most, conversion of designation.

### C. The Application is a Dilatory Tactic

**9.** Filed by Defendant No. 5 (Mukesh Gupta) through a DIFFERENT lawyer (Shreya Singh, Tis Hazari) from Defendant No. 1 (Kamal Gupta, through Kartik Malhotra, G10 Law). This suggests a coordinated strategy to file MULTIPLE applications through different Defendants to delay the suit.

# APPLICATION 2: ORDER VII RULE 11(a) AND (d) — LIMITATION + NO CAUSE OF ACTION
### Filed by: Defendant No. 5 (Mukesh Gupta) through Shreya Singh

---

## WHAT IT SAYS

**Ground I — Suit is really for enforcement of oral agreement, not partition:**
- The "true nature" is enforcement of oral agreement dated 30.09.2014
- Plaintiff uses "partition" label to avoid limitation bar
- No relief claimed as coparcener; properties held by companies, not joint family
- No declaration of the oral agreement sought in prayers

**Ground II — Barred by Limitation (Articles 54/55/58 = 3 years):**
- Breach occurred in November 2015 (ouster from family premises) → limitation expired 2018
- At latest, MOU 2019 = date of repudiation → limitation expired July 2022
- Suit filed 17.06.2026 = hopelessly time-barred

**Ground III — No Cause of Action for Partition:**
- Not a Hindu Succession Act partition between coparceners
- Properties are held by companies — not joint Hindu family property
- Suit is for apportionment per oral agreement, not partition in law

---

## WHY IT MUST FAIL

### A. A Suit for Partition Has NO Limitation

**1.** This is the fatal flaw in the limitation argument. **There is NO limitation for a suit for partition by a co-owner.** The right to seek partition is an INHERENT right of a co-owner that subsists as long as co-ownership subsists.

**Case Law:**
- **K. Laxmanan v. Thekkayil Padmini, (2009) 1 SCC 354:** *"The right to claim partition is an inherent right of every co-owner. It is a right which cannot be taken away by limitation or by adverse possession of a co-owner."*
- **Muhammad Hussain v. Kishva Nand, (1937) PC (reported in AIR 1937 PC 250):** *"A co-owner's right to partition cannot be defeated by limitation."*

**2.** The application argues the suit is "not a partition suit." But the plaint says it IS a partition suit (Para 603: "suit by a co-owner and co-sharer in joint and constructive possession seeking partition by metes and bounds"). Under O.VII R.11, the plaint's averments must be taken as true — not the Defendant's characterization.

### B. Even if Treated as "Enforcement" — Section 18 Limitation Act Saves It

**3.** Even assuming (without admitting) that the suit is purely for "enforcement of oral agreement" with a 3-year limitation — **Section 18 of the Limitation Act provides that every acknowledgment of a right restarts limitation.**

**The Defendants have acknowledged the 23% share on MULTIPLE occasions AFTER 2014:**

| Date | Acknowledgment | Effect |
|------|---------------|--------|
| 20.06.2015 | Memorandum 2015 — records 23% allocation | Restarts limitation |
| 09.07.2019 | MOU 2019 — reiterates 23% share | Restarts limitation |
| 22.03.2024 | **Undertaking recorded in Para 18 of DHC order in Arb.P. 1010/2022** | **Restarts limitation** |
| 2024 | Written submissions in IA 39500/2024: *"Shri Kamal Gupta undertakes to abide by both the aforesaid undertakings"* | Restarts limitation |
| 2024 | Reply before Arbitrator — acknowledges 23% ratio, vault of Plaintiff's choice | Restarts limitation |
| 13.08.2025 | SC judgment — directs parties to work out rights per 22.03.2024 order | Confirms subsistence |

**From the LAST acknowledgment (22.03.2024 undertaking) → 3 years = 22.03.2027.**
**Suit filed 17.06.2026 = WELL WITHIN LIMITATION even on 3-year computation.**

The application **completely ignores Section 18 Limitation Act** and the post-2019 acknowledgments. This is a material suppression.

### C. The Cause of Action is CONTINUING

**4.** The plaint explicitly pleads a continuing cause of action:

- **16.01.2026:** Property at R-2, Green Park Main sold — AFTER the undertaking before DHC
- **2026:** Defendant No. 1 in Transfer Petition (Criminal) No. 52/2026 now claims PP Jewellers Delhi belongs exclusively to him — directly contrary to the family settlement
- **2025-2026:** NCLT/NCLAT proceedings where Defendants suppressed the oral settlement
- **April 2026:** Fresh cause of action from CM(M)-IPD 26/2025 trademark dispute
- **June 2026:** Obstruction of Rawta land registration before SDM Kapashera

**Each new alienation, each contradictory stand, each fresh obstruction constitutes a FRESH cause of action.** The suit is not based solely on the 2014 settlement — it is based on continuing denial and ongoing dissipation.

**Case Law:**
- **State of Bihar v. Deokaran Nenshi, (1972) 2 SCC 890:** Where the cause of action is continuing, the suit is not barred by limitation merely because the initial wrong occurred outside the limitation period.

### D. The Suit DOES Disclose a Cause of Action

**5.** The application's argument under O.VII R.11(a) (no cause of action) is meritless:

- The plaint discloses: (a) Oral Family Settlement of 30.09.2014 allocating 23% to Plaintiff; (b) Memorandum 2015 and MOU 2019 recording the same; (c) Undertaking before DHC on 22.03.2024; (d) SC direction of 13.08.2025; (e) Failure to implement; (f) Continuing alienation of corpus
- This is a CLEAR cause of action — both for partition AND for enforcement
- Under O.VII R.11(a), the test is whether the plaint discloses a cause of action **IF THE AVERMENTS ARE TAKEN AS TRUE** — not whether the Defendant disputes the averments
- **Liverpool & London S.P. & I Association Ltd. v. M.V. Sea Success I, (2004) 9 SCC 512:** A plaint discloses a cause of action if it contains every fact which is necessary for the plaintiff to prove to support his right to the judgment

**6.** The argument that "no declaration of the oral agreement is sought" is irrelevant:
- Prayer A seeks to "give effect to the Plaintiff's antecedent and crystallised title to a 23% share" — this is functionally a declaration
- More importantly, the 23% share has been **ADMITTED** by Defendants — an admitted fact needs no declaration
- Order XII Rule 6 CPC (which the Plaintiff has already filed) precisely relies on these admissions

### E. "Not a Hindu Coparcenary Partition" — Red Herring

**7.** The application argues this is not a coparcenary partition under Hindu Succession Act. This is a RED HERRING:

- A "partition" need NOT be a coparcenary partition under HSA — any co-owner has the right to seek partition
- A **family settlement** creating defined shares IS a partition — it creates co-ownership with defined shares
- The Defendants themselves executed the Memorandum 2015 and MOU 2019 which divide assets in specific ratios (Kamal 18%, Pawan 23%, Rahul 23%, Mukesh 18%, Mohit 18%) — this IS a partition arrangement
- The fact that properties are held through companies doesn't prevent partition — the beneficial ownership is what matters, and the family settlement allocated beneficial interests

**Case Law:**
- **Kale v. Deputy Director of Consolidation, (1976) 3 SCC 119:** A family arrangement need not conform to any particular form or be under any particular statutory framework. It is binding on the parties even if it does not comply with requirements of registration or other formalities.

### F. MATERIAL SUPPRESSION in the Limitation Application

**8.** The application suppresses the following material facts:

|-----------------|-----------|

| SC judgment dt. 13.08.2025 directing rights per 22.03.2024 | Confirms subsistence of rights |
