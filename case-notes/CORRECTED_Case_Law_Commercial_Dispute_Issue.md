## WHAT WAS WRONG IN PREVIOUS CITATIONS

### 1. Ambalal Sarabhai Enterprise Ltd. v. K.S. Infraspace LLP, (2020) 15 SCC 585
**Citation number is CORRECT. But the holding I attributed was WRONG.**

**What I wrongly said:** "The nature of the dispute (not the property) determines whether it's 'commercial.' A dispute between family members regarding division of assets is NOT a commercial dispute merely because the assets include commercial properties."

**What the case ACTUALLY held:**
- *"A dispute relating to immovable property per se may not be a commercial dispute. But it becomes a commercial dispute, if it falls under sub-clause (vii) of Section 2(1)(c) of the Act viz. 'agreements relating to immovable property used exclusively in trade or commerce'."*
- The word **"used" means "ACTUALLY USED"** — it cannot mean "ready for use" or "likely to be used" or "to be used"
- The plaint must specifically show that the immovable property was ACTUALLY USED exclusively in trade/commerce
- **The case is about a land sale/specific performance dispute — NOT about family disputes or partition**

**However, the CORRECT holding is STILL useful because:**
- The PPJ Corpus includes residential properties (C-19 RPB, Green Park flat), agricultural lands (Rawta, Daulatpur, Qadipur) — these are NOT "actually used exclusively in trade or commerce"
- Even the commercial properties (2129-2130 KBG) are the subject of a FAMILY PARTITION, not a commercial agreement
- Per Ambalal Sarabhai, the plaint must show the property was "actually used exclusively" in trade — Mukesh Gupta's application does NOT demonstrate this for ALL suit properties

## CORRECT CASE LAWS FOR "FAMILY PARTITION ≠ COMMERCIAL DISPUTE"

### A. Supreme Court Judgments

**1. Ambalal Sarabhai Enterprise Ltd. v. K.S. Infraspace LLP, (2020) 15 SCC 585** (CORRECTED use)
- *"A dispute relating to immovable property per se may not be a commercial dispute."*
- Property must be "ACTUALLY USED EXCLUSIVELY" in trade/commerce
- **Use it for:** The suit involves mixed properties (residential, agricultural, commercial). The DISPUTE is a family partition, not a commercial transaction. The properties are not ALL "actually used exclusively" in trade/commerce.

**2. Money Recovery Case — SC (Justice Hrishikesh Roy & Justice Prashant Kumar Mishra, 2024)**
- *"Merely because the dispute is related to an immovable property wouldn't per se make it a commercial dispute under the Commercial Courts Act, 2015 unless the immovable property is 'actually used' exclusively in trade or commerce."*
- Followed Ambalal Sarabhai
- **Use it for:** D5 argues commercial properties make this a commercial dispute. SC says: the property must be "actually used exclusively" — not that any property involved in the dispute makes it commercial.

**3. Kale v. Deputy Director of Consolidation, (1976) 3 SCC 119** (Indirectly supports)
- *"Family arrangements are governed by principles which are not applicable to dealings between strangers."*
- Family settlements operate on principles of equity, good faith and family harmony — fundamentally different from commercial arm's-length transactions
- **Use it for:** The "agreement" here is a family settlement — governed by Kale principles, not commercial law. Section 2(1)(c)(vii) covers "agreements relating to immovable property" — this family settlement is NOT a commercial "agreement" within the statutory meaning.

**4. H. Srinivas Pai & Anr. v. H.V. Pai (D) Thr. LRs. & Ors., (2010) Supreme Court**
- Karnataka HC Division Bench observed: *"The right claimed by the respondent in the original suit for partition of the joint family properties, is a civil dispute, which does not attract the provisions of the Act."*
- Though SC reversed on a different point (arbitrability), the characterization of partition as a "civil dispute" — as opposed to commercial — was NOT disturbed.
- **Use with caution:** The SC corrected the HC on the specific issue of arbitrability. But the broader principle — that family partition is a civil dispute — supports our argument.

### B. High Court Judgments (Persuasive)

**5. Vasu Healthcare Pvt. Ltd. v. Gujarat Akruti TCG Biotech Ltd., AIR 2017 Gujarat 153 (Gujarat HC, Justice M.R. Shah — now SC Judge)**
- Section 2(1)(c)(vii) requires property to be "ACTUALLY USED" exclusively in trade/commerce
- Gave strict interpretation to the statute
- SLP dismissed by SC, effectively approving the Gujarat HC position
- Later expressly cited with approval in Ambalal Sarabhai (2020) 15 SCC 585

**6. Bombay HC (May 2026) — Mediation Cannot Be Forced in Family Disputes Not Governed by Commercial Courts Act**
- Bombay HC held: Family disputes, even involving commercial properties, are not governed by the Commercial Courts Act
- Mediation under Section 12A of the Commercial Courts Act does not apply to family disputes
- **Use it for:** Even the procedural provisions of CCA (like mandatory mediation) do not apply to family disputes — a fortiori, the substantive jurisdiction provisions don't either

**7. Delhi HC — CS(OS) 141/2022, Neelam Bhatia & Ors. v. Jaswant Singh (January 2026)**
- Partition suit for family property — Court rejected O7R11 application
- Held that "in a suit for partition, the matter has to be comprehensively adjudicated"
- NOT treated as commercial suit despite properties having commercial character

### C. Textual / Statutory Argument (STRONGEST)

**8. Section 2(1)(c)(vii) — Plain Language Argument**
- The provision covers: *"agreements relating to immovable property used exclusively in trade or commerce"*
- THREE requirements must ALL be met:
  - (i) There must be an **"agreement"** — A family settlement under Kale principles is NOT a commercial "agreement"
  - (ii) The agreement must **"relate to"** immovable property — The family settlement relates to DIVISION OF FAMILY ASSETS, not to the immovable property qua property
  - (iii) The property must be **"used exclusively in trade or commerce"** — Per Ambalal Sarabhai, "actually used." Many suit properties (C-19 RPB residence, Green Park flat, Rawta agricultural land, Daulatpur agricultural land) are NOT used in trade/commerce at all
- **If even ONE requirement fails, the provision doesn't apply.** Here, ALL THREE fail.

## CORRECTED READY-TO-USE ARGUMENT

*"My Lords, the Commercial Courts Act argument is misconceived for three reasons:*

*First, Section 2(1)(c)(vii) requires the property to be 'actually used exclusively in trade or commerce' — as held by the Supreme Court in Ambalal Sarabhai (2020) 15 SCC 585. The PPJ Corpus includes C-19 RPB (family residence), Green Park (residential flat), Rawta (agricultural land), Daulatpur (agricultural land) — none of these are 'actually used exclusively in trade or commerce.'*

*Second, Section 2(1)(c)(vii) covers 'agreements relating to immovable property' — meaning commercial agreements like leases, development agreements, or sale contracts. A family settlement under Kale v. DDC (1976) 3 SCC 119 is governed by special principles not applicable to strangers' dealings. It is NOT a 'commercial agreement' within the meaning of the Act.*

*Third, even if some properties have commercial character, the DISPUTE is not commercial. This is a family partition between brothers — not an arm's-length commercial transaction. As the Supreme Court noted in Ambalal Sarabhai, 'a dispute relating to immovable property per se may not be a commercial dispute.'*

*In any event, even if this were somehow held to be a 'commercial dispute,' the remedy is not return of plaint under O7R10. The suit would simply be re-designated as CS(COMM) within this very Court — the pecuniary jurisdiction remains with the Delhi High Court."*
