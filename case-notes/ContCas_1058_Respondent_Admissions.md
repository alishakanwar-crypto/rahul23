### Section 17, Indian Evidence Act — Statements by Parties Admissible Against Them

---

## LEGAL FRAMEWORK

Under **Section 17 of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872**, an admission is a statement — oral, documentary, or contained in electronic form — which suggests any inference as to any fact in issue or relevant fact, and which is made by any person and under the circumstances set out in Sections 18-23.

**Key Principle:** Statements made by parties in their own pleadings, affidavits, applications, and replies in other proceedings are admissible as **own admissions** against them in subsequent proceedings. These are substantive pieces of evidence and not merely corroborative. (*Nagindas Ramdas v. Dalpatram Ichharam, (1974) 1 SCC 242*)

## F. APPLICABLE CASE LAW

1. **Nagindas Ramdas v. Dalpatram Ichharam, (1974) 1 SCC 242** — Admissions are substantive evidence; the best evidence against the party making them.
2. **Basant Singh v. Janki Singh, (1967) 2 SCR 77** — Statements in pleadings in one case are admissible as admissions in another.
3. **Sita Ram v. Radha Bai, AIR 1968 SC 534** — Admissions in earlier proceedings can be used against the maker in subsequent proceedings.
4. **Bharat Singh v. Bhagirathi, (1966) 1 SCR 606** — Party cannot resile from own admission unless shown to be incorrect.
5. **Ranganayakamma v. K.S. Prakash, (2008) 15 SCC 673** — In partition suits, admissions regarding family property character are binding.
