# PART A: O7R10 APPLICATION — ARTIFICIAL VALUATION / PECUNIARY JURISDICTION

## Issue 1: Whether the Plaintiff's Valuation is "Artificial" to Confer Jurisdiction

### Our Position: Plaintiff is dominus litis — has the right to value the suit

**1. Khatri Hotels Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India, (2011) 9 SCC 126**
- The plaintiff has the right to value the suit for purposes of jurisdiction and court fee
- Court cannot reject the valuation at threshold unless it is **patently absurd**
- At threshold stage, valuation must be accepted unless manifestly unreasonable

**2. Tara Devi v. Sri Thakur Radha Krishna Maharaj, (1987) 4 SCC 69**
- *"The valuation in the plaint, unless shown to be arbitrary or mala fide, should be accepted for purposes of jurisdiction."*
- Burden is on the Defendant to show that the valuation is not just wrong but **arbitrary and mala fide**

**3. Sunit Gupta v. Deepika Singhania, 2024 SCC OnLine Del 4268 (Delhi HC)**
- Plaintiff is **dominus litis** and has the prerogative to determine the forum by appropriate valuation of the suit
- The defendant cannot dictate valuation at this stage

**4. Supreme Court in Civil Appeal arising from Order dated 20.06.2022 (Justice Dinesh Maheshwari & Justice Vikram Nath Bench)**
- *"It remains trite that it is the nature of relief claimed in the plaint which is decisive of the question of suit valuation. As a necessary corollary, the market value does not become decisive of suit valuation merely because an immovable property is the subject-matter of litigation."*
- The nature of relief — not market value of property — determines valuation
- **Directly applies here:** The Rs. 2 Cr "alternate properties" head is based on the nature of the relief (compensation for alienated properties worth Rs. 60 Cr), not the market value of properties

**5. M/s. Marg Limited v. Sushil Lalwani & Ors., 2026 INSC 402 (LATEST — April 2026)**
- *"The rejection of a plaint under Order VII Rule 11(b) or (c) is NOT automatic upon a finding of undervaluation or deficit court fee; rather, it is conditional upon non-compliance with the opportunity so granted by the Court."*
- **Two-step process mandated:** (i) Court forms opinion on undervaluation → (ii) Directs plaintiff to correct → (iii) ONLY on failure to comply → rejection
- Court cannot reject plaint outrightly for undervaluation without giving opportunity to cure
- **Directly answers Kamal Gupta's O7R11 AND the pecuniary jurisdiction issue** — even if valuation is found deficient, plaint cannot be rejected straightaway

# PART B: O7R11(a)(d) APPLICATION — LIMITATION & NO CAUSE OF ACTION

## Issue 2: No Limitation for a Suit for Partition by Co-Owner

### Our Position: Partition is an inherent right — NOT subject to limitation

**6. K. Laxmanan v. Thekkayil Padmini & Ors., (2009) 1 SCC 354**
- *"The right to claim partition is an inherent right of every co-owner."*
- This right cannot be taken away by limitation or by adverse possession of a co-owner
- **KEY PRINCIPLE:** As long as co-ownership subsists, the right to seek partition subsists — there is no starting point for limitation

**7. Muhammad Hussain v. Kishva Nand, AIR 1937 PC 250 (Privy Council)**
- A co-owner's right to partition cannot be defeated by limitation
- One of the earliest and most authoritative pronouncements on this principle

**8. Vidya Devi alias Vidya Vati (Dead) By LRs. v. Prem Prakash & Ors., 1995 INSC 361 (Supreme Court)**
- In partition suits under Delhi Land Reforms Act, *"the absence of a prescribed limitation period for filing a partition suit implies that co-bhumidars retain the perpetual right to seek partition"*
- A plea of adverse possession by a co-owner does not automatically oust the right to partition
- **Directly relevant:** Delhi property, co-ownership, no limitation

## Issue 3: Defendant Cannot Recharacterize the Suit as "Enforcement" to Import Limitation

### Our Position: Under O7R11, plaint must be taken at face value — not Defendant's characterization

**9. Dahiben v. Arvindbhai Kalyanji Bhanusali (Gajra)(D) Thr LRs., (2020) 7 SCC 366**
- *"Under Order VII Rule 11, a duty is cast on the Court to determine whether the plaint discloses a cause of action by scrutinizing the averments in the plaint, read in conjunction with the documents relied upon."*
- **Courts must DISREGARD the pleas taken by the defendant** in the written statement and application for rejection on merit
- The plaint must be construed **as it stands**, without addition or subtraction of words
- **Directly applies:** The plaint says "partition suit" (Para 603). The Defendant says "enforcement suit." Under Dahiben, the Court must go by the plaint.

**10. Liverpool & London S.P. & I Association Ltd. v. M.V. Sea Success I, (2004) 9 SCC 512**
- *"Whether a plaint discloses a cause of action or not is essentially a question of fact. But whether it does or does not must be found out from reading the plaint itself. For the said purpose, the averments made in the plaint in their entirety must be held to be correct. The test is as to whether if the averments made in the plaint are taken to be correct in their entirety, a decree would be passed."*
- This is the **gold standard test** for O7R11(a)

**11. Uma Devi & Ors. v. Anand Kumar & Ors., 2025 INSC 434 (Latest — April 2025)**
- SC dismissed a partition suit as barred by limitation — BUT only where:
  - Partition had already occurred 55 years ago
  - Registered sale deeds existed from 1978 providing constructive notice
  - Plaintiffs slept on rights for 45 years with NO intervening acknowledgments
- **Distinguishable from our case:** Here, Defendants ACKNOWLEDGED the 23% share as recently as 22.03.2024. No prior partition exists. Green Park sold just 5 months before suit.

## Issue 4: Section 18 Limitation Act — Acknowledgment Restarts Limitation

### Our Position: Even if 3-year limitation applies, last acknowledgment was 22.03.2024 → suit within time

**12. Khan Bahadur Shapoor Freedom Mazda v. Durga Prasad Chamaria, (1961) 1 SCR 140 / 1961 SCC OnLine SC 147**
- The earliest and most authoritative Supreme Court pronouncement on Section 18/19 (old Section 19)
- An acknowledgment need NOT amount to a promise to pay or perform — **mere affirmation of the existence of the liability/right is sufficient**
- Even a letter requesting restructuring of payment amounts to acknowledgment
- **Directly applies:** The Defendants' undertaking of 22.03.2024 before DHC acknowledges the 23% share

**13. Asset Reconstruction Company (India) Ltd. v. Bishal Jaiswal & Anr., (2021) 6 SCC 366**
- Supreme Court (3-Judge Bench) held: An entry in the balance sheet of a company acknowledging a liability constitutes a valid acknowledgment under Section 18
- *"When the principal borrower and/or the (corporate) guarantor admit and acknowledge their liability... it is not possible to extricate them from the renewed limitation accruing due to the effect of Section 18"*
- **Even statutory/compulsory documents** (like balance sheets prepared under Companies Act mandate) constitute valid acknowledgments
- **Relevance:** If even a mandated balance sheet entry suffices, a VOLUNTARY undertaking before a High Court (Para 18 of 22.03.2024 order) is an even stronger acknowledgment

**14. Kotak Mahindra Bank v. Girnar Corrugators, 2026 (Supreme Court — Latest)**
- Reaffirmed that: *"The period for filing an OA is three years from the date of accrual of the cause of action or from the date of the last valid acknowledgment, whichever is later."*
- Letters requesting reschedulement/restructuring = valid acknowledgment under Section 18
- **Principle:** Any written document signed by the party acknowledging the right/liability restarts limitation

**15. Section 18 — Critical Legal Point: Acknowledgment Must Be BEFORE Expiry**
- Section 18(1): *"Where, BEFORE the expiration of the prescribed period... an acknowledgment of liability... has been made in writing signed by the party..."*
- The chain of acknowledgments here is UNBROKEN:
  - 30.09.2014 (oral settlement) → 20.06.2015 (Memorandum — within 3 years) → 09.07.2019 (MOU — within 3 years of 2015) → 22.03.2024 (DHC undertaking — within 3 years of 2019) → Suit filed 17.06.2026 (within 3 years of 2024)
  - **Each acknowledgment was made BEFORE the previous limitation expired — chain is valid**

## Issue 5: Continuing Cause of Action — Section 22 Limitation Act

### Our Position: Fresh breaches/alienations = fresh cause of action at every moment

**16. Section 22 of the Limitation Act, 1963**
- *"In the case of a continuing breach of contract or in the case of a continuing tort, a fresh period of limitation begins to run at every moment of the time during which the breach or the tort, as the case may be, continues."*

**17. State of Bihar v. Deokaran Nenshi, (1972) 2 SCC 890**
- Where the cause of action is continuing, the suit is not barred merely because the initial wrong occurred outside the limitation period
- **Directly applies:** Even if ouster in Nov 2015 started the clock, the continuing denial + fresh alienations (Green Park sold 16.01.2026, Bahadurgarh sold 13.10.2021) restart limitation

**18. State of Punjab v. Darshan Singh, AIR 1996 SC 218**
- Continuing breach of contract allows fresh limitation while breach persists
- Failure to perform an ongoing obligation = continuing wrong

**19. Samruddhi Co-Op Housing Society v. Mumbai Mahalaxmi Construction, (2022) (Supreme Court — Justice D.Y. Chandrachud)**
- Failure to fulfill ongoing obligation (obtaining occupancy certificate) = continuing breach
- Limitation restarts at every moment the breach continues
- **Analogy:** Defendants' ongoing failure to implement the family settlement = continuing breach; each new alienation of corpus property = fresh breach

## Issue 6: Properties Held by Companies — Does This Defeat Partition?

### Our Position: Beneficial ownership through companies can still be subject of partition

**20. Shaifali Gupta v. Vidya Devi Gupta & Ors., 2025 INSC 739 (Supreme Court — May 2025, LATEST)**
- **DIRECTLY ON POINT.** Partition suit between family members. Defendant argued properties were held by companies/in individual names — not joint family property. Sought rejection under O7R11.
- SC held: *"A partition suit seeking division of joint family property CANNOT be dismissed under Order VII Rule 11 CPC at the preliminary stage when the plaintiff invokes an exception"*
- *"Determining whether properties are... joint family assets involves factual inquiries, such as the source of funds and family arrangements, necessitating a TRIAL"*
- **Key holding:** Whether properties held by companies are actually family properties = MIXED QUESTION OF FACT AND LAW → cannot be decided at threshold under O7R11 → requires trial
- **Dismisses the exact argument Mukesh Gupta is making** — that properties held by companies cannot be partitioned

**21. Sangram Singh P. Gaekwad v. Shantadevi P. Gaekwad (D) Through LRs., (2005) 11 SCC 314**
- In partition disputes, the corporate veil can be lifted to examine whether companies are merely vehicles for holding family property
- A company incorporated under Companies Act, though a separate legal entity, can be treated as a family asset where it is merely a holding vehicle
- **Directly applies:** PPJ Pvt. Ltd., PP Jewellers Retail, Venkatesh Jewellers etc. are all family-controlled companies — corporate veil argument available

## Issue 7: Family Settlement Binding Without Registration — Not "Oral Agreement" Requiring Declaration

### Our Position: Family settlement is self-contained — no separate declaration needed

**22. Kale v. Deputy Director of Consolidation, (1976) 3 SCC 119**
- The **locus classicus** on family settlements
- *"Family settlements are governed by principles which are not applicable to dealings between strangers"*
- A family arrangement need NOT conform to any particular form or statutory framework
- It is binding even if it does not comply with requirements of registration
- **Courts lean in favour of upholding family settlements** — even oral ones — because they promote family peace
- **KEY:** This is NOT a suit for specific performance of an "agreement" (Articles 54/55/58). This is a partition based on a FAMILY SETTLEMENT — governed by special principles

**23. H. Vasanthi v. A. Santha, 2025 (Supreme Court — Latest)**
- *"It is not compulsory that partition should be effected exclusively through a written instrument in strict accordance with the requirements of law"*
- **Partition can be achieved through settlement or verbal agreement**
- Oral partition, acted upon by family members, is valid and enforceable
- **Directly applies:** The oral family settlement of 30.09.2014, followed by Memorandum 2015 and MOU 2019, constitutes a valid partition arrangement

**24. Jugal Kishore Khanna (D) Thr. LRs. v. Sudhir Khanna (Supreme Court)**
- Oral settlement in 1979 between brothers allocating family property — held valid and binding
- Exclusive possession and rent collection after oral settlement = evidence of partition
- **Directly analogous** to the present case — oral settlement followed by allocation of shares

## Issue 8: "No Declaration Sought" — Red Herring

### Our Position: Admitted facts need no declaration; Prayer A is functional declaration; O12R6 covers this

**25. Order XII Rule 6 CPC — Judgment on Admissions (Latest SC Judgment: June 2026)**
- *"Where admissions of fact have been made either in the pleading or otherwise, whether orally or in writing, the Court may at any stage of the suit... make such order or give such judgment as it may think fit"*
- Admissions in connected proceedings (Cont.Cas(C) 1058/2023) can be used — Section 17 Evidence Act
- **No separate declaration needed** when facts are admitted by the opposite party

**26. Milkhiram v. Chamanlal, (2019) 18 SCC 335**
- O.12 R.6 can be invoked at any stage
- Admissions in connected proceedings are admissible
- **Applies:** Rashi Khemka's admission (PPJ = "family business", MOU = "family settlement") in Cont.Cas(C) 1058/2023

**27. Uttam Singh Duggal v. United Bank of India, (2000) 7 SCC 120**
- Admissions in one proceeding can be used in another under Section 17 Evidence Act
- **Applies:** Admissions by D1, D2, D3 in contempt proceedings, arbitration, NCLT — all usable in CS(OS) 545

# PART C: CONTRADICTORY & PECULIAR STANDS — ABUSE OF PROCESS

## Issue 9: Defendants Taking Mutually Contradictory Positions = Abuse of Process

### Our Position: The three applications are coordinated but contradictory — constitutes abuse of process

**28. Suzuki Parasrampuria Suitings Pvt. Ltd. v. Official Liquidator of Mahendra Petrochemicals Ltd., (2018) — Supreme Court (CJI Ranjan Gogoi, Navin Sinha & K.M. Joseph, JJ.)**
- *"A litigant can take different stands at different times but CANNOT take contradictory stands in the same case."*
- *"A party cannot be permitted to approbate and reprobate on the same facts and take inconsistent shifting stands."*
- The Court rejected a party that changed its position after its first claim was rejected
- **DIRECTLY APPLIES:** D1 says "partition suit" (court fee attack). D5 says "NOT partition suit" (limitation attack). D5 ALSO says "commercial suit" (jurisdiction attack). Three contradictory stands in the SAME suit.

**29. Amar Singh v. Union of India, (2011) 7 SCC 69**
- *"This Court wants to make it clear that an action at law is not a game of chess. A litigant who comes to Court and invokes its writ jurisdiction must come with clean hands. He cannot prevaricate and take inconsistent positions."*
- **Suppression of material facts** (Section 18 acknowledgments, Para 603 express plea) = "unclean hands"
- Courts have "over the centuries, frowned upon litigants who, with intent to deceive and mislead the courts, initiated proceedings without full disclosure of facts"

**30. Rajasthan State Industrial Development & Investment Corporation v. Diamond & Gem Development Corporation, (2013) 5 SCC 470**
- *"A party cannot be permitted to 'blow hot-blow cold', 'fast and loose' or 'approbate and reprobate'."*
- *"Where one knowingly accepts the benefits of a contract, or conveyance, or of an order, he is estopped from denying the validity of, or the binding effect of such contract, or conveyance, or order upon himself."*
- The doctrine of election is based on estoppel — **inherent in it is the principle that one cannot approbate and reprobate**
- Cited with approval: Nagubai Ammal v. B. Shama Rao (AIR 1956 SC 593), Pradeep Oil Corporation v. MCD (AIR 2011 SC 1869), V. Chandrasekaran v. Administrative Officer (JT 2012 (9) SC 260)
- **Directly applies:** Defendants accepted benefits of the MOU 2019 (allocating properties), gave undertakings before DHC — now cannot deny the settlement exists

**31. Defendants Cannot Approbate and Reprobate Across Different Forums — Supreme Court (Justice M.R. Shah & Justice B.V. Nagarathna, 2022)**
- *"The defendants cannot be permitted to approbate and reprobate the stand they took at different forums."*
- Where defendant successfully argued one forum had no jurisdiction (revenue court) → cannot then argue civil court also has no jurisdiction → would make plaintiff **remediless**
- **EXACT PARALLEL HERE:** If D5 succeeds on O7R10 (return to District Court) AND O7R11(d) (time-barred), the Plaintiff becomes remediless. This is impermissible.

**32. Mondira Ghosh v. Chaitali Ghosh, 2026 (Supreme Court — June 2026, LATEST)**
- Defendant cannot radically alter her defence to introduce a wholly contradictory stand
- Order VI Rule 7 CPC **prohibits departure from earlier pleadings** through inconsistent allegations
- Such conduct = **"clear abuse of process of law"**
- **Applies:** D1 and D5 are taking fundamentally inconsistent positions — D1 admits it's a partition suit (court fee challenge), D5 denies it's a partition suit (limitation challenge). These positions cannot coexist.

# PART D: READY-TO-USE COURT ARGUMENTS (COMBINING ALL CASE LAW)

## Argument 1 — On Limitation (Against O7R11 Limitation Application)

*"My Lords, the limitation argument fails on FOUR independent grounds:*

*(i) No limitation for partition: The right to seek partition is inherent and perpetual — K. Laxmanan (2009) 1 SCC 354; Muhammad Hussain AIR 1937 PC 250.*

*(ii) Plaint cannot be recharacterized: Under O7R11, the plaint's averments must be taken as true (Dahiben (2020) 7 SCC 366; Liverpool & London (2004) 9 SCC 512). The plaint says 'partition suit' — the Defendant cannot rewrite it as 'enforcement suit.'*

*(iii) Section 18 saves it anyway: Even on a 3-year clock, the last acknowledgment was 22.03.2024 (Para 18 undertaking before this Hon'ble Court). Suit filed 17.06.2026 = within 3 years. Shapoor Freedom Mazda (1961) 1 SCR 140; Bishal Jaiswal (2021) 6 SCC 366.*

*(iv) Continuing cause of action: Green Park sold 16.01.2026 — five months before suit. Each alienation restarts the clock — Section 22 Limitation Act; Deokaran Nenshi (1972) 2 SCC 890."*

## Argument 2 — On Artificial Valuation (Against O7R10 Application)

*"My Lords, the valuation challenge fails because:*

*(i) Plaintiff is dominus litis — Khatri Hotels (2011) 9 SCC 126; Tara Devi (1987) 4 SCC 69.*

*(ii) Nature of relief determines valuation, not market value — SC held 'market value does not become decisive merely because immovable property is the subject matter' (2022 bench of Justice Maheshwari).*

*(iii) Even if undervalued — Marg Limited v. Sushil Lalwani (2026 INSC 402): Rejection is NOT automatic; Court must first afford opportunity to cure. Plaintiff has already filed Section 149 application (allowed 19.06.2026)."*

## Argument 3 — On Properties Held by Companies

*"My Lords, the argument that properties held through companies cannot be partitioned was specifically rejected by the Supreme Court just last year in Shaifali Gupta v. Vidya Devi Gupta (2025 INSC 739) — whether properties held by companies are joint family assets involves factual inquiries necessitating a TRIAL. O7R11 is not the stage for this determination. Further, in Sangram Singh Gaekwad (2005) 11 SCC 314, the Court held that corporate veil can be lifted in partition disputes."*

## Argument 4 — On Contradictory Stands

*"My Lords, the Defendants' three applications constitute a textbook case of approbation and reprobation:*

*D1 (Kamal Gupta) says: 'It IS a partition suit — pay ad valorem fee.'*
*D5 (Mukesh Gupta) says: 'It is NOT a partition — it's enforcement of oral agreement, time-barred.'*
*D5 ALSO says: 'It is a commercial dispute — send to Commercial Division.'*

*As held in Suzuki Parasrampuria (2018): 'A litigant cannot take contradictory stands in the same case.' As held in Rajasthan SIDC (2013) 5 SCC 470: 'A party cannot blow hot and cold, fast and loose, or approbate and reprobate.' And as held just this month in Mondira Ghosh (June 2026): Inconsistent positions constitute abuse of process.*

*My Lords, if D5 succeeds on both O7R10 (return plaint — no jurisdiction) AND O7R11 (reject plaint — time-barred), the Plaintiff becomes remediless — which is exactly what the Supreme Court prohibited in the 2022 judgment of Justice Shah: defendants cannot take contradictory positions across forums that would leave the plaintiff without a remedy."*
