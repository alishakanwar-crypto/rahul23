# RESEARCH NOTE — FOR COUNSEL'S REVIEW (NOT A PLEADING)

## Limitation as a Mixed Question of Law and Fact — Impermissibility of Rejection under Order VII Rule 11(d) CPC Where Limitation Depends on Disputed Facts

**For use in:** Replies in CS(OS) No. 545/2026 — principally the reply to I.A. No. 16114/2026 (D5's O7R11(a)/(d) limitation application); the ouster-related propositions equally support the possession/court-fee answers in the reply to I.A. No. 16112/2026.

**Core proposition to be supported:** Where the plea of limitation depends upon disputed questions of fact — ouster of a co-owner, repudiation of a family arrangement, acknowledgment under Section 18 of the Limitation Act, 1963, or a continuing/recurring cause of action — the plaint cannot be rejected at the threshold under Order VII Rule 11(d) CPC; such questions can only be decided after evidence, on the plaint averments taken at face value.

---

### A. Supreme Court

**1. Balasaria Construction (P) Ltd. v. Hanuman Seva Trust, (2006) 5 SCC 658**
- *Proposition:* The question of limitation is a **mixed question of law and fact**; ex facie, a plaint cannot be rejected under Order VII Rule 11(d) on the ground of limitation unless, on a meaningful reading of the plaint, the suit appears barred on its own averments. (See para 8, resolving the conflict between earlier Calcutta decisions and approving *Narasimha Reddy*.)
- *How it strengthens the paragraph:* This is the foundational authority — it converts the entire limitation debate into a trial issue the moment the plaint discloses facts (date of knowledge of exclusion, subsequent acknowledgments, the 13.08.2025 SC working-out liberty) which must be proved or disproved by evidence.

**2. C. Natarajan v. Ashim Bai, (2007) 14 SCC 183**
- *Proposition:* An application under O7R11(d) must be decided **on the averments of the plaint alone**; the defence in the written statement is wholly irrelevant; whether the suit is barred by limitation would depend on the facts pleaded, and where adverse possession/possession issues are raised, they require evidence. (Paras 12–14.)
- *How it strengthens:* D5's application repeatedly travels outside the plaint (to his own characterisation of the 2014 arrangement and alleged 2015 ouster). This authority bars that exercise.

**3. Ram Prakash Gupta v. Rajiv Kumar Gupta, (2007) 10 SCC 59**
- *Proposition:* Rejection of a plaint on limitation **without framing an issue and without evidence**, on a truncated reading of the plaint, is impermissible; the plaint must be read as a whole. (Paras 5–12, 22–23.)
- *How it strengthens:* Directly answers the attempt to isolate the 2014/2015 averments while ignoring paragraph 66 (continuing joint/constructive possession) and the pleaded acknowledgments of the 23% share (2019 MOU, pleadings in ARB.P. 1010/2022).

**4. Chhotanben v. Kiritbhai Jalkrushnabhai Thakkar, (2018) 6 SCC 422**
- *Proposition:* Where the plaint raises a triable issue as to when the plaintiff acquired knowledge (there, of a registered sale deed), limitation is a **mixed question requiring evidence**; O7R11(d) cannot be invoked. (Paras 14–17.)
- *How it strengthens:* On D5's own ouster theory, limitation could begin only from the Plaintiff's *knowledge of exclusion* (Article 110) — a quintessential fact question identical in structure to Chhotanben.

**5. Shakti Bhog Food Industries Ltd. v. Central Bank of India, (2020) 17 SCC 260**
- *Proposition:* For O7R11(d), the court must read the plaint **as a whole and take its averments as correct**; where Article 113-type questions ("when the right to sue accrues") arise, accrual is fact-dependent and cannot be resolved at the threshold. (Paras 21–26.)
- *How it strengthens:* The suit pleads a continuing/recurring right to partition and successive acknowledgments; when the right to sue accrued is exactly the kind of accrual question Shakti Bhog reserves for trial.

**6. Nusli Neville Wadia v. Ivory Properties, (2020) 6 SCC 557**
- *Proposition:* Limitation may be decided as a preliminary/threshold issue **only when it is a pure question of law on admitted facts**; where it is a mixed question of law and fact, it cannot be so decided. (Paras 26–30, 50.)
- *How it strengthens:* Ouster, repudiation and Section 18 acknowledgments are all disputed here — none is admitted — so the threshold route is closed.

**7. Salim D. Agboatwala v. Shamalji Oddhavji Thakkar, 2021 SCC OnLine SC 735 : (2021) 20 SCC 302**
- *Proposition:* Rejection on limitation is permissible only where the suit is **ex facie hopelessly barred**; where any doubt exists or knowledge/accrual is pleaded, the plaintiff is entitled to trial. (Paras 9–13.)
- *How it strengthens:* A suit filed on 17.06.2026, within 12 years of even the alleged November 2015 "exclusion" (Article 110), cannot be described as ex facie barred on any reading.

**8. P.V. Guru Raj Reddy v. P. Neeradha Reddy, (2015) 8 SCC 331**
- *Proposition:* Rejection under O7R11 is a **drastic power**; the conditions are stringent; plaint averments must be assumed true and the defence cannot be looked at. (Paras 5–6.)
- *How it strengthens:* Sets the governing standard for both of D5's applications and D1's application alike.

**9. Sopan Sukhdeo Sable v. Asstt. Charity Commissioner, (2004) 3 SCC 137**
- *Proposition:* The plaint has to be read as a whole; **clever drafting by the defendant's characterisation cannot substitute the plaint**; only a plaint which is manifestly vexatious and meritless can be rejected. (Paras 11–17.)
- *How it strengthens:* Answers the defendants' re-characterisation of a partition/working-out suit as a time-barred specific-performance action.

**10. On the underlying limitation propositions (to pair with the above):**
- *Vidya Devi @ Vidya Vati v. Prem Prakash*, AIR 1995 SC 1789 — possession of one co-sharer is possession of all; **ouster must be clear, hostile and proved** — inherently an evidentiary question.
- *Shub Karan Bubna v. Sita Saran Bubna*, (2009) 9 SCC 689 — the right to partition is a **continuing/recurring right** of a co-owner.
- *Kale v. Deputy Director of Consolidation*, (1976) 3 SCC 119 — a family arrangement operates on **antecedent title**; a suit working it out is not a specific-performance action attracting the 3-year articles.
- Section 18, Limitation Act — each acknowledgment in writing (2019 MOU; verified pleadings in ARB.P. 1010/2022) gives a fresh start; whether a document amounts to an acknowledgment is again fact-sensitive (*Food Corporation of India v. Assam State Coop. Marketing*, (2004) 12 SCC 360).

### B. Delhi High Court

**11. Saroj Salkan v. Hukam Singh, 2010 SCC OnLine Del 2137 (and the line following it)**
- *Proposition:* In partition suits between family members, questions of ouster/exclusion and the point from which limitation runs are questions of fact; a plaint pleading joint possession cannot be rejected at the threshold on an assumed ouster.
- *How it strengthens:* Home-court authority applying the co-sharer possession fiction at the O7R11 stage.

**12. Rajinder Kumar v. Kuldeep Singh, 2014 SCC OnLine Del 1077**
- *Proposition:* Where the plaint in a partition suit pleads that the plaintiff continued to be in joint possession and the defendants deny it, ouster and limitation require trial; O7R11(d) is not attracted.
- *How it strengthens:* Squarely parallels paragraph 66 of the plaint.

Natarajan, (2007) 14 SCC 183; Ram Prakash Gupta, (2007) 10 SCC 59; Chhotanben, (2018) 6 SCC 422; Shakti Bhog, (2020) 17 SCC 260; Nusli Neville Wadia, (2020) 6 SCC 557; Salim D. No. 1010/2022 constitute acknowledgments under Section 18 of the Limitation Act, and when the Plaintiff's continuing right to seek partition was first clearly and hostilely denied, are all disputed questions of fact requiring evidence, and are incapable of adjudication at the threshold."
