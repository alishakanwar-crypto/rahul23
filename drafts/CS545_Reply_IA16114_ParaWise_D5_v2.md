A. PRELIMINARY SUBMISSIONS

1. That at the outset, the present application is wholly misconceived and is a mala fide attempt on the part of the Applicant/Defendant No. 5 to obtain, at the threshold and without filing any written statement on oath, an adjudication of defences on merits. It is respectfully submitted that the pleas now sought to be raised were never taken by the Applicant or the members of his group in any of the connected proceedings, including the proceedings before the Hon'ble Supreme Court, and are in fact contrary to the stands taken by the said group on oath in those very proceedings to obtain relief for themselves. The application deserves to be dismissed with exemplary costs.

2. THE SUIT IS A PARTITION SUIT BY A CO-OWNER HOLDING ANTECEDENT TITLE AND IS NOT A SUIT FOR ENFORCEMENT OF AN ORAL AGREEMENT

That the plaint, when read as a whole, clearly avers that the Plaintiff is a member of the P.P. Jewellers family and holds an antecedent 23% share in the PPJ Family Corpus; that the oral family settlement dated 30.09.2014, the Memorandum/Family Settlement Deed dated 20.06.2015 and the MOU dated 09.07.2019 merely acknowledge and work out the said pre-existing entitlement of the Plaintiff; and that the Plaintiff continues to be in part, joint and constructive possession of the corpus, as specifically pleaded inter alia in paragraph 66 of the plaint, including actual control and management of assets such as Glow Jewellery Pvt. Ltd., whose principal asset is the land at Village Rawta, which stands allocated to the share of the Plaintiff. It is settled law that a family arrangement does not create or transfer title, and that it only acknowledges the antecedent title which always resided in each member of the family. This proposition is not being urged in the abstract. It is the law laid down in Kale v. Deputy Director of Consolidation, (1976) 3 SCC 119, as extracted and applied in paragraph 23 of the judgment dated 22.03.2024 passed by this Hon'ble Court in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022, being a judgment inter partes, whereunder the disputes of the Applicant's own group were referred to arbitration, and which judgment the Hon'ble Supreme Court, vide its judgment dated 13.08.2025 in SLP(C) No. 4775/2025 and connected matters, has left the parties free to work out their rights in accordance with.

3. THE PRINCIPLES OF KALE AS EXTRACTED IN PARAGRAPH 23 OF THE JUDGMENT DATED 22.03.2024

That the judgment dated 22.03.2024 passed in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022 holds as under:

"23. Further, it is well settled that unlike commercial contracts which focus on business interests, family settlements aim to preserve peace and goodwill within the family. This social objective is recognized by courts, and it has been held in umpteen judgments that it is the duty of the courts to uphold and give full effect to such an arrangement instead of disturbing the same on technical or trivial grounds. In Kale v. Dy. Director of Consolidation (supra), it has been held as under:"

The said judgment thereafter reproduces the propositions laid down in Kale, namely, that family arrangements are governed by a special equity peculiar to themselves; that the object of a family arrangement is to avoid long-drawn litigation and to preserve peace in the family; that family arrangements are governed by principles which are not applicable to dealings between strangers; that matters which would be fatal to similar transactions between strangers are not objections to the binding effect of a family arrangement; and that a party who has enjoyed material benefits under a family arrangement is estopped from turning around and unsettling the same. It is respectfully submitted that the present application, which seeks to defeat the partition claim of a member of the family on technical grounds of nomenclature and by invoking articles of limitation applicable to commercial contracts between strangers, is directly contrary to the law of the case as declared in the said judgment.

4. THE CLAUSES OF THE MEMORANDUM DATED 20.06.2015 EVIDENCE SUBSISTING AND EXECUTORY OBLIGATIONS

That the Memorandum/Family Settlement Deed dated 20.06.2015, which is annexed to the plaint, records inter alia as under:

Clause 8: "Stock shall be handed over to the Third Party by the Second Party from anyone vault of the choice of the Third Party."

Clause 11: "Sh. Rahul Gupta ji, Third Party shall be entitled to receive the rent of the property coming to his share after deduction of service tax and income tax liabilities on account of rent."

The Plaintiff is the "Third Party" thereunder. It is respectfully submitted that the stock which remains to be handed over under Clause 8 and the rents which remain unpaid under Clause 11 constitute continuing and executory obligations owed to the Plaintiff as a co-owner. The said subsisting obligations completely belie the theory of a completed refusal or repudiation in the year 2015 which is now sought to be set up by the Applicant.

5. THE PLEADINGS OF THE APPLICANT'S OWN GROUP ARE DESTRUCTIVE OF THE PRESENT APPLICATION

That the Applicant's own group has, on oath, in proceedings up to the Hon'ble Supreme Court, taken stands which are wholly destructive of the present application:

(i) That in the Reply dated 15.12.2022 filed in the proceedings arising out of ARB.P. No. 1010/2022 and the Section 17 proceedings, it was pleaded as under: "It is vehemently denied that in terms of the family settlement, the distribution of the stock… is still to be achieved. It is submitted and reiterated that the stocks were distributed between the family members in terms of the settlement dated 30.09.2014 as per the respective ratio of the respondents and the petitioner i.e. 77:23 respectively." The said pleading is an acknowledgment of the settlement dated 30.09.2014 and of the ratio of 77:23 thereunder.

(ii) That in the same proceedings it was further pleaded that "Any claim on the immovable properties before the bearing the said share of liabilities is pre-mature and cannot be awarded by this hon'ble tribunal." It is respectfully submitted that a claim cannot be premature before the learned Arbitrator and at the same time be barred by time since 2015 or 2018 before this Hon'ble Court.

(iii) That in SLP(C) No. 4775/2025, filed by the Kamal Gupta group challenging the order dated 12.11.2024, Ground W pleads that the Plaintiff "while not acknowledging his 23% has taken control of certain assets from the family properties through the Intervenor Companies, which have been enlisted in Annexure A to alleged FSD 2019, which already form part of the share of 23% allocated to him (and may even value more than 23%)." The said ground is an admission on oath, made in the year 2025, that a 23% share stands allocated to the Plaintiff and that the Plaintiff is in control and possession of assets forming part of the said share. The said single ground is destructive of both limbs of the present application, inasmuch as it negates the theory of ouster in 2015, the Plaintiff being admittedly in possession of assets of his share, and it equally negates the plea of absence of cause of action, the share being admittedly allocated and only its complete working out by metes and bounds remaining.

(iv) That in the List of Dates of the said SLP, the grievance raised was that the Plaintiff "refused to accept the 23% of shares as apportioned to him", which is a plea of non-acceptance of an apportionment and not of ouster. Further, in Grounds R and LL thereof, the complaint was that the Plaintiff had never filed any application or sought any specific relief allocating him a 23% share. The said complaint was an objection that the Plaintiff had not yet sued, and the same stands answered by the institution of the present suit. The said stand is the very opposite of a plea that such a suit would be barred by limitation.

(v) That in I.A. No. 39500/2024, filed on 12.09.2024 in the disposed-of ARB.P. No. 1010/2022, and in the joint written submissions dated 13.10.2024 filed therein, the defendants' side defended the judgment dated 22.03.2024, including its recognition of the family settlement and the protections recorded therein in favour of the Plaintiff, in order to persuade this Hon'ble Court not to recall the reference to arbitration. At no stage in the said proceedings was it pleaded that the family settlement stood repudiated in 2015 or that the claims thereunder were barred by time.

It is further submitted that the aforesaid statements, having been made within three years prior to the institution of the present suit, would in any event constitute acknowledgments within the meaning of Section 18 of the Limitation Act, 1963, and it is settled law that the question whether a statement constitutes such an acknowledgment is itself a question of fact requiring evidence, as held in Food Corporation of India v. Assam State Coop. Marketing, (2004) 12 SCC 360, which alone is fatal to any rejection of the plaint at the threshold.

6. ARTICLE 110 OF THE LIMITATION ACT GOVERNS AND OUSTER IS A TRIABLE ISSUE

That the right of a co-owner holding antecedent title to seek partition is a continuing right, as held in Shub Karan Bubna v. Sita Saran Bubna, (2009) 9 SCC 689, and there is no single date of accrual from which any period of three years can be computed. If limitation runs at all against a member of the family, it can only be under Article 110 of the Limitation Act, 1963, being a suit by a person excluded from joint family property to enforce a right to a share therein, for which the period is twelve years from when the exclusion becomes known to the plaintiff. It is further settled that exclusion or ouster must be clear, hostile and communicated, as held in Vidya Devi @ Vidya Vati v. Prem Prakash, AIR 1995 SC 1789. On the averments of the plaint itself, the Plaintiff remains in part, joint and constructive possession of the corpus and continues to enjoy assets falling to his share, which position stands admitted by the Applicant's own group in Ground W of the SLP as extracted hereinabove. There is no pleaded ouster of the nature required by law, and in any event the questions whether there was ouster, when the same became known, and whether the settlement was acknowledged, are all mixed questions of law and fact requiring trial.

7. LIMITATION IS A MIXED QUESTION OF LAW AND FACT AND THE PLAINT CANNOT BE REJECTED ON DISPUTED FACTS

That it is settled law that a plaint can be rejected on the ground of limitation only where the suit is ex facie barred on the averments of the plaint itself as a pure question of law. Reliance is placed on Balasaria Construction (P) Ltd. v. Hanuman Seva Trust, (2006) 5 SCC 658; C. Natarajan v. Ashim Bai, (2007) 14 SCC 183; Chhotanben v. Kiritbhai Jalkrushnabhai Thakkar, (2018) 6 SCC 422, wherein the date of knowledge was held to be a triable issue; Shakti Bhog Food Industries Ltd. v. Central Bank of India, (2020) 17 SCC 260, wherein it was held that the plaint must be read as a whole and that the accrual of the right to sue is fact-dependent; Nusli Neville Wadia v. Ivory Properties, (2020) 6 SCC 557; and Salim D. Agboatwala v. Shamalji Oddhavji Thakkar, (2021) 20 SCC 302. The power under Order VII Rule 11 CPC is a drastic power and the conditions for its exercise are stringent, as held in P.V. Guru Raj Reddy v. P. Neeradha Reddy, (2015) 8 SCC 331 and Sopan Sukhdeo Sable v. Assistant Charity Commissioner, (2004) 3 SCC 137. The assertion of the Applicant in paragraph 17 of the application that there is no factual controversy is belied by his own paragraph 2, whereunder every averment, share, possession and allocation pleaded in the plaint is disputed. A plaint which discloses triable issues cannot be rejected at the threshold.

8. APPROBATION AND REPROBATION AND THE SPECIAL EQUITY OF FAMILY ARRANGEMENTS

That the Applicant's group has taken the benefit of paragraph 23 of the judgment dated 22.03.2024, and of the special equity attaching to family settlements as derived from Kale, in order to have their own inter se disputes referred to arbitration and to preserve the said reference, and they defended the said judgment in the recall proceedings. Having done so, it is no longer open to them to contend that another member of the same family should be denied the benefit of the same doctrine. Non-existent legal lacunae, namely, objections of nomenclature, absence of a declaratory prayer and the invocation of three-year articles applicable to commercial contracts, are being set up only in order to deprive the Plaintiff of the special equity attaching to family arrangements and to subject him to the very long-drawn and attritional litigation which, as held in Kale, it is the duty of the courts to prevent. The Plaintiff is a member of the family holding antecedent title and his suit cannot be dealt with as if it were a dealing between strangers.

B. PARA-WISE REPLY (WITHOUT PREJUDICE TO THE PRELIMINARY SUBMISSIONS)

9. That the contents of paragraph 1 of the application are wrong and denied. It is denied that the plaint is barred by limitation or that it does not disclose a cause of action. It is denied that the Plaintiff has attempted to present a time-barred claim for enforcement of an alleged oral family settlement under the guise of a suit for partition. It is reiterated that the plaint, on its own averments, is a suit for partition and consequential reliefs by a co-owner in part, joint and constructive possession of the PPJ Family Corpus, holding an antecedent 23% share which stands acknowledged, and not created, by the family arrangements dated 30.09.2014, 20.06.2015 and 09.07.2019. The contents of the Preliminary Submissions hereinabove are reiterated and not repeated for the sake of brevity.

10. That the contents of paragraph 2 of the application need no reply, save and except to point out that the blanket non-admission by the Applicant of every allegation, assertion, document, arrangement, share, allocation, undertaking, valuation, possession, title and cause of action pleaded in the plaint is itself fatal to the application, inasmuch as it concedes that every material question is disputed and triable. It is further pointed out that the Applicant's own group has, on oath before the Hon'ble Supreme Court, admitted the apportionment of a 23% share to the Plaintiff and the control of the Plaintiff over assets forming part of the said share, as extracted in paragraph 5 hereinabove.

11. That the contents of paragraph 3 of the application are wrong and denied. It is denied that the plaint is liable to be rejected under Order VII Rule 11(d) CPC or under Order VII Rule 11(a) CPC. Neither of the said clauses is attracted, for the reasons set out in the Preliminary Submissions hereinabove.

12. That the contents of paragraph 4 of the application are formal and need no reply. The purported grounds are dealt with hereinbelow.

13. That the contents of paragraphs 5 to 9 of the application are wrong and denied. It is denied that the suit is one for enforcement of an alleged oral agreement or that the expression partition has been used only to avoid the law of limitation. The submission of the Applicant inverts the settled law. As held in Kale, and as extracted in paragraph 23 of the judgment dated 22.03.2024 passed inter partes, a family arrangement does not create or transfer title and only acknowledges the antecedent title residing in each member of the family. A suit by a member of the family for partition in accordance with a family arrangement is precisely the suit of a co-owner working out his pre-existing share, and is not a suit for specific performance of a commercial contract. The reliance placed by the Applicant on nomenclature in fact cuts the other way, inasmuch as it is the Applicant who seeks to re-characterise a partition suit as a contract suit in order to attract the articles of limitation applicable to commercial contracts. It is respectfully submitted that the principle against clever drafting, as recognised in Sopan Sukhdeo Sable (supra), equally prohibits re-characterisation of the plaint by the defendant. It is further denied that the properties held through companies are outside the corpus. The Applicant's own group has pleaded before the Hon'ble Supreme Court that the assets of the intervenor companies stand enlisted in Annexure A to the FSD 2019 and form part of the 23% share of the Plaintiff.

14. That the contents of paragraph 10 of the application are wrong and denied. It is denied that any declaration in respect of the family arrangement is required to be sought. Since the family arrangement merely acknowledges antecedent title, the Plaintiff sues upon his title as a co-owner and not upon the contract, and it is settled law that a co-owner seeking partition is not required to seek a declaration in respect of the instrument recording the family arrangement, and that even an oral or unregistered family arrangement can be given effect to and can found or corroborate the claim, as held in Kale (supra). The objection is a technical and trivial ground of precisely the nature which, as held in paragraph 23 of the judgment dated 22.03.2024, cannot be permitted to disturb a family arrangement. It is further pointed out that in the SLP the Applicant's group complained that the Plaintiff had not sought any specific relief allocating his 23% share, and now that the Plaintiff has sued, it is contended that the suit requires yet another prayer. A defendant cannot dictate the prayers of the plaintiff and thereafter seek rejection of the plaint for the absence of such prayers.

15. That the contents of paragraphs 11 and 12 of the application are wrong and denied. It is specifically denied that the plaint discloses knowledge of any alleged breach in 2015 or any ouster by 06.11.2015 or by 09.07.2019. The plaint does not plead a completed repudiation. On the contrary, the plaint pleads subsisting arrangements, which were reaffirmed by the parties themselves in 2015 and again by the MOU dated 09.07.2019, being a document upon which the Applicant himself places reliance as a fresh reference point, thereby admitting that the arrangement was alive in 2019; continuing obligations under Clauses 8 and 11 of the Memorandum dated 20.06.2015 as extracted hereinabove; continuing part, joint and constructive possession of the Plaintiff as pleaded in paragraph 66 of the plaint; and the continuing enjoyment by the Plaintiff of allocated assets, including Glow Jewellery Pvt. Ltd. and the Rawta land. The Applicant's own group pleaded in the Section 17 proceedings that claims on the immovable properties were premature, and pleaded in the SLP in the year 2025 that assets already form part of the share of 23% allocated to the Plaintiff. It is respectfully submitted that a party who is in possession of assets of his share, and whose share stands admitted as allocated by the opposite side in the year 2025, cannot be held, least of all at the threshold and without evidence, to have been ousted to his knowledge in the year 2015. The questions of ouster, its hostility, its communication and the date of knowledge thereof are all triable issues, as held in Vidya Devi (supra) and Chhotanben (supra).

16. That the contents of paragraphs 13 to 15 of the application are wrong and denied. It is denied that Articles 54, 55 or 58 of the Limitation Act, 1963 have any application to the present suit, or that limitation expired in 2018 or on 09.07.2022. The said articles govern suits between contracting strangers and have no application to the continuing right of a co-owner to seek partition, as held in Shub Karan Bubna (supra). If any article applies, it is Article 110, under which the period is twelve years from the date on which the exclusion becomes known to the plaintiff. Even on the Applicant's own untenable hypothesis of the year 2019 as the reference point, the suit filed on 17.06.2026 would require a trial on the question as to when the alleged exclusion, if any, became known, which is a mixed question of law and fact incapable of determination at the threshold. Further, the acknowledgments of the years 2022, 2024 and 2025, as extracted in paragraph 5 hereinabove, squarely raise questions under Section 18 of the Limitation Act which require evidence.

17. That the contents of paragraph 16 of the application are wrong and denied. It is denied that the alleged breach is not a continuing one. The withholding of stock deliverable from a vault of the choice of the Plaintiff under Clause 8, the non-payment of the rents of the properties falling to the share of the Plaintiff under Clause 11, and the obstruction of the enjoyment by the Plaintiff of the assets allocated to him, including by way of objections before the SDM, Kapashera in respect of the Rawta property and proceedings before the NCLT in respect of Glow Jewellery Pvt. Ltd., constitute continuing wrongs and continuing denials, and not any completed repudiation of the year 2015. It is further pointed out that the Applicant's group simultaneously seeks stays and status quo orders in collateral fora which freeze the enjoyment by the Plaintiff of the very assets of his share, and thereafter pleads the non-enjoyment by the Plaintiff as ouster. A party cannot be permitted to rely upon an exclusion of its own making.

18. That the contents of paragraph 17 of the application are wrong and denied. It is denied that only a pure question of law arises. The contents of paragraph 7 of the Preliminary Submissions hereinabove are reiterated. The Applicant's own paragraph 2 disputes every fact; the pleadings of his own group admit the allocation of the share and the control of the Plaintiff over assets thereof; and the questions of ouster, knowledge, acknowledgment and possession are all questions of fact. On the authorities cited hereinabove, rejection of the plaint under Order VII Rule 11(d) CPC is impermissible.

19. That the contents of paragraphs 18 and 19 of the application are wrong and denied. Since the foundational premise of the Applicant, namely, that the suit is a time-barred contract claim, is itself erroneous, the superstructure raised thereon must fall. The reliefs of partition, possession, rendition of accounts, mesne profits and injunction are the ordinary and well-recognised reliefs in a partition suit of a co-owner and are fully maintainable.

20. That the contents of paragraph 20 of the application are wrong and denied. The submission is contrary to the case of the Applicant's own group, which has, throughout the proceedings in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022, the recall proceedings and SLP(C) No. 4775/2025, litigated the disputes as disputes arising out of a family settlement concerning the PPJ Family Corpus and the 23% share of the Plaintiff therein, and never as disputes arising out of any commercial contract. The special equity attaching to family arrangements, as recorded in paragraph 23 of the judgment dated 22.03.2024, applies to precisely such family property arrangements, irrespective of whether the properties are held through family companies, and the pleadings of the Applicant's own group treat the assets held by the companies as part of the corpus apportioned in the ratio of 77:23.

21. That the contents of paragraph 21 of the application are wrong and denied for the reasons set out hereinabove. Neither clause (a) nor clause (d) of Order VII Rule 11 CPC is attracted. The plaint discloses a complete cause of action of a co-owner for partition, and no bar of limitation arises ex facie on the averments of the plaint.

22. That the contents of paragraph 22 of the application are wrong and denied. Balance of convenience is not a ground available under Order VII Rule 11 CPC. In any event, the prejudice lies entirely upon the Plaintiff, inasmuch as the Applicant's side, having enjoyed the benefits of the family arrangement and of the judgment dated 22.03.2024, now seeks to subject the Plaintiff to attritional threshold litigation while withholding his stock, his rents and his properties. As held in Kale, and as applied in the judgment dated 22.03.2024, a party who has enjoyed material benefits under a family arrangement is estopped from turning around and unsettling the same.

23. That the contents of paragraph 23 of the application are wrong and denied. The application is not bona fide and is an abuse of the process of this Hon'ble Court. The application is in substance a written statement in disguise, advancing defences on merits without pleading the same on oath, and takes stands contrary to those taken by the Applicant's own group before the Hon'ble Supreme Court and before this Hon'ble Court. The application deserves dismissal with exemplary costs under Sections 35A and 35B CPC.
