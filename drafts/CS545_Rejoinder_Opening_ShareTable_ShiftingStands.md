## TABLE 1 — THE FAMILY SHARE ALLOCATION: THE 23% GAP IS ARITHMETICALLY CLEAR

The share allocation recorded in the Oral Family Settlement of 30.09.2014, carried into the FSD dated 20.06.2015 and the MOU/FSD dated 09.07.2019 (the Defendants' own documents), is:

| Party | Share | Position under the family documents |
|---|---|---|
| Kamal Gupta (D-1) | 18% | First Party — signatory |
| Pawan Gupta (D-7) | 23% | Second Party — signatory (founding member) |
| **Rahul Gupta (Plaintiff)** | **23%** | Third Party — NON-signatory to FSD 2015 and MOU 2019 |
| Mukesh Gupta (D-5) | 18% | Fourth Party — signatory |
| Mohit Gupta (D-6) | 18% | Fifth Party — signatory |
| **Total** | **100%** | |

The arithmetic is decisive: 18 + 23 + 18 + 18 = **77%** for the signatories; the remaining **23% is the Plaintiff's share** — a gap the Defendants' own documents create and cannot explain away. Even the reference to arbitration dated 22.03.2024 proceeded only qua the signatories' 77%, keeping the Plaintiff's 23% outside the arbitration.

**Their own admission in SLP(C) 4775-4779/2025.** In the Rejoinder filed by Mr. Kamal Gupta & Mrs. Veena Gupta (the very Defendant Nos. 1–2 who now file the Reply under rejoinder) to the Counter Affidavit of Respondent Nos. 1–6, para 34:

> "It is submitted that the Petitioner merely stated that the FSDs recognised independent rights of Rahul Gupta to the extent of 23%, however the same was refused by Rahul Gupta and therefore, 23% share was included in the 77% share of the Petitioner. However, any share of Respondent No.10 remained contingent upon Respondent No.10 discharging corresponding liabilities of the family."

Thus, on their own sworn pleading before the Hon'ble Supreme Court, (i) the FSDs **recognised independent rights of Rahul Gupta to the extent of 23%**, and (ii) that 23% was unilaterally "included" in their 77% — the very usurpation this suit seeks to undo. The alleged "contingency" of liabilities is answered by Table 2 below: the signatories themselves allocated the Plaintiff's liabilities to Kamal Gupta.

## TABLE 2 — THE 2019 MOU CLAUSE: ALL SIGNATORIES ALLOCATED RAHUL GUPTA'S LIABILITY TO KAMAL GUPTA

The MOU/FSD dated 09.07.2019 — executed by ALL the answering/contesting Defendants' groups (KG Group and PG Group), with the Plaintiff excluded — itself records:

**Recital VII:**
> "Although, the alleged claims of Mr. Rahul Gupta, remain unresolved for the time being, as his share of 23% has been included in the share of KG Group, **Mr. Kamal Gupta has taken over the responsibilities/accountabilities/liabilities of Mr. Rahul Gupta, as on 30.09.2014.** It is made clear that the decision taken under this MOU shall be binding upon all the parties, who have agreed and signed this MOU, and shall not be altered in any circumstance."

**Clause 1:**
> "The share of Rahul Gupta - 23%, which includes liabilities of the five companies/firms namely: (i) P.P. Jewellers Pvt Ltd. (ii) P.P. Jewellers (Exports) (iii) P.P. Jewellers (Delhi) (iv) Appy Diamonds Pvt. Ltd. (v) L.R. Builders Pvt. Ltd. existing as on 30.09.2014, and his share and the assets, has been apportioned to the share of KG Group. **KG Group has taken over the responsibilities/accountabilities/liabilities**, therefore, in the event of any dispute is raised by Mr. Rahul Gupta, his legal heirs and/or by anyone on his behalf, with regard to any claim in respect of business or assets dealt with in the present framework, under this MOU, shall be the sole responsibility of Kamal Gupta (KG) Group alone. PG Group shall not be responsible/accountable/liable for such claims of Mr. Rahul Gupta."

**Consequence for their ₹698.54 Crore "assets-with-liabilities" defence (Reply, Section E):** by their own binding instrument — which they declared "shall not be altered in any circumstance" — the Plaintiff's liabilities as on 30.09.2014 were **taken over by Mr. Kamal Gupta/KG Group** along with the 23% share and assets. Defendant No. 1 cannot take over the Plaintiff's 23% assets under the MOU and then charge the Plaintiff with 23% of the group liabilities (₹160.67 Cr) to defeat interim protection. Either the MOU binds them (they signed it) — in which case the liability sits with KG Group — or no concluded settlement exists (their Section C) — in which case the corpus must be preserved for partition and accounts under Order XX Rule 18 CPC. On no view does the liability argument defeat preservation of the corpus pendente lite.

## TABLE 3 — THE MANHANDLING / OUSTER OF 2015: THEIR SHIFTING STANDS ACROSS PROCEEDINGS

The Reply (para 15 and preliminary chart) attacks the Plaintiff's pleading of the 06.11.2015 incident (denial of control over PP Jewellers Exports' stock; violent manhandling substantiated by MLC dated 06.11.2015; compelled to leave the family home) as "contradictory". Their own pleadings across proceedings tell a different story:

| # | Proceeding / Document | Whose pleading | What they said about the 2015 removal/incident |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CS(OS) 581/2023 — Written Statement of Defendant Nos. 1–7 (Kamal Gupta group side), Preliminary Objections para 23 | Kamal Gupta group | "Since **his removal from the family home and business(es) in 2015**, Mr. Rahul Gupta has been attempting to harm his father and other family members..." — i.e., they themselves plead that the Plaintiff was REMOVED from the family home and businesses in 2015. A person "removed" did not voluntarily walk out; removal corroborates ouster. |
| 2 | Reply of D1–2 to I.A. 16084/2026 (the Reply under rejoinder), Section F (limitation) | Kamal & Veena Gupta | They INVOKE the very 2015 exclusion/MLC of 06.11.2015 as the date of "repudiation" to run limitation — while simultaneously disputing that the manhandling/ouster occurred. They cannot rely on the 2015 ouster for limitation and deny it for ouster/possession. |
| 3 | Reply's own comparative chart quoting the letter dated 05.05.2025 (Rahul Gupta to Mukesh Gupta and Mohit Gupta) | Quoted by D1–2 themselves | The letter records "(i) Mr. **Mukesh Gupta and his son manhandled a Director on 08.11.15**..." — the manhandling allegation against Mukesh Gupta's side has been consistently on record and was placed before the Defendants without contemporaneous denial being shown in the Reply. |
| 4 | Rejoinder of Kamal Gupta & Veena Gupta in SLP(C) 4775-4779/2025, para 12.14 (quoting FSD 2019 Recital IV) | Kamal & Veena Gupta | "...Mr. Rahul Gupta has taken control of certain assets of Joint Family group, without discharging his share of the liabilities and **he has been working separately since 2015; without any knowledge/consent of either KG or PG Groups** (KG and PG Groups have no access to his personally controlled business affairs)..." — an admission that the Plaintiff was out of the family fold and businesses from 2015, consistent with the pleaded ouster. |
| 5 | Counter filed in the Purnima Colonisers matter, signed by Mohit Gupta on behalf of Mukesh Gupta and Veena Gupta | Mukesh/Mohit/Veena Gupta | [COUNSEL TO VERIFY / DOCUMENT AWAITED — the counter in the Purnima Colonisers proceedings (and the 2018 oppression & mismanagement case referred to therein) is stated to contain their stand on the manhandling/2015 incident; the document is not in the record available to the drafter. To be inserted verbatim on receipt.] |

**The point for the rejoinder:** whether the Plaintiff "went to take over the stocks and was manhandled" (suit para 15) or was "coerced to leave and thrown out" (I.A. 13282/2023, para III(A)) is not a contradiction at all — both describe the same continuum of November 2015: denial of control, physical assault (MLC dated 06.11.2015), and expulsion from home and business by 07.11.2015. The Defendants' own pleadings — "removal from the family home and business(es) in 2015" (CS(OS) 581/2023 WS) and "working separately since 2015" (FSD 2019 Recital IV, relied on in their SLP 4775 Rejoinder) — accept the fact of the 2015 exclusion. Their real quarrel is with its characterisation, which is a triable issue; it is no answer to preservation of the corpus.
