MOST RESPECTFULLY SHOWETH:

A. PRELIMINARY SUBMISSIONS

1. That the application is a mala fide attempt to secure, at the threshold and without a written statement on oath, a merits adjudication of defences which — on the Applicant's own group's pleadings before the Hon'ble Supreme Court and before this Hon'ble Court — have never previously been taken, and which are contradicted by the stands his own side has taken to obtain relief in connected proceedings. The application deserves dismissal with exemplary costs.

2. THE SUIT IS A PARTITION SUIT BY A CO-OWNER WITH ANTECEDENT TITLE, NOT A SUIT FOR "ENFORCEMENT OF AN ORAL AGREEMENT". The plaint, read as a whole, pleads that the Plaintiff is a member of the P.P. Jewellers family holding an antecedent 23% share in the PPJ Family Corpus; that the oral family settlement dated 30.09.2014, the Memorandum/Family Settlement Deed dated 20.06.2015 and the MOU dated 09.07.2019 acknowledge and work out that pre-existing entitlement; and that the Plaintiff is in part/joint/constructive possession of the corpus (inter alia paragraph 66 of the plaint), including actual control and management of assets such as Glow Jewellery Pvt. Ltd. (whose principal asset is the Rawta land) allocated to his share. A family arrangement does not create or transfer title; it acknowledges antecedent title which always resided in each member. This is not the Plaintiff's ipse dixit — it is the law declared in Kale v. Deputy Director of Consolidation, (1976) 3 SCC 119, as extracted and applied in paragraph 23 of the judgment dated 22.03.2024 passed by this Hon'ble Court in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022 — a judgment inter partes, by which the Applicant's own group's disputes were referred to arbitration, and which the Hon'ble Supreme Court, by judgment dated 13.08.2025 in SLP(C) No. 4775/2025 and connected matters, left the parties "free to work out their rights in accordance with."

3. THE KALE PRINCIPLES — AS EXTRACTED IN PARAGRAPH 23 OF THE JUDGMENT DATED 22.03.2024 (NOT INVOKED IN THE ABSTRACT). The said judgment holds:

"23. Further, it is well settled that unlike commercial contracts which focus on business interests, family settlements aim to preserve peace and goodwill within the family. This social objective is recognized by courts, and it has been held in umpteen judgments that it is the duty of the courts to uphold and give full effect to such an arrangement instead of disturbing the same on technical or trivial grounds. In Kale v. Dy. Director of Consolidation (supra), it has been held as under:"

and thereafter reproduces the Kale propositions that family arrangements are governed by "a special equity peculiar to themselves"; that they seek to avoid long-drawn litigation and preserve family peace; that "family arrangements are governed by principles which are not applicable to dealings between strangers"; that matters which would be fatal to similar transactions between strangers are not objections to the binding effect of a family arrangement; and that a party who has enjoyed material benefits under an arrangement is estopped from unsettling it. The Applicant's entire application — which seeks to defeat a family member's partition claim on technical grounds of nomenclature and limitation articles applicable to commercial contracts between strangers — runs directly contrary to the law of the case.

4. VERBATIM CLAUSES SHOWING SUBSISTING, EXECUTORY OBLIGATIONS (NOT A COMPLETED REPUDIATION). The Memorandum/Family Settlement Deed dated 20.06.2015, annexed to the plaint, records inter alia:

Clause 8: "Stock shall be handed over to the Third Party by the Second Party from anyone vault of the choice of the Third Party."

Clause 11: "Sh. Rahul Gupta ji, Third Party shall be entitled to receive the rent of the property coming to his share after deduction of service tax and income tax liabilities on account of rent."

The Plaintiff is the "Third Party". Undelivered stock (Clause 8) and unpaid rents (Clause 11) are continuing, executory obligations owed to a co-owner — the very antithesis of the "completed refusal/repudiation in 2015" now theorised by the Applicant.

5. THE APPLICANT'S OWN GROUP'S PLEADINGS DESTROY THE APPLICATION. The Applicant's side has, on oath, in proceedings up to the Hon'ble Supreme Court:

(i) In the Reply dated 15.12.2022 filed in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022 / Section 17 proceedings: "It is vehemently denied that in terms of the family settlement, the distribution of the stock… is still to be achieved. It is submitted and reiterated that the stocks were distributed between the family members in terms of the settlement dated 30.09.2014 as per the respective ratio of the respondents and the petitioner i.e. 77:23 respectively." — an acknowledgment of the 30.09.2014 settlement and the 77:23 ratio;

(ii) In the same proceedings, it was pleaded that "Any claim on the immovable properties before the bearing the said share of liabilities is pre-mature and cannot be awarded by this hon'ble tribunal." — a claim cannot be "premature" before the arbitrator and simultaneously "time-barred since 2015/2018" before this Hon'ble Court;

(iii) In SLP(C) No. 4775/2025 (filed by the Kamal Gupta group challenging the order dated 12.11.2024), Ground W pleads that the Plaintiff "while not acknowledging his 23% has taken control of certain assets from the family properties through the Intervenor Companies, which have been enlisted in Annexure A to alleged FSD 2019, which already form part of the share of 23% allocated to him (and may even value more than 23%)." — a 2025 admission on oath that (a) a 23% share stands "allocated to him", and (b) the Plaintiff is in control/possession of assets forming part of that share. This single ground is destructive of both limbs of the application: it negates the "ouster in 2015" theory (the Plaintiff holds assets of his share) and negates "no cause of action" (the share is allocated; only its complete working-out by metes and bounds remains);

(iv) In the SLP's List of Dates, the grievance was that the Plaintiff "refused to accept the 23% of shares as apportioned to him" — non-acceptance of an apportionment, not ouster; and the complaint (Grounds R/LL) was that the Plaintiff had "never filed any application or sought any specific relief allocating him a 23% share" — an objection that he had not yet sued, which is answered by this very suit and is the opposite of a plea that such a suit is barred;

(v) In I.A. No. 39500/2024 (filed 12.09.2024 in the disposed-of ARB.P. No. 1010/2022) and the joint written submissions of 13.10.2024, the defendants' side defended the judgment dated 22.03.2024 — including its recognition of the family settlement and the protections in favour of the Plaintiff — to persuade the Court not to recall the arbitration reference. Nowhere was it pleaded that the family settlement stood repudiated in 2015 or that claims under it were time-barred.

These acknowledgments, made within three years of the institution of the suit, are in any event acknowledgments within the meaning of Section 18 of the Limitation Act, 1963 — and whether a statement constitutes such an acknowledgment is itself a question of fact requiring evidence (Food Corporation of India v. Assam State Coop. Marketing, (2004) 12 SCC 360), fatal to a threshold rejection.

6. ARTICLE 110 — NOT ARTICLES 54/55/58 — GOVERNS, AND OUSTER IS A TRIABLE ISSUE. The right of a co-owner holding antecedent title to seek partition is a continuing right (Shub Karan Bubna v. Sita Saran Bubna, (2009) 9 SCC 689); there is no single "accrual" from which a three-year clock can run. If limitation runs at all against a family member, it is under Article 110 of the Limitation Act (suit by a person excluded from joint family property to enforce a right to share therein: twelve years from when the exclusion becomes known to the plaintiff), and exclusion/ouster must be clear, hostile and communicated (Vidya Devi @ Vidya Vati v. Prem Prakash, AIR 1995 SC 1789). On the plaint's own averments the Plaintiff remains in part/joint/constructive possession and enjoys assets of his share (as the Applicant's own group admitted in Ground W of the SLP); there is no pleaded ouster of the kind the law requires — and whether there was ouster, when it was known, and whether the settlement was acknowledged are all mixed questions of law and fact.

7. LIMITATION IS A MIXED QUESTION OF LAW AND FACT — NO O7R11(d) REJECTION ON DISPUTED FACTS. It is settled that a plaint can be rejected on limitation only where the suit is ex facie barred on the plaint's own averments as a pure question of law: Balasaria Construction (P) Ltd. v. Hanuman Seva Trust, (2006) 5 SCC 658; C. Natarajan v. Ashim Bai, (2007) 14 SCC 183; Chhotanben v. Kiritbhai Jalkrushnabhai Thakkar, (2018) 6 SCC 422 (date of knowledge a triable issue); Shakti Bhog Food Industries Ltd. v. Central Bank of India, (2020) 17 SCC 260 (plaint read as a whole; accrual fact-dependent); Nusli Neville Wadia v. Ivory Properties, (2020) 6 SCC 557; Salim D. Agboatwala v. Shamalji Oddhavji Thakkar, (2021) 20 SCC 302. The power is drastic and its conditions stringent (P.V. Guru Raj Reddy v. P. Neeradha Reddy, (2015) 8 SCC 331; Sopan Sukhdeo Sable v. Assistant Charity Commissioner, (2004) 3 SCC 137). The Applicant's own paragraph 17 assertion that "there is no factual controversy herein" is belied by his simultaneous non-admission (paragraph 2) of every averment, share, possession and allocation — a plaint disclosing triable issues cannot be rejected.

8. APPROBATE AND REPROBATE / SPECIAL EQUITY. The Applicant's group took the benefit of paragraph 23 of the judgment dated 22.03.2024 — the family-settlement special equity derived from Kale — to have their own inter se disputes referred to and preserved in arbitration, and defended that judgment in the recall proceedings. They cannot now contend that another member of the same family should be denied the benefit of the same doctrine. Non-existent legal lacunae (nomenclature, absence of a declaratory prayer, three-year commercial articles) are being set up to deprive the Plaintiff of the special equity attaching to family arrangements and to subject him to the very long-drawn attritional litigation that Kale holds it is the duty of courts to prevent. The Plaintiff is a family member with antecedent title; his suit is not to be dealt with as though it were a dealing between strangers.

B. PARA-WISE REPLY (WITHOUT PREJUDICE TO THE ABOVE)

9. Re paragraph 1: Denied. The plaint, on its own averments, is a suit for partition and consequential reliefs by a co-owner in part/joint/constructive possession of the PPJ Family Corpus, holding an antecedent 23% share acknowledged (not created) by the family arrangements of 30.09.2014, 20.06.2015 and 09.07.2019. It is denied that the claim is time-barred or is an "enforcement of an oral agreement under the guise of partition". The contents of Preliminary Submissions above are reiterated.

10. Re paragraph 2: The Applicant's blanket non-admission of every "allegation, assertion, document, arrangement, share, allocation, undertaking, valuation, possession, title" is itself fatal to the application: it concedes that everything material is disputed and triable. It is however pointed out that the Applicant's own group has, on oath before the Hon'ble Supreme Court, admitted the apportionment of a 23% share to the Plaintiff and his control of assets forming part of that share (SLP(C) No. 4775/2025, Ground W and List of Dates, extracted above).

11. Re paragraph 3: Denied. Neither clause (a) nor clause (d) of Order VII Rule 11 CPC is attracted, for the reasons set out in the Preliminary Submissions.

12. Re paragraph 4: This is an index paragraph; the "grounds" are dealt with hereinbelow.

13. Re paragraphs 5 to 9 (alleged "enforcement of oral agreement"): Denied. The submission inverts the settled law. Under Kale — as extracted in paragraph 23 of the judgment dated 22.03.2024 inter partes — a family arrangement does not create or transfer title; it acknowledges antecedent title residing in each member. A suit by a family member for partition in accordance with a family arrangement is precisely a suit of a co-owner working out his pre-existing share; it is not a suit for specific performance of a commercial contract. The reliance on "nomenclature" cuts the other way: it is the Applicant who seeks to re-characterise a partition suit as a contract suit to attract commercial limitation articles — the "clever drafting" jurisprudence (Sopan Sukhdeo Sable) prohibits re-characterisation by the defendant as much as camouflage by the plaintiff. It is further denied that the properties held through companies are outside the corpus: the Applicant's own group's pleadings treat the intervenor companies' assets as enlisted in Annexure A to the FSD 2019 and as forming part of the Plaintiff's 23% share.

14. Re paragraph 10 (absence of declaratory prayer): Denied. No declaration is required. Since the family arrangement merely acknowledges antecedent title, the Plaintiff sues on his title as co-owner, not on the contract; a co-owner seeking partition need not seek a declaration of the instrument recording the arrangement (Kale, supra — even an unregistered/oral family arrangement can be given effect and can found or corroborate the claim). The objection is a technical or trivial ground of exactly the kind paragraph 23 of the judgment dated 22.03.2024 holds must not be allowed to disturb a family arrangement. Notably, in the SLP the Applicant's group complained that the Plaintiff had not sought specific relief allocating his 23% share; now that he has sued, it is said the suit needs yet another prayer. A defendant cannot prescribe the plaintiff's prayers and then seek rejection for their absence.

15. Re paragraphs 11 and 12 (alleged knowledge of breach in 2015 / ouster by 06.11.2015 or 09.07.2019): Denied. The plaint does not plead a completed repudiation; it pleads subsisting arrangements (reaffirmed by the parties themselves in 2015 and again by the MOU of 09.07.2019 — a document the Applicant himself relies upon as a fresh reference point, thereby admitting the arrangement was alive in 2019), continuing obligations (Clauses 8 and 11 extracted above), continuing part/joint/constructive possession (paragraph 66 of the plaint), and continuing enjoyment of allocated assets (Glow Jewellery/Rawta). The Applicant's own group pleaded in the Section 17 proceedings that claims on immovable properties were "pre-mature", and in the SLP (2025) that assets "already form part of the share of 23% allocated to him". A party in possession of assets of his share, whose share is admitted as "allocated" by the opposite side in 2025, cannot be said — least of all at the threshold and without evidence — to have been ousted to his knowledge in 2015. Ouster, its hostility, its communication and the date of knowledge are triable issues (Vidya Devi; Chhotanben).

16. Re paragraphs 13 to 15 (Articles 54, 55, 58; limitation expired 2018/2022): Denied. Articles 54, 55 and 58 govern suits between contracting strangers; they have no application to a co-owner's continuing right to partition (Shub Karan Bubna). If any article applies, it is Article 110 (twelve years from known exclusion). Even on the Applicant's own untenable hypothesis of a 2019 reference point, the suit filed on 17.06.2026 would require a trial of when exclusion, if any, became known — a mixed question incapable of threshold determination. Further, the acknowledgments of 2022 (Section 17 reply), 2024 (recall proceedings) and 2025 (SLP Ground W) squarely raise Section 18 issues requiring evidence.

17. Re paragraph 16 (no continuing breach): Denied. The withholding of stock deliverable from a vault of the Plaintiff's choice (Clause 8), the non-payment of rents of properties of the Plaintiff's share (Clause 11), and the obstruction of the Plaintiff's enjoyment of allocated assets — including objections before the SDM, Kapashera concerning the Rawta property and proceedings before the NCLT concerning Glow Jewellery — are continuing wrongs and continuing denials, not a completed 2015 repudiation. The Applicant's group simultaneously seeks stays/status quo in collateral fora that freeze the Plaintiff's enjoyment of the very assets of his share, and then pleads the Plaintiff's non-enjoyment as "ouster". A party cannot rely on an exclusion of his own making.

18. Re paragraph 17 ("pure question of law"): Denied. See Preliminary Submission 7. The Applicant's own paragraph 2 disputes every fact; his group's own pleadings admit allocation and the Plaintiff's control of assets; ouster, knowledge, acknowledgment and possession are all factual. On the authorities (Balasaria; Chhotanben; Shakti Bhog; Nusli Neville Wadia; Salim D. Agboatwala), rejection under Order VII Rule 11(d) is impermissible.

19. Re paragraphs 18 and 19 (consequential reliefs): Denied. Since the foundational premise (a time-barred contract claim) is wrong, the superstructure falls. The reliefs of partition, possession, accounts, mesne profits and injunction are the ordinary reliefs of a co-owner's partition suit and are maintainable.

20. Re paragraph 20 (not a suit between coparceners): Denied. The submission is contrary to the Applicant's group's own case: throughout ARB.P. No. 1010/2022, the recall proceedings and SLP(C) No. 4775/2025, they have litigated this as a family settlement dispute concerning the PPJ Family Corpus and the Plaintiff's 23% share therein — never as a commercial contract dispute. The special equity of family arrangements (paragraph 23, judgment dated 22.03.2024) applies to precisely such family property arrangements, whether or not the properties are held through family companies; the group's own pleadings treat the company-held assets as part of the corpus apportioned 77:23.

21. Re paragraph 21: Denied for the reasons above. Neither clause (a) nor clause (d) is attracted; the plaint discloses a complete cause of action of a co-owner for partition, and no ex facie bar of limitation arises on the plaint's averments.

22. Re paragraph 22 (balance of convenience): Denied. Balance of convenience is not a ground under Order VII Rule 11 CPC. In any event, the prejudice lies entirely on the Plaintiff: the Applicant's side, having enjoyed the benefits of the family arrangement and of the judgment dated 22.03.2024, seeks to subject the Plaintiff to threshold attrition while withholding his stock, his rents and his properties. A party who has enjoyed material benefits under a family arrangement is estopped from turning around and unsettling it (Kale, as applied in the judgment dated 22.03.2024).

23. Re paragraph 23 (bona fides): Denied. The application is an abuse of process: it is a written statement in disguise, advancing merits defences without pleading them on oath, and taking stands contrary to those taken by the Applicant's own group before the Hon'ble Supreme Court and this Hon'ble Court. It deserves dismissal with exemplary costs under Sections 35A and 35B CPC.
