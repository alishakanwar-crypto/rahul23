## REPLY ON BEHALF OF THE PLAINTIFF TO THE APPLICATION UNDER ORDER VII RULE 10 CPC FILED BY DEFENDANT NO. 5

### MOST RESPECTFULLY SHOWETH:

**1.** That the present Reply is being filed on behalf of the Plaintiff in response to the Application filed by Defendant No. 5 (Mr. Mukesh Gupta) under Order VII Rule 10 CPC seeking return of the Plaint on two grounds: (i) alleged artificial valuation to confer pecuniary jurisdiction; and (ii) the suit allegedly being a "commercial dispute" under the Commercial Courts Act, 2015. Both grounds are misconceived and legally untenable.

## A. ON PECUNIARY JURISDICTION — VALUATION IS NOT ARTIFICIAL

**2.** The Application contends that the valuation of Rs. 2,00,00,000/- under the head "compensation / possession by allotment of alternate properties" is artificial and unsupported by any prayer. This submission is factually incorrect.

**3.** The Plaint discloses that properties valued by the Defendants themselves at approximately Rs. 60 Crores have already been alienated, transferred or otherwise dealt with without the Plaintiff's consent (Para 574 of the Plaint). Four specific properties have been identified as sold — Plot No. 88-A Bahadurgarh (sold 13.10.2021), Plot No. 5 Lajpat Nagar (sold 13.04.2022), Khasra No. 13/23 Daulatpur (sold 13.04.2023), and R-2 Green Park (sold 16.01.2026). The Plaintiff has expressly reserved the right to seek compensation/allotment of equivalent properties in respect of alienated assets (Para 606). The Rs. 2 Crore valuation under this head is, if anything, a **gross undervaluation** of the actual claim, not an artificial inflation.

**4.** Furthermore, the total suit valuation of Rs. 2,00,01,300/- includes MULTIPLE heads:
   - Partition of the PPJ Corpus (23% of assets valued at over Rs. 1,327 Crores)
   - Compensation for alienated properties (Rs. 60 Crores worth already sold)
   - Mesne profits from 30.09.2014 till date
   - Rendition of accounts
   - Injunctive relief

The Rs. 2 Crore head is ONE of several. Even if this one head were excluded, the remaining reliefs far exceed the pecuniary threshold of this Hon'ble Court.

**5.** The Plaintiff, as dominus litis, has the prerogative to value the suit for court fee and jurisdiction purposes. The Defendant cannot dictate valuation at this stage.

**Case Law:**
- **Khatri Hotels Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India, (2011) 9 SCC 126:** The plaintiff has the right to value the suit for purposes of jurisdiction and court fee; the court cannot reject the valuation at threshold unless patently absurd.
- **Tara Devi v. Sri Thakur Radha Krishna Maharaj, (1987) 4 SCC 69:** The valuation in the plaint, unless shown to be arbitrary or mala fide, should be accepted for purposes of jurisdiction.

## B. ON COMMERCIAL COURTS ACT — PARTITION IS NOT A "COMMERCIAL DISPUTE"

**6.** The Application's second ground — that the suit involves "commercial disputes" triable only before the Commercial Division — is wholly misconceived and represents a fundamental misunderstanding of the Commercial Courts Act, 2015.

**7.** The present suit is a **suit for partition of family assets based on an Oral Family Settlement**. It is NOT a dispute "arising out of" commercial transactions or agreements relating to immovable property used in trade or commerce. The nature of the DISPUTE — not the nature of the PROPERTY — determines whether the Commercial Courts Act applies.

**8.** Section 2(1)(c)(vii) of the Commercial Courts Act defines a "commercial dispute" as arising out of *"agreements relating to immovable property used exclusively in trade or commerce."* The operative words are **"agreements relating to immovable property"** — meaning commercial agreements/transactions concerning the property. An Oral Family Settlement for partition of family assets is NOT a "commercial agreement" within the meaning of this provision, regardless of whether some of the assets happen to be commercial properties.

**Case Law:**
- **Ambalal Sarabhai Enterprise v. K.S. Infraspace LLP, (2020) 15 SCC 585:** The nature of the dispute and its connection with trade/commerce determines whether it qualifies as a "commercial dispute." A dispute between family members regarding partition of assets does not become a "commercial dispute" merely because the assets include commercial properties.

**9.** The Applicant's own co-Defendant (Defendant No. 1, Mr. Kamal Gupta) has filed a separate application under Order VII Rule 11(b) and (c) treating the suit as a **partition suit** and challenging the court fee on that basis. **The Defendants cannot simultaneously argue that this is a partition suit (for court fee purposes) AND a commercial suit (for jurisdiction purposes).** These are mutually contradictory positions revealing a coordinated strategy to delay the proceedings.

**10.** Even assuming arguendo that any relief touches upon "commercial property," the proper remedy is NOT return of the plaint. At most, the suit would be re-designated from CS(OS) to CS(COMM) within the SAME court — the Delhi High Court. Return of plaint under O.VII R.10 to a different court is not warranted, as the pecuniary jurisdiction remains with this Hon'ble Court.
