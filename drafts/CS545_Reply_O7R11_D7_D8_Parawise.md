## REPLY ON BEHALF OF THE PLAINTIFF TO THE APPLICATION UNDER ORDER VII RULE 11 READ WITH SECTION 151 CPC FILED BY DEFENDANT NOS. 7 AND 8 (MR. PAWAN GUPTA AND MRS. RENU GUPTA) SEEKING REJECTION OF THE PLAINT

MOST RESPECTFULLY SHOWETH:

## PRELIMINARY SUBMISSIONS / OBJECTIONS

1. The present Reply is filed on behalf of the Plaintiff to the application dated 21.06.2026 filed by Defendant Nos. 7 and 8 under Order VII Rule 11 read with Section 151 CPC seeking rejection of the plaint. The application is misconceived, proceeds on a selective, distorted and suppressive reading of the plaint, mischaracterises the nature of the suit, misstates the judicial record, and is only the latest step in the Applicants' sustained campaign to stall, obstruct and derail the adjudication of the Plaintiff's rights. It deserves dismissal with exemplary costs. The contents of the application are denied in their entirety save what is specifically admitted hereinafter, and the para-wise reply below is without prejudice to these preliminary submissions, each of which may be read as part of the reply to each paragraph of the application.

2. **The Applicants' conduct disentitles them to any indulgence — SLP(C) No. 22163/2026 and the Plaintiff's Section 340 CrPC application.** The Applicants (Mr. Pawan Gupta and Mrs. Renu Gupta) have simultaneously approached the Hon'ble Supreme Court by way of Special Leave to Appeal (C) No. 22163/2026 against the simple order dated 19.06.2026 passed by this Hon'ble Court — an order by which this Hon'ble Court did nothing more than issue notice on the Plaintiff's applications. In the said SLP, the Applicants have pleaded on oath that copies of the plaint and accompanying applications had not been supplied to them and that they were denied any opportunity to respond — a plea demonstrably falsified by the Affidavit of Service on the record of this Hon'ble Court (service effected on 18.06.2026 by e-mail on pawan@ppj.co.in and accountsdept@ppj.co.in and by WhatsApp, and again on 20.06.2026 pursuant to this Hon'ble Court's directions) and by the Applicants' own conduct in filing, on 21.06.2026, the present detailed application analysing the plaint paragraph by paragraph, prayer by prayer and valuation by valuation. The Plaintiff has accordingly filed an application under Section 340 read with Section 195 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 (corresponding to Sections 379 and 215 of the BNSS, 2023) dated 23.06.2026 in the said SLP(C) No. 22163/2026 seeking initiation of perjury proceedings against the Applicants for deliberately making false statements and suppressing material facts before the Hon'ble Supreme Court. A litigant who tells the Hon'ble Supreme Court on oath that he has not even received the plaint, while simultaneously filing before this Hon'ble Court a granular Order VII Rule 11 application founded on a minute analysis of that very plaint, approbates and reprobates and does not come to Court with clean hands. Such a litigant cannot invoke the discretionary and drastic jurisdiction of Order VII Rule 11 CPC.

3. **A pattern of obstructive litigation.** The application is not one filed to assist the Court but to stall the Plaintiff's urgent interim application (I.A. No. 16084/2026 under Order XXXIX Rules 1 and 2 CPC) seeking protection of the Plaintiff's 23% share in the PP Jewellers Family Corpus. The aforesaid SLP is the third proceeding initiated by Defendant No. 7 before the Hon'ble Supreme Court in relation to the disputes concerning the PPJ Family Corpus — the first being SLP(C) No. 18873/2024 (withdrawn on 20.12.2024), and the second being SLP(C) D. No. 9078/2025 (decided by the judgment dated 13.08.2025) — a pattern of litigation directed at delaying and frustrating the adjudication of the Plaintiff's claims.

4. **The true nature of the suit: partition and working out of an antecedent, crystallised share — not specific performance.** The entire application is built on a single mischaracterisation: that the suit is a disguised suit for specific performance of the family arrangement. It is not. The suit is founded primarily on the oral family partition dated 30.09.2014, under which the Plaintiff's 23% share in the PPJ Family Corpus stood recognised and crystallised — the Memorandum dated 20.06.2015 and the document dated 09.07.2019 being merely records/memoranda of the antecedent arrangement. It is settled since *Kale v. Deputy Director of Consolidation*, (1976) 3 SCC 119, that a family settlement is not a transfer or a contract creating rights de novo; it works on the footing of antecedent title, and each member takes the properties in recognition of pre-existing rights. A suit to work out, by metes and bounds, a share already recognised in a family settlement is a suit for partition, maintainable with a preliminary decree under Order XX Rule 18 CPC; it is not a suit for specific performance of a contract attracting Section 16(c) of the Specific Relief Act, 1963 or Article 54 of the Limitation Act, 1963.

5. **The plaint pleads joint and constructive possession — suppressed by the Applicants.** Paragraph 66 of the plaint expressly pleads that the Plaintiff continues to remain a co-owner and co-sharer in the entire PPJ Family Corpus, is deemed to be in joint and constructive possession thereof, and has at all material times remained in possession, control and management of certain assets and businesses forming part of his allocated share. In law, the possession of one co-sharer is the possession of all; exclusion from particular premises is not ouster from co-ownership. Whether the Plaintiff stands "ousted" in the legal sense — hostile animus, exclusive possession to his knowledge, exclusion from co-ownership rights — is a question of fact par excellence, incapable of resolution on demurrer at the Order VII Rule 11 stage.

6. **The Applicants' own prior pleadings contradict the ouster premise.** The Pawan Gupta Group has itself pleaded, in its Reply/Objections in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022, that the Plaintiff had "taken control of the assets of the Joint Family Group", was "in control of multiple businesses mentioned in Annexure A to the 2019 MOU", and "admittedly controls" companies of the group *(counsel to verify paragraph numbers against the certified copy)*. Having pleaded on oath that the Plaintiff possesses and controls substantial assets of the corpus, the Applicants cannot now assert his complete ouster for the collateral purpose of a court-fee and limitation objection. This is approbation and reprobation.

7. **The suit is the very "working out of rights" directed by the Hon'ble Supreme Court.** By the judgment dated 13.08.2025 in SLP(C) No. 4775/2025 and connected matters (2025 INSC 975), the Hon'ble Supreme Court, while setting aside the order dated 12.11.2024 on the ground that applications could not have been entertained in a disposed-of Section 11 petition, expressly held (paragraph 25): "The parties to the present proceedings are free to work out their rights in accordance with the order dated 22.03.2024." The Plaintiff, admittedly a non-signatory to the FSDs and expressly held (order dated 22.03.2024) not to be a necessary party to and not bound by the arbitration, can work out his rights only by an independent civil suit. The present suit is precisely that working out. Far from being barred, it is the remedy the judicial record itself contemplates.

8. **The settled standard under Order VII Rule 11 CPC.** The plaint must be read meaningfully and as a whole, its averments taken as true, and the defendant's defence cannot be looked into at this stage (*T. Arivandandam v. T.V. Satyapal*, (1977) 4 SCC 467; *Dahiben v. Arvindbhai Kalyanji Bhanusali*, (2020) 7 SCC 366). Tested on that standard, the plaint discloses a clear cause of action for partition, is properly valued, is within limitation, and is barred by no law. Further, under clauses (b) and (c) of Order VII Rule 11 rejection is not the first step: the Court must first require correction of the valuation or supply of the requisite stamp paper within a time fixed by the Court, and rejection can follow only upon default; the plaint (paragraph 65) contains the Plaintiff's express undertaking to make good any deficiency as directed.

## PARA-WISE REPLY

9. **Reply to paragraph 1 of the application** (application stated to be filed by Defendant Nos. 7 and 8 at the threshold, summons yet to be issued): The filing of the application is a matter of record; the rest is denied. It is denied that there is any warrant for "rejection at the threshold". It is significant that even while appearing before this Hon'ble Court pursuant to notice, the Applicants simultaneously carried a false case of non-service to the Hon'ble Supreme Court in SLP(C) No. 22163/2026 — conduct set out in Preliminary Submission 2 above and in respect of which the Plaintiff's application under Section 340 CrPC dated 23.06.2026 stands filed before the Hon'ble Supreme Court.

10. **Reply to paragraph 2 of the application** (application stated to be without prejudice, plaint denied, reliance on *T. Arivandandam* for "nipping in the bud"): The contents are denied save that the pendency of the application is a matter of record. *T. Arivandandam* condemns vexatious and meritless litigation — a description that fits the present application, not the plaint. The same authority requires a meaningful — not formal — reading of the plaint **as a whole**, which the application studiously avoids by suppressing paragraph 66 of the plaint and the possession pleadings.

11. **Reply to paragraph 3 of the application** (claim allegedly rests entirely on the FSDs; Plaintiff a "stranger" to both instruments; share "at best a contractual allocation"): Denied. The Plaintiff's claim rests primarily on the oral family partition dated 30.09.2014, of which the 1st FSD dated 20.06.2015 and the document dated 09.07.2019 are merely records. That the Plaintiff is not a signatory to the FSDs is precisely why his rights were expressly insulated in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022 (order dated 22.03.2024, paragraphs 17 and 18) and why an independent civil suit is his only remedy. A share under a family settlement is a share in recognition of antecedent title (*Kale*, supra) — not a "contractual allocation"; the label "stranger" is both wrong and self-defeating, for a stranger's property could not have been included in the Applicants' own FSD annexures at all.

12. **Reply to paragraph 4 of the application** (suit alleged to be, in pith and substance, for specific performance and not partition): Denied. The suit is for partition and working out of the Plaintiff's antecedent and crystallised 23% share by a preliminary decree under Order XX Rule 18 CPC, in terms of the liberty recognised by the Hon'ble Supreme Court on 13.08.2025. The reliefs of possession/delivery are consequential to partition, not freestanding claims for enforcement of a contract.

13. **Reply to paragraph 5 of the application** (prayer clauses said to show "enforcement of a concluded arrangement"): Denied. The prayers, read with the plaint as a whole, seek the working out of an already-crystallised share — which is exactly what a partition decree does: it recognises pre-existing shares and separates them by metes and bounds. Prayer C itself recites that title always resided in the Plaintiff and that no fresh acquisition is sought — the language of antecedent title, not of contract enforcement. The Applicants' argument, taken to its logical end, would convert every partition suit founded on a family settlement into a specific-performance suit — a proposition contrary to *Kale* (supra) and to a century of partition jurisprudence.

14. **Reply to paragraph 6 of the application** (partition suits allegedly presuppose unascertained shares; shares here already "settled and allocated"): Denied. A partition suit does not cease to be one because the shares are defined; indeed every partition suit proceeds on definite shares — the decree works them out by metes and bounds. Where a family settlement has recognised shares but division and delivery remain incomplete, the co-sharer's remedy is a suit for partition/working out with a preliminary decree under Order XX Rule 18 CPC. The grievance of non-implementation of a family settlement operating on antecedent title sounds in partition, not specific performance.

15. **Reply to paragraph 7 of the application** (alleged evasion of Section 16(c) SRA, ad valorem court fee and Article 54 limitation): Denied. There is no evasion because none of the three rigours applies: (a) Section 16(c) of the Specific Relief Act applies to contract-enforcement suits, not partition suits on antecedent title; (b) the court fee payable by a co-owner in joint and constructive possession is fixed court fee under Article 17(vi) of Schedule II of the Court Fees Act, 1870; and (c) Article 54 of the Limitation Act has no application to a continuing right to seek partition. The sub-paragraphs (a) to (c) are denied accordingly.

16. **Reply to paragraph 8 of the application** (substance over form; "clever drafting"; reliance on *T. Arivandandam*, *ITC*, *Dahiben*, *Madanuri*): The propositions of law, in the abstract, are not disputed; their application is denied. The selfsame authorities mandate that the plaint be read meaningfully and as a whole, taking its averments as true, and that the defendant's defence cannot be looked into. Tested on that standard, the plaint discloses a clear cause of action for partition. It is the application — which suppresses paragraph 66 of the plaint, the possession pleadings and the Applicants' own recorded assurances — that is the exercise in clever drafting.

17. **Reply to paragraph 9 of the application** (alleged gross undervaluation; recital of plaint values and Prayer C properties): Denied as misleading. The values disclosed in the plaint reflect candour in pleading the extent of the corpus; they do not determine the court fee for a partition suit by a co-owner in joint and constructive possession, which carries fixed court fee under Article 17(vi) of Schedule II of the Court Fees Act, 1870. Valuation for pecuniary jurisdiction and valuation for court fee are distinct concepts; the plaint (paragraph 64) sets out a relief-wise valuation table in accordance with law.

18. **Reply to paragraph 10 of the application** (suit valuation of INR 2,00,02,200/- allegedly "engineered"; fixed fees on Prayer A and other reliefs): Denied. The valuation is in accordance with the settled rule for partition suits by co-owners in joint/constructive possession; there is nothing "engineered" about paying the court fee the law prescribes. The Applicants' real complaint is with the law itself, not with the plaint.

19. **Reply to paragraph 11 of the application** (Plaintiff allegedly out of possession on his own pleadings; paragraph 66 dismissed as a "bare legal conclusion"): Emphatically denied. Paragraph 66 of the plaint pleads facts: continuing co-ownership, joint and constructive possession, and actual possession, control and management of certain assets and businesses forming part of the Plaintiff's allocated share at all material times. Exclusion from a residence or particular premises in 2015 is not, in law, ouster from co-ownership of the corpus. Whether the alleged conduct amounts to ouster — hostile animus, exclusive possession to the Plaintiff's knowledge, exclusion from co-ownership rights — is a question of fact for trial, not demurrer. The Applicants' own group has pleaded on oath in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022 that the Plaintiff had "taken control of the assets of the Joint Family Group" and "admittedly controls" group companies — pleadings squarely destructive of the ouster premise (Preliminary Submission 6).

20. **Reply to paragraph 12 of the application** (reliance on *Neelavathi v. N. Natarajan* and *Suhrid Singh v. Randhir Singh* for ad valorem court fee): Denied as an incorrect application of both authorities. *Neelavathi*, (1980) 2 SCC 247, in fact assists the Plaintiff: it holds that the question of court fee must be considered on the plaint averments alone, that possession of one co-owner is presumed to be the possession of all, and that mere non-participation in profits or exclusion from particular premises does not amount to ouster displacing constructive possession. *Suhrid Singh*, (2010) 12 SCC 112, concerned a non-executant seeking cancellation of sale deeds and recovery of possession — it has no application to a partition suit on antecedent title. The residual assertion that the suit is "in substance for specific performance" is denied for the reasons in Preliminary Submission 4 and paragraphs 12 to 16 above.

21. **Reply to paragraph 13 of the application** (suit allegedly time-barred; events of 2014, 2015, 2019 and 2022): Denied. Limitation is a mixed question of law and fact and can found rejection under Order VII Rule 11(d) only where the plaint on its face and without more shows the suit to be hopelessly barred — which it does not. The right of a co-owner in joint and constructive possession to seek partition is a continuing right. The plaint further pleads continuing acknowledgements, part-implementation, and subsequent events — including the proceedings of 2022–2025 in which the Defendant groups themselves repeatedly assured judicial fora that the Plaintiff's rights and interests would not be affected — each furnishing a fresh and continuing cause of action. These are matters of evidence.

22. **Reply to paragraph 14 of the application** (Articles 54, 58 and 59 of the Limitation Act; accrual by 2015/2019/2022; suit "hopelessly barred"): Denied. Articles 54, 58 and 59 are all premised on the Applicants' erroneous re-characterisation of the suit as one for specific performance/declaration/cancellation. The suit seeks none of those reliefs as its substance; it seeks partition and working out of an antecedent share. No article prescribing three years applies; the suit is well within limitation.

23. **Reply to paragraph 15 of the application** (plaint allegedly founded on an "undertaking recorded in Para 18 of the order dated 22.03.2024"): Denied as framed. The plaint relies on the order dated 22.03.2024 as a whole, including paragraph 17 (recording the categorical statement of learned senior counsel for the petitioners and respondents — which includes the Applicants' group — that none of the properties owned by the intervenor companies would be dealt with/divided in the arbitration) and paragraph 18 (recording this Hon'ble Court's finding that "the intent of Pawan Gupta Group and Kamal Gupta Group when they executed FSD, was to settle their inter se disputes while at the same time not affecting the rights and interest of Mr. Rahul Gupta").

24. **Reply to paragraph 16 of the application** (paragraph 18 allegedly contains "no undertaking whatsoever"): Denied. Paragraph 18 records, as the very basis of the Court's decision, that the two groups' intent in executing the FSDs was to settle their inter se disputes "while at the same time not affecting the rights and interest of Mr. Rahul Gupta". Most significantly, the Applicants' own group, in its reply to I.A. No. 39500/2024 in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022, expressly pleaded that "the assurances provided in paras 17 and 18 protect the interests of non-parties" and that "the assurances given in paras 17 and 18 of the judgment dated 22.03.2024 have been fully adhered to." Having themselves characterised paragraphs 17 and 18 as **assurances** protecting the Plaintiff's interests and pleaded scrupulous adherence thereto, the Applicants cannot now contend that those paragraphs contain nothing at all.

25. **Reply to paragraph 17 of the application** (paragraph 17 said to relate only to the intervenor companies' properties): The text of paragraph 17 is a matter of record. It is denied that the plaint misuses it. Paragraph 17 and paragraph 18 operate together: the intervenor companies' properties were expressly kept out, and the Plaintiff's rights and interests were expressly declared to be unaffected. Both are part of the protective framework on which the plaint relies.

26. **Reply to paragraph 18 of the application** (alleged "fabrication and misattribution" of an undertaking; alleged design to mislead): Emphatically denied. There is no fabrication or misattribution; at its very highest, the point is one of characterisation of judicial observations — an arguable matter of construction, which is never a ground under Order VII Rule 11 CPC and does not permit a mini-trial on the correctness of pleaded quotations. It does not lie in the mouths of these Applicants — who told the Hon'ble Supreme Court on oath that they had never been served with this very plaint while simultaneously filing this granular analysis of it — to accuse the Plaintiff of misleading any court.

27. **Reply to paragraph 19 of the application** (Applicants stated to have moved a perjury application; Plaintiff allegedly disentitled to relief for unclean hands): Denied. The threatened/filed perjury application is a pressure tactic and will be answered in accordance with law. It is, in fact, the Applicants against whom an application under Section 340 read with Section 195 CrPC dated 23.06.2026 stands filed by the Plaintiff before the Hon'ble Supreme Court in SLP(C) No. 22163/2026, for their demonstrably false statements on oath that copies of the plaint and applications had not been supplied to them — statements falsified by the Affidavit of Service on record and by their own detailed applications before this Hon'ble Court. The doctrine of clean hands applies with full force — against the Applicants.

28. **Reply to paragraph 20 of the application** (recital of ARB.P. No. 1010/2022 and refusal of the Plaintiff's intervention): The filing and disposal of ARB.P. No. 1010/2022 are matters of record. It is denied that the order dated 22.03.2024 operates against the Plaintiff. The order held that the Plaintiff, as a non-signatory, was neither a necessary party to nor **bound by** the arbitration — while simultaneously recording the assurances in paragraphs 17 and 18. A finding that the Plaintiff has no place *in the arbitration* is the very reason he must sue *in a civil court*; it cannot conceivably bar the civil suit.

29. **Reply to paragraph 21 of the application** (post-disposal applications allegedly seeking the "selfsame reliefs" as the present suit): Denied. The post-disposal applications concerned the scope and conduct of the arbitration; the present suit seeks partition and working out of the Plaintiff's share against all necessary parties in a properly constituted civil action. The reliefs are neither identical nor before the same forum, and no forum has ever adjudicated the Plaintiff's share on merits.

30. **Reply to paragraph 22 of the application** (I.A. No. 39500/2024 and the order dated 12.11.2024): The order dated 12.11.2024 is a matter of record. The Plaintiff does not rely upon it as a subsisting order.

31. **Reply to paragraph 23 of the application** (judgment dated 13.08.2025 setting aside the order dated 12.11.2024): The judgment is a matter of record, but the Applicants suppress its operative liberty: while setting aside the order dated 12.11.2024 on the procedural ground that applications could not be entertained in a disposed-of Section 11 petition, the Hon'ble Supreme Court expressly held that "the parties to the present proceedings are free to work out their rights in accordance with the order dated 22.03.2024." The present suit is that working out.

32. **Reply to paragraph 24 of the application** (the "substratum" of the plaint allegedly set aside; costs of Rs. 3,00,000/-): Denied. The Hon'ble Supreme Court did not adjudicate — much less negate — the Plaintiff's 23% share; it decided only that the disposed-of Section 11 proceedings could not be reopened by applications, and imposed costs referable to that procedural course alone. The substratum of the plaint is the oral family partition dated 30.09.2014 and the Plaintiff's antecedent title — matters never adjudicated by any court.

33. **Reply to paragraph 25 of the application** (suit allegedly a fresh attempt to obtain "the selfsame relief" refused/set aside): Denied. No court has ever refused the Plaintiff's claim to his 23% share on merits: the order dated 22.03.2024 declined intervention *in an arbitration* while protecting his rights; the judgment dated 13.08.2025 set aside the order dated 12.11.2024 on procedure while leaving parties free to work out their rights. It is a strange doctrine under which a party held to have no remedy in one forum is thereby barred from every forum.

34. **Reply to paragraph 26 of the application** (res judicata, constructive res judicata, forum shopping, abuse of process, Order VII Rule 11(d)): Denied. Res judicata under Section 11 CPC requires a former **suit** in which the matter directly and substantially in issue was heard and **finally decided on merits** between the same parties. Neither the intervention application in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022 (a Section 11 proceeding for appointment of an arbitrator), nor the post-disposal applications therein, nor the SLP proceedings, was a suit; and no court has ever adjudicated the existence or extent of the Plaintiff's 23% share on merits. There is no forum shopping in filing the only competent proceeding — a civil suit — expressly contemplated by the Hon'ble Supreme Court's liberty of 13.08.2025. The plaint is barred by no law; the plea under Order VII Rule 11(d) fails.

35. **Reply to paragraph 27 of the application** (power under Order VII Rule 11 said to be mandatory; plaint allegedly discloses no cause of action, barred, undervalued, founded on falsehood): Denied. No ground under Order VII Rule 11 is made out: the plaint, read meaningfully and as a whole, discloses a complete cause of action for partition; it is properly valued and stamped; it is within limitation; it is barred by no law; and it is founded on the judicial record, not on any false statement. Even qua clauses (b) and (c), the mandatory statutory sequence — opinion, time to correct, default — has not even commenced, and the plaint (paragraph 65) contains the Plaintiff's undertaking to make good any deficiency as directed.

36. **Reply to paragraph 28 of the application** (application stated to be bona fide; alleged prejudice to the Applicants and stalling of the arbitration): Denied. The application is neither bona fide nor in the interest of justice; it is a delaying device filed alongside a Supreme Court petition founded on a false non-service plea. The plea of prejudice from "stalling of the arbitration" is contrived: the Plaintiff is admittedly not a party to the arbitration, and this suit concerns his independent proprietary rights which, on the Applicants' own repeated assurances recorded in judicial orders, the arbitration will not touch. It is the Plaintiff who suffers grave prejudice from the Applicants' obstruction of his urgent interim application.

37. **Reply to paragraph 29 of the application** (application stated to be confined to the plaint averments; rights reserved): The professed confinement to the plaint is belied by the application's suppression of paragraph 66 and the possession pleadings of the very plaint it purports to analyse. The reservation of rights is a matter of record; the Plaintiff equally reserves all rights and contentions.

38. **Reply to the Prayer of the application:** It is denied that the plaint is liable to be rejected under Order VII Rule 11 CPC or at all. The application deserves dismissal with exemplary costs.
