## PART I — THE DEFENDANTS' APPLICATIONS AND OUR ANSWERS

The Defendants have mounted a coordinated threshold attack through different counsel — **I.A. 16112/2026** (D-1 Kamal Gupta, O.VII R.11 — court fee), **I.A. 16113/2026** (D-5 Mukesh Gupta, O.VII R.10 — jurisdiction), **I.A. 16114/2026** (D-5 Mukesh Gupta, O.VII R.11 — limitation), plus **two further applications by D-7 & D-8 (Pawan & Renu Gupta)**: an O.VII R.11 application dated 21.06.2026 and a Section 340 CrPC/S.379 BNSS perjury application dated 22.06.2026 (both from the case folder; I.A. numbers to be confirmed from the court record). The attacks are **mutually contradictory** — and, crucially, they emanate from **one and the same group**: Defendant No. 5 (Mr. Mukesh Gupta) is himself part of the **Kamal Gupta group** and acts in concert with Defendant No. 1. The "different defendants through different counsel" presentation is a façade — it is the same group speaking through two mouths, taking irreconcilable stands. That is our opening point:

> **D-1 (Kamal Gupta) says** it *is* a partition suit but wrongly court-fee'd (ad valorem, because Plaintiff is "ousted").
> **D-5 (Mukesh Gupta) says** it is *not* a partition suit at all — it is enforcement of an oral agreement and time-barred; and *also* that it is a *commercial* dispute for the Commercial Division.
> They cannot have it all three ways. Either it is a partition suit (no limitation; fixed court fee for a co-owner in constructive possession), or an enforcement suit (limitation restarted by their own acknowledgments), or a commercial suit (which stays in this Court anyway, only re-designated). Because D-1 and D-5 are of the **same group acting in concert**, these are not independent bona fide objections of separately-affected defendants but a single litigant's contradictory pleas split across two names — an abuse of process. A party (and those claiming with/through it) cannot approbate and reprobate: having taken the stand through D-1 that this is a partition suit, the group cannot through D-5 assert it is not one. The coordinated but contradictory strategy reveals the sole purpose — delay while the corpus is dissipated. (Costs I.A.s 16109, 16110 and 17519/2026 already on file against exactly this conduct.)

### A. I.A. 16112/2026 — D-1 Kamal Gupta's application under O.VII R.11(b) & (c) — court fee / undervaluation

**Their case:** Plaintiff pleads "ouster" (Nov. 2015) → not in joint/constructive possession → must pay ad valorem fee on market value of 23% share; attacks Prayers B and C.

**Our points:**

1. **Suppression of Para 66 of the plaint — the dispositive paragraph.** The application does not quote, cite or even acknowledge Para 66, which expressly pleads: *"the Plaintiff… continues to remain a co-owner and co-sharer in the entire PP Jewellers Family Corpus and is deemed to be in joint and constructive possession thereof… not a suit by an excluded stranger seeking recovery of possession, but a suit by a co-owner and co-sharer in joint and constructive possession seeking partition by metes and bounds."* At the O.VII R.11 stage the plaint averments alone are read and taken as true — *Dahiben v. Arvindbhai*, (2020) 7 SCC 366; *Sopan Sukhdeo Sable*, (2004) 3 SCC 137 (plaint read as a whole, not sentences culled out).
2. **Ouster from the family house ≠ loss of co-ownership.** The plaint pleads exclusion from the family residence and business management after the assault of 06–07.11.2015 — but the Plaintiff is in *physical* possession of the Rawta lands (Para 55) and in *constructive* possession of 2129-2130 Karol Bagh and the Mumbai property (Para 56). A co-owner's constructive possession subsists until partition — *Suraj Bhan*, (2007) 6 SCC 186; *Dorab Cawasji Warden*, (1990) 2 SCC 117.
3. **Joint/constructive possession = fixed court fee only — no ad valorem fee.** Where the plaintiff co-owner is in joint or constructive possession, a partition suit attracts only fixed court fee under Art. 17(vi), Sch. II, Court Fees Act; ad valorem fee under S.7(iv)(b)/(v) applies only where the plaintiff is actually excluded/ousted. *Neelavathi v. N. Natarajan*, (1980) 2 SCC 247 — the possession of one co-owner is in law the possession of all co-owners; exclusion is not to be assumed and must be specifically established, and at the threshold the plaint averments are taken as true. See also *Satheedevi v. Prasanna Kumar*, (2010) 5 SCC 622; *Suhrid Singh v. Randhir Singh*, (2010) 12 SCC 112 (fee depends on the relief as framed by the plaintiff). Para 66 of the plaint expressly pleads joint and constructive possession of the entire PPJ Family Corpus — fixed fee squarely applies. **Possession of even one joint property = constructive possession of all.** It is settled that a co-owner in possession of any part of the joint estate is in law in joint/constructive possession of the whole — the possession of one co-sharer enures to all (*Neelavathi*, supra; *Sant Ram Nagina Ram v. Daya Ram Nagina Ram*, AIR 1961 P&H 528 (FB) principles as consistently applied). Here the plaint specifically pleads the Plaintiff's actual physical possession of the Village Rawta lands (Glow Jewellery) and constructive possession of 2129-2130 Karol Bagh and 24A-24B Mumbai — being in possession of part of the corpus, he is deemed in constructive possession of the entire PPJ Family Corpus; the fixed-fee position is unanswerable. And every partition suit seeks separate possession — that does not convert it into a possession suit (*Meenakshisundaram*, (1979) 3 SCC 481); the prayer for separate possession of the share is incidental to partition.
4. **Court fee already paid — the objection is academic.** Court fee of Rs. 2,00,000/- was paid on 22.06.2026 itself and the receipt stands filed on the record. The application, which proceeds on the premise of non-payment/deficiency, is thus academic on its own terms. In any event, even a genuine fee deficiency ≠ rejection: Section 149 CPC applies and the Plaintiff undertakes to pay any additional fee as directed — *Noor Mohammad v. Jethanand*, (2013) 5 SCC 202. O.VII R.11 is a drastic power exercised sparingly — *T. Arivandandam*, (1977) 4 SCC 467.
5. **Costs:** the deliberate suppression of Para 66 in an application verified as "nothing material has been concealed" grounds the exemplary-costs I.A.s.
6. **The Defendants cannot invoke the revenue while defrauding the revenue.** D-1 presses court fee on the valuations in the 2015 document, yet suppresses that the very properties for which prayers are sought have already been sold off by the Defendants themselves at awfully low considerations — far below circle rates — thereby evading stamp duty. The object of court-fee and stamp law is protection of the revenue; if the revenue's interest is engaged at all, it is the Defendants' own undervalued sale deeds that warrant reference by this Court to the Collector of Stamps/revenue authorities (S.33/S.47-A Stamp Act principles), not rejection of the Plaintiff's plaint. A party cannot approbate the revenue's cause to defeat the suit while itself evading that very revenue.

   **The suppressed-value sales — from I.A. 17519/2026 (Defendants' own 20.06.2015 Memorandum valuations vs their own sale deeds):**

   | S.No. | Property | Valuation per Memorandum made by Defendants on 20.06.2015 | Valuation per sale deed executed by Defendants in favour of third parties (2021/2022/2025) |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 1 | 2129-2130 Gurudwara Road, Karol Bagh | Rs. 30,00,00,000 | Rs. 5,00,00,000 (sold in 2025) |
   | 2 | 16/2699, Naiwala/DBG Road, Karol Bagh | Rs. 50,00,00,000 | Rs. 4,49,72,000 (sold in 2022 — to a company of the daughter-in-law of Defendant No. 5) |
   | 3 | 88-A, Sector-16, HSIIDC, Village Jakhodai Kassar, Bahadurgarh, Distt. Jhajjar | Rs. 17,00,00,000 | Rs. 7,31,47,500 (sold in 2021) |

   Properties valued by the Defendants themselves at ~Rs. 97 Cr in 2015 sold for ~Rs. 16.8 Cr — a suppression of ~Rs. 80 Cr in disclosed consideration, with corresponding evasion of stamp duty. **Ask: reference of these sale deeds to the State revenue department / Collector of Stamps.**

### B. I.A. 16113/2026 — D-5 Mukesh Gupta's application under O.VII R.10 — return of plaint (jurisdiction / Commercial Courts Act)

**Their case:** (i) the Rs. 2 Cr "alternate properties" valuation head is artificial, inserted only to cross the DHC pecuniary threshold; (ii) the properties are commercial → "commercial dispute" under S.2(1)(c)(vii) of the Commercial Courts Act → Commercial Division.

**Our points:**

1. **Valuation not artificial.** The head is founded on the plaint’s valuation head F — compensation/possession by allotment of alternate properties from the Residual PPJ Corpus in lieu of alienated properties, valued at Rs. 2,00,00,000 — read with Para 46 (assets discovered through DORIS/NGDRS to have been alienated for assets already alienated — four sales identified, incl. R-2 Green Park on 16.01.2026); properties worth ~Rs. 60 Cr already alienated; if anything the Rs. 2 Cr figure is a gross *under*-statement. And it is only one of several heads — the 23% claim in a corpus exceeding Rs. 1,327 Cr crosses the threshold on any view. Plaintiff is dominus litis; valuation is not rejected at threshold unless patently absurd — *Khatri Hotels*, (2011) 9 SCC 126; *Tara Devi*, (1987) 4 SCC 69.
2. **A family partition suit is not a "commercial dispute."** S.2(1)(c)(vii) covers *agreements* relating to immovable property used exclusively in trade or commerce — the nature of the *dispute*, not of the *property*, governs — *Ambalal Sarabhai Enterprise*, (2020) 15 SCC 585. An oral family settlement is not a commercial transaction.
3. **Even arguendo, the remedy is re-designation, not return.** The pecuniary jurisdiction remains with this Court; at worst the suit is re-numbered CS(COMM). O.VII R.10 return to a *different court* is not warranted.
4. **Same group, contradictory mouths:** D-5 is of the Kamal Gupta group itself and acts in concert with D-1. D-1 treats it as a partition suit for the court-fee attack; D-5 as a commercial suit for the jurisdiction attack. This is not two defendants with distinct grievances but one group approbating and reprobating through two counsel — by itself a ground to reject the application with costs.

### C. I.A. 16114/2026 — D-5 Mukesh Gupta's application under O.VII R.11(a) & (d) — limitation / no cause of action

**Their case:** the suit is "really" enforcement of the oral agreement of 30.09.2014; limitation (3 yrs, Arts. 54/55/58) ran from the 2015 ouster or the 2019 MOU; no coparcenary → no partition; suit of 17.06.2026 time-barred.

**Our points:**

1. **No limitation for a partition suit by a co-owner.** The right to seek partition is an inherent, continuing incident of co-ownership — *K. Laxmanan v. Thekkayil Padmini*, (2009) 1 SCC 354. The plaint (Para 66) pleads exactly such a suit; at the R.11 stage the Defendant's re-characterisation is impermissible.
2. **Even on their own "enforcement" theory — Section 18 Limitation Act.** Every acknowledgment restarts limitation, and the Defendants have acknowledged the 23% share repeatedly *after* 2014: the 2015 Memorandum; the 2019 MOU; **the undertaking recorded in para 18 of this Hon'ble Court's own order dated 22.03.2024 in ARB.P. 1010/2022**; D-1's written submissions in I.A. 39500/2024; the reply before the Arbitrator; and the SC judgment of 13.08.2025 directing rights to be worked out per the 22.03.2024 order. From 22.03.2024 alone, limitation runs to 22.03.2027 — suit of 17.06.2026 is well within time. The application suppresses every one of these.
3. **Continuing cause of action:** each fresh alienation (R-2 Green Park sold 16.01.2026 — *after* the undertaking), each contradictory stand (Transfer Petition (Crl.) 52/2026 claiming PPJ Delhi exclusively), each fresh obstruction is a fresh cause of action.
4. **"No coparcenary" is a red herring.** Partition is not confined to HSA coparcenaries; a family settlement creating defined shares (18/23/23/18/18) *is* a partition arrangement, valid without form or registration — *Kale*, (1976) 3 SCC 119, as applied by **this very Bench** in the 22.03.2024 order (para 23) at the *Defendants' own* instance. And per *Bhupendra Kumar Sood v. GNCTD*, W.P.(C) 14448/2025 (Sachin Datta, J., 01.07.2026), a document recording a prior settlement is not itself the partition — the division stood effectuated earlier; what remains is **implementation and delivery of assets**, which also answers the limitation theory.
5. **The group's own acknowledgments bind D-5 too.** Being of the Kamal Gupta group, D-5 cannot distance himself from the group's acknowledgments — the 2015 Memorandum and 2019 MOU (which allot D-5 his own 18% share and under which he has taken benefit), the 22.03.2024 undertaking and the arbitral-proceedings admissions. A party who has enjoyed benefits under the family arrangement is estopped from unsettling it (*Kale*) — the estoppel runs against D-5 as much as D-1.
6. **Cause of action plainly disclosed** on the *Liverpool & London S.P.* test — the 2014 settlement, its records, the 22.03.2024 undertaking, the SC direction, non-implementation and continuing alienation.
7. **Their limitation theory is destroyed by Pawan Gupta's own conduct.** D-7 himself filed ARB.P. 1010/2022 in **2022** to enforce the *same* 30.09.2014 oral family settlement — eight years after 2014, with no limitation qualms — pleading (petition, paras 20–22, 26) that the settlement was *"arrived at between the family members on 30.09.2014, which has been recorded in the family settlement deed dated 20.06.2015"*, was **acted upon from the very next day (01.10.2014)** through his taking over the Karol Bagh showroom, and that the ~Rs. 500 Cr stock (para 28: the three-entity Stock Summary of 30.09.2014) *"till date… [has] not been distributed"* — i.e., non-implementation as a live, subsisting grievance in 2022. If the 2014 arrangement was alive and enforceable for Pawan Gupta in 2022, it cannot be dead for Rahul Gupta in 2026 — particularly with the intervening acknowledgments of 22.03.2024 and 13.08.2025.

### D. D-7 & D-8 (Pawan & Renu Gupta) — application under O.VII R.11 r/w S.151 CPC dated 21.06.2026

**Their case:** the suit is "specific performance of a family arrangement camouflaged as a partition suit" (S.16(c) SRA readiness/willingness not pleaded; ad valorem fee; Art. 54 limitation); gross undervaluation (Rs. 146.75 Cr+ claim valued at Rs. 2,00,02,200); limitation (2015 ouster / 2019 MOU / 2022 knowledge); plaint founded on a "false" para-18 undertaking; and res judicata/abuse — the 12.11.2024 order was set aside by the SC on 13.08.2025 with Rs. 3 lakh costs.

**Our points:**

1. **The conduct point comes first — and it is devastating.** On 19.06.2026 D-7's counsel told the Court that copies of the plaint and applications had *not been supplied*. Yet on **21.06.2026** — two days later — D-7 & D-8 filed this very O.VII R.11 application **quoting the plaint's paras, prayers, List of Dates, Statement of Truth and valuation table verbatim**, and on 22.06.2026 a S.340 application doing the same. They plainly *had* the plaint (served via WhatsApp/e-mail). The non-service plea stands falsified by their own filings — and still **no reply to I.A. 16084**. Meanwhile they filed and withdrew SLP(C) 22163/2026.
2. **"Specific performance, not partition" — wrong in law.** A family arrangement is not an ordinary executory contract requiring specific performance; per *Kale* (as applied by this Bench on 22.03.2024) it *settles and crystallises* pre-existing rights. Per *Bhupendra Kumar Sood* (this Bench, 01.07.2026) and *Shub Karan Bubna*, (2009) 9 SCC 689, what remains after a concluded arrangement is **implementation and delivery** of already-vested assets — the classic office of a partition decree (O.XX R.18), not S.16(c) SRA readiness/willingness. Their own para 3 admits the 2014 settlement and its recording in the 2015/2019 FSDs — an acknowledgment, not a defence.
3. **"Non-signatory/stranger" cuts against them.** If the Plaintiff is a non-signatory whose rights the FSDs could not affect (their own 22.03.2024 order, para 18: the groups' intent was to settle inter se disputes *"while at the same time not affecting the rights and interest of Mr. Rahul Gupta"*), then his antecedent 2014 allocation survives untouched — supporting the suit, not barring it. **And Pawan Gupta is himself not a signatory of the 2015 document.** Kamal Gupta's own reply dated 09.10.2022 in ARB.P. 1010/2022 (preliminary objection 6) pleads: *"The 2015 unsigned Deed is a draft document, which is not signed by all the purported parties thereof. It may be noted that **petitioner No.1 [Mr. Pawan Gupta]**, Mr. Rahul Gupta and Mr. Mohit Gupta (Stakeholder and Family Member) have not signed said deed."* Yet Pawan Gupta founded and prosecuted ARB.P. 1010/2022 on that very 2015 FSD, and obtained the 22.03.2024 reference. On his own record, non-signature of the 2015 document neither destroyed his rights under the 2014 arrangement nor barred his remedy — the same must hold for the Plaintiff. The "non-signatory stranger" objection collapses on the applicants' own test.
4. **The "false undertaking" ground is an interpretation quarrel, not an O.VII R.11(d) bar.** Paras 17 and 18 of the 22.03.2024 order must be read together: para 17 records the parties' senior counsel's *"categorical statement"* (a statement to the Court — binding as an undertaking in the classic sense) and para 18 records the intent not to affect Rahul Gupta's rights and interest. Whether that is pleaded as arising from para 17, para 18 or both is a question of construction — not a plaint-rejection ground. In any event O.VII R.11 does not permit a mini-trial on the correctness of the plaint's characterisation of documents (*Dahiben*). *(Counsel: verify the plaint's exact citation of para 17 vs para 18 and, if a cross-reference slip exists, cure it by amendment/clarification before they make capital of it.)*
5. **Res judicata — misconceived.** The SC's 13.08.2025 judgment set aside the 12.11.2024 order because post-disposal applications in a S.11 proceeding were *procedurally* misconceived — and expressly left the parties *"free to work out their rights in accordance with the order dated 22.03.2024."* There has been **no adjudication on the merits of the 23% share by any court in any suit** — a S.11 intervention refusal decides no title (S.11(6A) confines the court to the arbitration agreement's existence). Res judicata requires a former *suit* heard and finally decided on merits (S.11 CPC explanation apart); there is none. Far from barring the suit, the SC's relegation is precisely *why* the suit was filed — it is the remedy in accordance with law that even their own application (para 28) concedes must remain open to the Plaintiff.
6. **Court fee and limitation:** same answers as Parts A and C above — Para 66 constructive-possession plea (fixed fee; *Satheedevi*), court fee of Rs. 2,00,000/- already paid on 22.06.2026 (receipt filed), no limitation for partition, S.18 acknowledgments running to 22.03.2027, continuing alienations.

### E. D-7 & D-8's application under S.340 CrPC / S.379 & 215 BNSS dated 22.06.2026 (perjury)

**Their case:** the plaint attributes to para 18 of the 22.03.2024 order an "undertaking" (*"None of the properties or shares belonging to Rahul Gupta would be dealt with or divided…"*) which appears (if at all) in para 17 and is confined to *intervenor companies' properties* *in the arbitration*; hence perjury under Ss. 227-229/235-237 BNS.

**The knockout answer — Pawan Gupta's own pleaded reading of paras 17–18 (Reply to recall I.A. 39500/2024):** In his reply opposing the recall application in ARB.P. 1010/2022, D-7 himself pleaded, **on affidavit**:

> **Para 76:** *"… the assurance was adhered to, ensuring that the properties of **Mr. Rahul Gupta** and the intervenor companies would not be dealt with in the arbitration."*
> **Para 77:** *"The assurances provided in **paras 17 and 18** protect the interests of **non-parties**…"*

So Pawan Gupta's own sworn pleading (i) extends the para 17/18 assurance to *"the properties of Mr. Rahul Gupta"* — not merely intervenor-company properties — and (ii) treats paras 17 **and 18 together** as protective of non-parties like the Plaintiff. That is substantially the *very reading* his S.340 application now brands a "deliberate and dishonest" fabrication. A construction which the applicant himself swore to cannot be a perjurious falsehood when pleaded by the Plaintiff — the S.340 application is self-destroyed by the applicant's own prior affidavit, and its filing despite that record is itself vexatious (feeding the S.35A costs ask).

**Our points:**

1. **S.340 is not a weapon for interpretation disputes.** Prosecution requires a *deliberate, conscious falsehood of fact*, established prima facie to a near-certain standard, and an inquiry must be *expedient in the interests of justice* — not every inaccuracy or contested reading qualifies: *Chajoo Ram v. Radhey Shyam*, (1971) 1 SCC 774; *K.T.M.S. Mohd. v. UOI*, (1992) 3 SCC 178; *Iqbal Singh Marwah*, (2005) 4 SCC 370; *Amarsang Nathaji*, (2017) 1 SCC 113. The plaint's reading of paras 17+18 — a categorical statement by senior counsel not to deal with/divide the properties, coupled with the recorded intent not to affect Rahul Gupta's rights — is at minimum a *tenable construction* of a judicial order, openly annexed to the plaint itself. Nothing is concealed: the Court's own order is on the record for the Court to read. There can be no perjury in inviting the Court to construe its own order.
2. **A mis-citation of paragraph number is not a false statement of fact.** The substance pleaded — that a protective statement was made and recorded on 22.03.2024, and that the SC on 13.08.2025 left parties to work out rights *in accordance with that order* — is true on the face of the record.
3. **VERIFIED VULNERABILITY — must be cured before Monday.** I have checked the plaint (file 1 in the case folder) against the 22.03.2024 order. The List of Dates entry for 22.03.2024 states: *"the judgment recorded undertakings by all parties in Para 18: 'None of the properties or shares belonging to Rahul Gupta would be dealt with or divided by the said parties in the arbitration.'"* That sentence, **presented within quotation marks, does not appear verbatim in para 17 or para 18**. Para 17 records: *"none of the properties **owned by the intervenor companies**, though mentioned in annexures of the FSD, will be dealt/divided by the said parties in the arbitration"*; para 18 records the intent *"not affecting the rights and interest of Mr. Rahul Gupta."* The plaint has thus (i) cited para 18 where the statement is in para 17, and (ii) rendered as a quotation what is in truth a characterisation combining paras 17 and 18. **Recommended cure:** a short clarificatory affidavit / amendment (or a corrigendum in the replies) *before* the S.340 application is argued — candidly correcting the citation, setting out paras 17 and 18 verbatim, and explaining that the pleaded sentence was a bona fide characterisation of their combined effect (intervenor-company properties + the recorded intent qua Rahul Gupta's rights and interest). A prompt, voluntary correction destroys the "deliberate and conscious falsehood" element and the "expediency" requirement of S.340 (*Chajoo Ram*; *Amarsang Nathaji*) — whereas leaving it to be exposed in court hands them their best point.
4. **Their own glass house.** The same applicants told this Court on 19.06.2026 they were *unserved*, then filed merits applications quoting the plaint within 48 hours; their S.340 application itself is verified as "nothing material concealed" while suppressing the affidavit of service and the WhatsApp/e-mail delivery. If S.340 standards are to be applied, they must be applied even-handedly — the Plaintiff's own S.340/perjury material (including I.A. 183603/2026 filed in their SLP before its withdrawal) is on record.
5. **Purpose and timing:** filed 22.06.2026 — the day of the Vacation Bench hearing — as leverage against the interim application. A S.340 application decides nothing about interim protection and cannot defer I.A. 16084.
6. **Multiple parallel attacks — pendency itself being used as prejudice.** Pawan Gupta's O.VII R.11 application and his S.340 CrPC/S.379 BNSS application both remain pending (not cleared); he simultaneously filed and prosecuted SLP(C) 22163/2026 as well — three parallel attacks arising from the same plaint/order, maintained not for adjudication but so that their very pendency prejudices and defers I.A. 16084. The Court should decline to let pending threshold/counter-attack applications operate as de facto stay of interim protection.
7. **Even-handedness — the Plaintiff invoked S.379 BNSS first, before the Hon'ble Supreme Court itself.** While Pawan Gupta was pressing his SLP, the Plaintiff filed his own S.379 BNSS/perjury application (I.A. 183603/2026) in that very SLP — squarely raising the falsity of the non-service plea before the Hon'ble Supreme Court — and the SLP was thereafter withdrawn on 13.07.2026. The perjury issue therefore cuts against Pawan Gupta, not the Plaintiff; his S.340/379 counter-attack is a mirror-image retaliation filed after being confronted with his own falsehood.

### G-1. Issue-wise comparison — what the judgment dated 22.03.2024 (ARB.P. 1010/2022) has ALREADY dealt with

Every substantive issue Kamal Gupta (I.A. 16112) and Mukesh Gupta (I.A. 16113/16114) now raise was already considered — and answered against them — in the judgment dated 22.03.2024, in proceedings to which they (or their group) were parties:

1. **"Commercial dispute" (Mukesh, 16113)** — already dealt with. The judgment (para 23) expressly distinguishes the two categories: *"unlike commercial contracts which focus on business interests, family settlements aim to preserve peace and goodwill within the family."* The Court characterised this very family's disputes as family-settlement disputes, not commercial ones. Mukesh's plea that the same disputes are a "commercial dispute" under S.2(1)(c)(vii) runs contrary to the character already judicially attributed to them.

2. **Technical objections (court fee/stamp/valuation — Kamal, 16112)** — already dealt with. When the identical species of objection (insufficient stamping of the FSD) was raised in ARB.P. 1010/2022, the group itself opposed it — the judgment (paras 12–13) records their contention that family settlements recording an oral partition require no mandatory stamping — and the objection was rejected (para 32). Para 23 (applying *Kale*) holds that courts must uphold family arrangements "instead of disturbing the same on technical or trivial grounds." The court-fee attack is the same species of technical objection, now deployed from the opposite side of their own mouth.

3. **Existence and enforceability of the 2014/2015/2019 family arrangement (Mukesh's limitation/no-cause plea, 16114)** — already dealt with. The judgment proceeded on those very documents, applied *Kale* and *K.K. Modi* (paras 23–24: a family settlement "should not be lightly interfered with… especially when it has been substantially acted upon"), and referred the disputes to arbitration at Pawan Gupta's instance — a petition itself founded on enforcing the 2014 arrangement/2015 FSD eight years on. The group cannot now contend the same arrangement is stale, unenforceable, or gives no cause of action.

4. **Non-signatory objection (Pawan/Renu O.VII R.11; echoed by the group)** — already dealt with. Kamal Gupta's own reply of 09.10.2022 (prelim. obj. 6) pleaded that Pawan Gupta, Rahul Gupta and Mohit Gupta had not signed the 2015 deed — yet the reference was granted on Pawan Gupta's petition founded on that very deed. Non-signature was thus no bar when the group wanted the settlement enforced; it cannot be a bar to Rahul Gupta's suit.

5. **Rahul Gupta's 23% / protection of his interest** — already dealt with. Paras 17–18 of the judgment record the statements/undertakings that the properties of the intervenor companies would not be dealt with in the arbitration and that the settlement documents were "not affecting the rights and interest of Mr. Rahul Gupta"; the recall application (I.A. 39500/2024) was disposed of permitting him to sit as observer, on the footing that his rights were preserved outside the arbitration. His crystallised interest was therefore recognised, not negated — the present suit merely works it out.

6. **Net effect:** each threshold plea invites this Court to re-open questions already traversed in the 22.03.2024 judgment inter partes — squarely barred by approbate-reprobate/estoppel, and precisely the "technical or trivial grounds" *Kale* forbids.

### H. Common sequencing point — their applications cannot stall our injunction

1. *R.K. Roja v. U.S. Rayudu*, (2016) 14 SCC 275 requires O.VII R.11 to be decided before **trial** — not before interim protection. O.XXXIX jurisdiction is independent and preservative — *Wander Ltd. v. Antox*, 1990 Supp SCC 727; where dissipation is imminent, protection must issue at once — *Deoraj*, (2004) 4 SCC 697.
2. Even if a plaint were rejected/returned, a fresh suit lies — but **dissipated assets cannot be recovered**. Preservation is therefore logically prior.
3. At worst: offer that the threshold I.A.s be heard together with or immediately after I.A. 16084 — **with status quo operating in the meanwhile.**
