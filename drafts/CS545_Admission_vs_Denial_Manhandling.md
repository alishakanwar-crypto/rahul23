### THEIR OWN ADMISSION THEN vs THEIR STAND NOW — THE MANHANDLING/06.11.2015 INCIDENT

| | **THEIR ADMISSION IN 2019** — Reply of R-1 to R-4 in CP 206/241-242/ND/2018 (NCLT), sworn by Mohit Gupta, adopted & affirmed by Veena Gupta (R-2) and Mukesh Gupta (R-3) | **THEIR STAND NOW (31.07.2026)** — D1–2's Reply to I.A. 16084/2026, para-wise reply para 15 |
|---|---|---|
| The incident | "It is however **a matter of record that a scuffle did take place between family members** when the petitioner's husband (Rahul Gupta) attempted to snatch away the keys of one of the vaults at PP Jewellers Pvt. Ltd." | "**It is denied that Plaintiff was violently manhandled, and physically assaulted** by his own family members, as substantiated by the MLC dated 06.11.2015" — recast as "an altercation wherein the Plaintiff tried to unlawfully and aggressively demanded further stocks / jewellery". |
| The MLC | "**The MLC placed on record clearly reflects** that a minor scuffle took place and **some scratches were present on physical self of the petitioner's husband**." — they themselves treated the MLC as genuine and probative. | The MLC's substantiation of the assault is denied; the incident is portrayed as the Plaintiff's own aggression. |
| The November 2015 exit | "Frustrated at the failed attempt to take control of the jewelry stock, Rahul and his wife (petitioner herein) **left the family home in the month of November 2015**." | "It is a matter of record that plaintiff left the family house along with wife and 2 minor children in November 2015 after an altercation..." — and the same November 2015 exit is INVOKED as the start of limitation (Reply, Section F, paras 53–54). |
| Rahul's role in the business | Until November 2015 "all statutory compliances... was being looked after... by the petitioner's husband", who maintained "all books of accounts" and handled "banking operations". | The Plaintiff is portrayed as having no allocated stock, no share and no role — while the same para admits the businesses of PPJ (Delhi) and PPJ (Exports) "were under the Plaintiff's operational control and... the keys to the safes... were in his possession". |

**The submission:** the answering Defendants' family group has, on sworn affidavit before the NCLT in 2019, ADMITTED (i) the physical scuffle at the PP Jewellers vault, (ii) the MLC recording injuries on the Plaintiff's person, and (iii) the November 2015 departure of the Plaintiff and his family from the family home. Veena Gupta — Defendant No. 2 HERE — is one of the respondents who adopted and affirmed that reply. Defendant Nos. 1–2 now flatly deny the manhandling in their Reply while simultaneously using the very same November 2015 incident as the springboard for their limitation defence. A party cannot admit the incident when defending an oppression petition, deny it when opposing an injunction, and rely on it when pleading limitation — all on oath. This is a textbook case of approbation and reprobation, and at minimum shows that the incident, the MLC and the 2015 exclusion are seriously triable facts that cannot be brushed aside at the interim stage.
