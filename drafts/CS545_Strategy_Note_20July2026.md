## II. Justice Sachin Datta and *Kale* — the two judgments to cite

### 1. His own judgment of 22.03.2024 in ARB.P. 1010/2022 (Pawan Gupta v. Kamal Gupta)
Justice Sachin Datta himself, in this very family's matter, relied on ***Kale v. Dy. Director of Consolidation*, (1976) 3 SCC 119** (quoting para 9) and held (para 23):

> "…unlike commercial contracts which focus on business interests, family settlements aim to preserve peace and goodwill within the family. This social objective is recognized by courts, and it has been held in umpteen judgments that **it is the duty of the courts to uphold and give full effect to such an arrangement instead of disturbing the same on technical or trivial grounds**."

This is the best possible foundation: the Defendants themselves invoked *Kale* before this very Judge to enforce the 2015/2019 FSDs; the Judge accepted the principle. They cannot now resile from the *Kale* framework when the Plaintiff invokes the anterior Oral Family Partition of 30.09.2014 recorded in those same documents. Para 18 of the same judgment records the Defendants' undertaking acknowledging Rahul Gupta's allocation (the plaint's Prayer A is expressly built on it).

### 2. *Bhupendra Kumar Sood & Anr. v. GNCTD*, W.P.(C) 14448/2025, decided 01.07.2026 — **Sachin Datta, J.** (coram verified)
This is the judgment squarely making the "**this is not a partition — it is only working out / delivery of already-vested assets**" point. Holding (paras 27, 34–41):

- The sine qua non of a partition is division of jointly-held property; an instrument is a partition only if it is *"the operative source of such division, and not merely a record of a pre-existing arrangement"* (para 27).
- *"the partition of the property, insofar as division of ownership is concerned, **stood effectuated as early as in the year 2000**"* [the date of the Memorandum of Family Settlement] (para 34).
- The later (2023) document *"does not effect any division of jointly held property. It merely records a re-adjustment… between parties who were already absolute owners of their respective shares"* (para 35); *"Arrangements which define or adjust pre-existing rights without effecting a fresh division of joint property"* are not instruments of partition (para 37).
- He relied on ***Shub Karan Bubna v. Sita Saran Bubna*, (2009) 9 SCC 689** — partition is only *"a redistribution or adjustment of pre-existing rights… and delivery thereof to the respective allottees"* — and *Jinesh Jain v. Amit Jain*, AIR 2025 (Del) 136.

**How to use it on Monday:** Rahul Gupta's title to 23% vested on 30.09.2014 (oral partition); the 2015 Memorandum / 2019 MOU merely *record* it (and per *Kale*, a memorandum of a completed oral arrangement needs no registration/stamp); the suit therefore seeks not the creation of rights but the **implementation and delivery** of assets already allocated — precisely the framework this Judge applied on 01.07.2026. This also defuses the O.VII R.11 attack (limitation/valuation): a suit to work out an antecedent title with continuing exclusion is different from a suit to establish new rights.

*(Comparative support, different coram: Purushaindra Kumar Kaurav, J. in CS(OS) 348/2025 discusses Kale; use only as secondary authority.)*

## III. How to get interim on Monday — graduated ask on IA 16084/2026

Ask in descending steps, so the Court can grant the lowest step without hesitation:

**Step 1 — Status quo (the floor; already foreshadowed on 22.06.2026).** On 22.06.2026 plaintiff's counsel told the Vacation Judge that *"at this stage, the Plaintiff is only seeking an order directing the parties to maintain status quo with respect to the properties mentioned in Schedule-B of the plaint."* Renew exactly this: **status quo as to title, possession and encumbrance of all Schedule-B properties** and a restraint on further alienation, pledge, or third-party rights. This is unanswerable: Schedule-C (IA 16111/2026) shows ~Rs. 238.50 cr already sold/alienated during the family litigation; Section 52 TPA is a backstop, not a substitute for an injunction (*lis pendens* does not prevent sale, only subordinates it).

**Step 2 — Stock preservation (the chart's core: PPJ(D) & PPJ(E) jewellery stock, ~Rs. 500 cr per the 05.07.2022 order in O.M.P.(I)(COMM) 186/2022).** Restraint on removal, melting, hypothecation or disposal of the stock of PP Jewellers (Delhi) & PP Jewellers (Exports) otherwise than in the ordinary course of retail sale with accounts; plus **disclosure on affidavit** of the stock summaries/registers (2014-15 to date) and current inventory. If the Court hesitates on sealing, the disclosure + restraint combination still fixes the corpus.

**Step 3 — Local Commissioner / inventory.** Appointment of an LC to inventory and videograph the stock at the showrooms/vaults, sealed report to the Court — the classic preservation order under O.XXXIX R.7 / O.XXVI CPC; it decides nothing final.

**Step 4 — Accounts/receiver-lite.** Defendants to file audited statements of the PPJ Corpus entities and not to create new entities/transfer business except in ordinary course. Keep the full rendition-of-accounts and mesne-profits claims for trial.

**What NOT to press on Monday:** actual delivery of the 23% stock, transfer of the Mumbai (Cat Eye Creation) property, or substitute NSEZ/showroom properties — these are final reliefs (or interim-mandatory, requiring the highest threshold). Mention them as the destination, seek only preservation now, and expressly reserve them.

**IA 16089/2026 (O.XII R.6):** do not press for a preliminary decree on Monday; instead use the admissions (para 18 undertaking of 22.03.2024; the Defendants' own FSD pleadings; SC order 13.08.2025 in SLP(C) 4775-4779/2025 preserving RG's rights to be worked out) as the *prima facie* case for the injunction. A judgment-on-admissions hearing can be sought on the next date once pleadings are complete.
