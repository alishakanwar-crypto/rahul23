### COUNTER TO D1–2's PARA-WISE REPLY, PARA 10 — KAMAL GUPTA'S OWN SECTION 17 REPLY ADMITS THE VAULT/STOCK OBLIGATION TO RAHUL GUPTA

D1–2's Reply (para-wise reply, para 10) denies that it was agreed under the Oral Partition "or otherwise" that 23% of the family jewellery stocks would be "transferred and handed over" in the name of the Plaintiff, and instead alleges that the Plaintiff and Defendant No. 7 "usurped the stocks".

This stands squarely contradicted by **Defendant No. 1, Mr. Kamal Gupta's own pleading** in the Reply filed by the Kamal Gupta Group to I.A. Nos. 1 & 2 of 2024 (applications under Section 17 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996) before the Ld. Sole Arbitrator, Hon'ble Mr. Justice (Retd.) Sanjay Kishan Kaul (arising out of the reference by order dated 22.03.2024 in ARB.P. 1010/2022). The said Reply is already on the record of the present suit as an annexure (Document A-25 / Annexure P-3 to Cont.Cas.(C) 1044/2026), and para 13 thereof pleads, verbatim:

> "...after the aforesaid distribution the FSD 2015 recorded the fact of **only the pending portion of stock distribution between the Petitioner No.1 [Mr. Pawan Gupta] and Rahul Gupta. That Clause 8 of the FSD 2015 recorded the obligation of the petitioner no.1 to handing over of the excess stock in his possession to Rahul Gupta from any one vault of the choice of Rahul Gupta.**"

Clause 8 of the FSD 2015 itself reads: "Stock shall be handed over to the Third Party [Rahul Gupta] by the Second Party [Pawan Gupta] from any one vault of the choice of the Third Party."

**The point in rejoinder:**
1. Mr. Kamal Gupta has himself pleaded, on the record of the family arbitration, that the family settlement documents RECORDED a subsisting, **pending** obligation to hand over stock to Rahul Gupta — from "any one vault of the choice of Rahul Gupta". A "pending portion of stock distribution between... Pawan Gupta and Rahul Gupta" necessarily admits that Rahul Gupta had a recognised share in the family stocks which remained to be delivered to him.
2. Having so pleaded before the Arbitral Tribunal to support his own case there (that the stocks otherwise "stood distributed"), Mr. Kamal Gupta cannot now, in reply para 10, deny that any stock was ever agreed to be handed over to the Plaintiff. He seeks to retract before this Hon'ble Court the very admission he made before the Tribunal — a further, and stark, instance of approbation and reprobation.
3. The admission also demolishes the "usurpation" theory: on Defendant No. 1's own showing, stock was OWED to Rahul Gupta under the settlement documents and was never handed over — which is exactly the Plaintiff's case of a crystallised share denied in implementation.
