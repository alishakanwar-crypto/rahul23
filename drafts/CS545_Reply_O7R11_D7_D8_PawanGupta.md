## REPLY ON BEHALF OF THE PLAINTIFF TO THE APPLICATION UNDER ORDER VII RULE 11 READ WITH SECTION 151 CPC FILED BY DEFENDANT NOS. 7 AND 8 (MR. PAWAN GUPTA AND MRS. RENU GUPTA) SEEKING REJECTION OF THE PLAINT

## INTRODUCTORY SUBMISSIONS

1. The present Reply is filed on behalf of the Plaintiff to the application dated 21.06.2026 filed by Defendant Nos. 7 and 8 under Order VII Rule 11 read with Section 151 CPC seeking rejection of the plaint. The application is misconceived, proceeds on a selective, distorted and suppressive reading of the plaint, mischaracterises the nature of the suit, misstates the judicial record, and is, in truth, only the latest step in the Applicants' sustained campaign to stall, obstruct and derail the adjudication of the Plaintiff's rights. It deserves dismissal with exemplary costs.

2. At the very threshold, the conduct of the Applicants disentitles them to any indulgence. The Applicants (Mr. Pawan Gupta and Mrs. Renu Gupta) have simultaneously approached the Hon'ble Supreme Court by way of Special Leave to Appeal (C) No. 22163/2026 against the simple order dated 19.06.2026 passed by this Hon'ble Court in the present suit — an order by which this Hon'ble Court did nothing more than issue notice on the Plaintiff's applications. In the said SLP, the Applicants have pleaded on oath that copies of the plaint and accompanying applications had not been supplied to them and that they were denied any opportunity to respond — a plea demonstrably falsified by the Affidavit of Service on the record of this Hon'ble Court (service effected on 18.06.2026 by e-mail on pawan@ppj.co.in and accountsdept@ppj.co.in and by WhatsApp, and again on 20.06.2026 pursuant to this Hon'ble Court's directions) and by the Applicants' own conduct in filing, on 21.06.2026, the present detailed application analysing the plaint paragraph by paragraph, prayer by prayer and valuation by valuation. The Plaintiff has accordingly filed an application under Section 340 read with Section 195 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 (corresponding to Sections 379 and 215 of the BNSS, 2023) dated 23.06.2026 in the said SLP(C) No. 22163/2026 seeking initiation of perjury proceedings against the Applicants for deliberately making false statements and suppressing material facts before the Hon'ble Supreme Court. A litigant who tells the Hon'ble Supreme Court on oath that he has not even received the plaint, while simultaneously filing before this Hon'ble Court a granular Order VII Rule 11 application founded on a minute analysis of that very plaint, approbates and reprobates and does not come to Court with clean hands. Such a litigant cannot invoke the discretionary and drastic jurisdiction of Order VII Rule 11 CPC.

3. The application, moreover, is not one filed to assist the Court but to stall the Plaintiff's urgent interim application (I.A. No. 16084/2026 under Order XXXIX Rules 1 and 2 CPC) seeking protection of the Plaintiff's 23% share in the PP Jewellers Family Corpus. The present SLP is the third proceeding initiated by Defendant No. 7 before the Hon'ble Supreme Court in relation to the disputes concerning the PPJ Family Corpus — the first being SLP(C) No. 18873/2024 (withdrawn on 20.12.2024), and the second being SLP(C) D. No. 9078/2025 (decided by the judgment dated 13.08.2025) — a pattern of litigation directed at delaying and frustrating the adjudication of the Plaintiff's claims.

## PRELIMINARY OBJECTIONS

4. **The true nature of the suit: partition and working out of an antecedent, crystallised share — not specific performance.** The entire application is built on a single mischaracterisation: that the suit is a disguised suit for specific performance of the family arrangement. It is not. The suit is founded primarily on the oral family partition dated 30.09.2014, under which the Plaintiff's 23% share in the PPJ Family Corpus stood recognised and crystallised — the Memorandum dated 20.06.2015 and the document of 09.07.2019 being merely records/memoranda of the antecedent arrangement. It is settled since *Kale v. Deputy Director of Consolidation*, (1976) 3 SCC 119, that a family settlement is not a transfer or a contract creating rights de novo; it works on the footing of antecedent title, and each member takes the properties in recognition of pre-existing rights. A suit to work out, by metes and bounds, a share already recognised in a family settlement is a suit for partition, maintainable with a preliminary decree under Order XX Rule 18 CPC; it is not a suit for specific performance of a contract attracting Section 16(c) of the Specific Relief Act, 1963 or Article 54 of the Limitation Act, 1963. The Applicants' entire edifice of "camouflage" collapses with this settled position.

5. **The plaint pleads joint and constructive possession — suppressed by the Applicants.** The application (paragraph 11) dismisses paragraph 66 of the plaint as "a bare legal conclusion". Paragraph 66 expressly pleads that the Plaintiff continues to remain a co-owner and co-sharer in the entire PPJ Family Corpus, is deemed to be in joint and constructive possession thereof, and has at all material times remained in possession, control and management of certain assets and businesses forming part of his allocated share. In law, the possession of one co-sharer is the possession of all; exclusion from particular premises is not ouster from co-ownership. Whether the Plaintiff stands "ousted" in the legal sense — hostile animus, exclusive possession to his knowledge, exclusion from co-ownership rights — is a question of fact par excellence, incapable of resolution on demurrer at the Order VII Rule 11 stage.

6. **The Applicants' own prior pleadings contradict the ouster premise.** The Pawan Gupta Group has itself pleaded, in its Reply/Objections in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022, that the Plaintiff had "taken control of the assets of the Joint Family Group", was "in control of multiple businesses mentioned in Annexure A to the 2019 MOU", and "admittedly controls" companies of the group *(counsel to verify paragraph numbers against the certified copy)*. Having pleaded on oath that the Plaintiff possesses and controls substantial assets of the corpus, the Applicants cannot now assert his complete ouster for the collateral purpose of a court-fee and limitation objection. This is approbation and reprobation.

7. **The suit is the very "working out of rights" directed by the Hon'ble Supreme Court.** By the judgment dated 13.08.2025 in SLP(C) No. 4775/2025 and connected matters (2025 INSC 975), the Hon'ble Supreme Court, while setting aside the order dated 12.11.2024 on the ground that applications could not have been entertained in a disposed-of Section 11 petition, expressly held (paragraph 25): "The parties to the present proceedings are free to work out their rights in accordance with the order dated 22.03.2024." The Plaintiff, admittedly a non-signatory to the FSDs and expressly held (order dated 22.03.2024) not to be a necessary party to and not bound by the arbitration, can work out his rights only by an independent civil suit. The present suit is precisely that working out. Far from being barred, it is the remedy the judicial record itself contemplates.

## GROUND-WISE REPLY

### RE: "TRUE NATURE OF THE SUIT" (PARAGRAPHS 3 TO 8 OF THE APPLICATION)

8. **Reply to paragraphs 3 and 4:** Denied. The Plaintiff's claim does not "rest entirely" on the FSDs; it rests primarily on the oral family partition dated 30.09.2014, of which the 1st FSD dated 20.06.2015 and the document dated 09.07.2019 are records. That the Plaintiff is not a signatory to the FSDs is precisely why his rights were expressly insulated in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022 (order dated 22.03.2024, paragraphs 17 and 18) and why an independent suit is his only remedy. A share under a family settlement is a share by recognition of antecedent title (*Kale*, supra) — not "at best an alleged contractual allocation".

9. **Reply to paragraphs 5 to 7:** Denied. The prayers, read with the plaint as a whole, seek partition and working out of the Plaintiff's 23% share by a preliminary decree under Order XX Rule 18 CPC (Prayer A); the reliefs of possession/delivery are consequential to partition, the prayer itself reciting that title always resided in the Plaintiff and that no fresh acquisition is sought. A partition suit by a co-owner in joint/constructive possession does not attract Section 16(c) of the Specific Relief Act, ad valorem court fee on the corpus, or Article 54 of the Limitation Act. The Applicants' attempt to re-label the suit is answered by the very rule they invoke: the substance of the plaint — antecedent title, co-ownership, crystallised share, working out by metes and bounds — is partition.

10. **Reply to paragraph 8:** The proposition that clever drafting cannot create an illusion of a cause of action is not disputed; its application is. *T. Arivandandam v. T.V. Satyapal*, (1977) 4 SCC 467, *Dahiben v. Arvindbhai Kalyanji Bhanusali*, (2020) 7 SCC 366 and the connected line of authority equally mandate that the plaint be read meaningfully and **as a whole**, taking its averments as true, and that a defendant's defence cannot be looked into at this stage. Tested on that standard, the plaint discloses a clear cause of action for partition; it is the application, not the plaint, which is an exercise in clever re-drafting of the Plaintiff's case.

### RE: GROUND I — VALUATION AND COURT FEE [ORDER VII RULE 11(b) AND (c)]

11. **Reply to paragraphs 9 to 12:** Denied. The suit is properly valued and the court fee paid is in accordance with law. The plaint (paragraph 64) sets out a relief-wise valuation table; the principal relief of partition by a co-owner in joint and constructive possession (paragraph 66) carries fixed court fee under Article 17(vi) of Schedule II of the Court Fees Act, 1870, as is settled for partition suits by co-owners in joint/constructive possession. *Neelavathi v. N. Natarajan*, (1980) 2 SCC 247 in fact assists the Plaintiff: it holds that the question of court fee must be considered on the plaint averments alone, that possession of one co-owner is possession of all, and that a mere plea of non-participation in profits or exclusion from particular premises does not amount to ouster displacing constructive possession. *Suhrid Singh v. Randhir Singh*, (2010) 12 SCC 112 concerned a non-executant seeking cancellation of deeds and recovery of possession — it has no application to a partition suit on antecedent title. Without prejudice, even if this Hon'ble Court were to take a different view on any head of valuation, the law under clauses (b) and (c) of Order VII Rule 11 is that rejection is not the first step: the Court must first require correction of the valuation or supply of the requisite stamp paper within a time fixed by the Court, and rejection can follow only upon default. The plaint (paragraph 65) contains the Plaintiff's express undertaking to make good any deficiency as directed. The prayer for outright rejection is contrary to the statutory scheme itself.

### RE: GROUND II — LIMITATION [ORDER VII RULE 11(d)]

12. **Reply to paragraphs 13 and 14:** Denied. Limitation is a mixed question of law and fact and can found rejection under Order VII Rule 11(d) only where the plaint on its face and without more shows the suit to be hopelessly barred. It does not. The suit being one for partition by a co-owner in joint and constructive possession, the right to sue for partition is a continuing right; Articles 54, 58 and 59 of the Limitation Act — all premised on the specific-performance/declaration/cancellation re-characterisation — have no application. Further, the plaint pleads continuing acknowledgements, part-implementation and subsequent events (including the proceedings of 2022–2025 in which the Defendant groups themselves repeatedly assured judicial fora that the Plaintiff's rights and interests would not be affected), each of which furnishes a fresh and continuing cause of action. These are matters of evidence, not demurrer.

### RE: GROUND III — ALLEGED FALSE STATEMENT REGARDING THE ORDER DATED 22.03.2024

13. **Reply to paragraphs 15 to 19:** Denied. There is no fabrication or misattribution, and it does not lie in the mouths of these Applicants — of all parties — to so allege. Paragraph 17 of the order dated 22.03.2024 records the categorical statement of learned senior counsel for the petitioners and respondents (which includes the Applicants' group) that none of the properties owned by the intervenor companies would be dealt with/divided in the arbitration; and paragraph 18 records this Hon'ble Court's finding that "the intent of Pawan Gupta Group and Kamal Gupta Group when they executed FSD, was to settle their inter se disputes while at the same time not affecting the rights and interest of Mr. Rahul Gupta." Most significantly, the Applicants' own group, in its reply to I.A. No. 39500/2024 in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022, expressly pleaded that "the assurances provided in paras 17 and 18 protect the interests of non-parties" and that "the assurances given in paras 17 and 18 of the judgment dated 22.03.2024 have been fully adhered to." Having themselves characterised paragraphs 17 and 18 as assurances protecting the Plaintiff's interests and pleaded scrupulous adherence thereto, the Applicants cannot now brand the Plaintiff's reliance on the selfsame paragraphs as "fabrication". At its highest, the point is one of characterisation of judicial observations — an arguable matter of construction, never a ground under Order VII Rule 11, which does not contemplate a mini-trial on the correctness of pleaded quotations. The threat of "perjury proceedings" (paragraph 19) is a pressure tactic; it is, in fact, the Applicants against whom a perjury application under Section 340 CrPC dated 23.06.2026 stands filed by the Plaintiff before the Hon'ble Supreme Court in SLP(C) No. 22163/2026 for their demonstrably false statements regarding non-service of the plaint.

### RE: GROUND IV — RES JUDICATA / ABUSE OF PROCESS [ORDER VII RULE 11(d)]

14. **Reply to paragraphs 20 to 26:** Denied. The plea of res judicata is misconceived on first principles:

    (a) Res judicata under Section 11 CPC requires a former **suit** in which the matter directly and substantially in issue was heard and **finally decided on merits** between the same parties. Neither the intervention application in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022 (a Section 11 proceeding for appointment of an arbitrator), nor the post-disposal applications therein, nor the SLP proceedings, was a suit; and no court has ever adjudicated, on merits, the existence or extent of the Plaintiff's 23% share in the PPJ Family Corpus.

    (b) The order dated 22.03.2024 refused the Plaintiff's intervention in the arbitration on the ground that he was a non-signatory, not a necessary party, and **not bound by any award** — while simultaneously recording the assurances of both groups that his rights and interests and the intervenor companies' properties would not be affected/dealt with in the arbitration. A finding that the Plaintiff has no place *in the arbitration* is the very reason he must sue *in a civil court*; it cannot conceivably bar the civil suit.

    (c) The judgment dated 13.08.2025 set aside the order dated 12.11.2024 on the procedural ground that applications could not be entertained in a disposed-of Section 11 petition; the Hon'ble Supreme Court did not adjudicate the Plaintiff's share, and expressly left the parties "free to work out their rights in accordance with the order dated 22.03.2024." The Plaintiff does not rely on the order dated 12.11.2024; he has instituted the present suit precisely as the working out contemplated. It is a strange doctrine of res judicata under which a party held to have no remedy in one forum is thereby barred from every forum.

    (d) The costs of Rs. 3,00,000/- imposed by the judgment dated 13.08.2025 related to the maintainability of applications in the disposed-of Section 11 proceedings — not to the merits of the Plaintiff's share, and cannot be pressed into service to shut out a first-ever suit on those merits.

15. **Reply to paragraphs 27 to 29:** Denied. It is the application, not the plaint, that is an abuse of process. The plaint, read meaningfully and as a whole, discloses a complete cause of action for partition; it is properly valued; it is within limitation; and it is barred by no law. The Applicants' plea of prejudice from "stalling of the arbitration" is contrived: the Plaintiff is admittedly not a party to the arbitration, and this suit concerns his independent proprietary rights which, on the Applicants' own repeated assurances recorded in judicial orders, the arbitration will not touch.
