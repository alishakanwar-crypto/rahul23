## REPLY ON BEHALF OF THE PLAINTIFF TO I.A. No. 16113/2026, BEING THE APPLICATION UNDER ORDER VII RULE 10 CPC FILED BY DEFENDANT NO. 5 (MR. MUKESH GUPTA) SEEKING RETURN OF THE PLAINT FOR ALLEGED WANT OF PECUNIARY JURISDICTION AND ON THE GROUND THAT THE SUIT RAISES "COMMERCIAL DISPUTES"

MOST RESPECTFULLY SHOWETH:

## INTRODUCTORY SUBMISSIONS

1. The present Reply is filed on behalf of the Plaintiff to the application of Defendant No. 5 under Order VII Rule 10 CPC seeking return of the plaint on two alternative and mutually inconsistent grounds: (i) that one head of the plaint's valuation (Rs. 2,00,00,000/- for "compensation/possession by allotment of alternate properties") is artificial and, once excluded, the suit falls below the pecuniary threshold of this Hon'ble Court; and (ii) that the suit raises "commercial disputes" under Section 2(1)(c)(vii) of the Commercial Courts Act, 2015 and had to be filed before the Commercial Division/Commercial Court. Both grounds are contrary to the plaint, contrary to law, and contrary to Defendant No. 5's own conduct and stands in earlier proceedings. The Plaintiff has separately filed I.A. No. 16110/2026 seeking exemplary costs against Defendant No. 5 for filing the present and the companion misleading applications, and its contents may be read as part hereof.

2. The application is also internally and externally self-contradictory: (i) internally, because a family-corpus partition claim cannot at once be below Rs. 2 crores in value (ground I) and yet comprise "commercial disputes of specified value" (ground II); and (ii) externally, because Defendant No. 5's own group companion application (I.A. No. 16112/2026 of Defendant No. 1) asserts that the plaint concerns properties worth approximately Rs. 81 crores and is grossly *under*valued. A plaint cannot be simultaneously undervalued at Rs. 81 crores and overvalued at Rs. 2 crores. Such contradictory threshold attacks through split applications constitute abuse of process (cf. *Future Sector Land Developers LLP v. Bagmane Developers (P) Ltd.*, (2023) 5 SCC 368, on inconsistent invocation of Order VII Rules 10 and 11).

## PRELIMINARY OBJECTIONS

3. **The valuation head attacked has a corresponding pleaded relief.** The head "compensation/possession by allotment of alternate properties from the Residual PPJ Corpus in lieu of alienated properties, together with permanent injunction restraining alienation of the alternate/residual properties" (plaint paragraph 64, third entry of the valuation table, valued at Rs. 2,00,00,000/- with court fee paid thereon) corresponds directly to: Prayer A (which in terms seeks "equitable compensation for alienated properties"); Prayer C's residual clause ("and/or such other properties forming part of the PPJ Family Corpus as may be found available and suitable for allotment"); and Prayer G ("allotment of balance share in remaining properties and jewellery and payment of mesne profits, the quantum whereof shall be determined upon rendition of accounts and the auditor's report"). The application's premise that "the prayers sought in the plaint do not contain any relief which corresponds" to that head is factually incorrect on the face of the plaint.

4. **Plaint valuation is, in law, the plaintiff's domain absent demonstrable mala fides.** In suits where the relief is incapable of precise ascertainment at institution (here: compensation in lieu of alienated properties, quantifiable only upon rendition of accounts and the auditor's report, as the valuation table itself records), the plaintiff's bona fide valuation governs pecuniary jurisdiction, and the Court does not exclude heads of valuation at the threshold on a defendant's say-so. Moreover, the plaint (paragraph 67) values the suit above Rs. 2,00,00,000/- on the aggregate of all reliefs — including working out of the 23% share in a corpus which, on Defendant No. 1's own companion application, includes immovable properties worth approximately Rs. 81 crores. The suggestion that the suit's value falls below the threshold of this Hon'ble Court's ordinary original civil jurisdiction is untenable on any view.

5. **A partition/family-arrangement suit is not a "commercial dispute".** The suit seeks partition and working out of the Plaintiff's antecedent 23% share in a family corpus pursuant to an oral family settlement — a dispute among family members governed by the principles of *Kale v. Deputy Director of Consolidation*, (1976) 3 SCC 119. It does not "arise out of" any "agreement relating to immovable property used exclusively in trade or commerce" within Section 2(1)(c)(vii) of the Commercial Courts Act, 2015. The Hon'ble Supreme Court in *Ambalal Sarabhai Enterprises Ltd. v. K.S. Infraspace LLP*, (2020) 15 SCC 585 — the very authority whose "actually used" test the application borrows at paragraph 13 — held that the Commercial Courts Act must be strictly construed and a dispute does not become commercial merely because immovable property with commercial user is involved. The character of the action is determined by the plaint: a family partition action does not change character because some assets in the corpus have commercial user.

6. **The issues have been dealt with in earlier proceedings in which Defendant No. 5 participated.** Defendant No. 5 was a respondent in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022 and participated in the proceedings culminating in the judgment dated 22.03.2024, in the recall proceedings in I.A. No. 39500/2024, and in the proceedings before the Hon'ble Supreme Court culminating in the judgment dated 13.08.2025 *(extent of Defendant No. 5's individual participation at each stage to be verified by counsel from the certified records)*. At no stage in those proceedings — in which the family disputes, the FSDs and the family corpus were squarely in issue — was it ever contended by Defendant No. 5 or his group that the disputes were "commercial disputes" triable only by a Commercial Court; on the contrary, the disputes were uniformly characterised as family settlement disputes. The judgment dated 22.03.2024 (paragraphs 17–18) records the Defendant groups' statements that the arbitration would not affect the rights and interest of the Plaintiff, and the Hon'ble Supreme Court's judgment dated 13.08.2025 (paragraph 25) left the parties "free to work out their rights in accordance with the order dated 22.03.2024". The present suit is that working-out; the belated forum objection is an afterthought designed to defer it.

7. **Clarification regarding the order dated 12.11.2024.** The Plaintiff places no reliance on the order dated 12.11.2024, which stands set aside by the Hon'ble Supreme Court's judgment dated 13.08.2025. Reliance is placed only on the subsisting judgment dated 22.03.2024, the statements recorded therein, and the Supreme Court's judgment dated 13.08.2025.

## PARAGRAPH-WISE REPLY ON MERITS

8. **Reply to paragraph 1 of the application** (application under Order VII Rule 10 alleging want of jurisdiction): Denied. This Hon'ble Court possesses pecuniary and subject-matter jurisdiction. The suit is valued above Rs. 2,00,00,000/- (plaint paragraphs 64 and 67); the subject matter is a family partition/working-out action within the ordinary original civil jurisdiction; and no part of it is a "commercial dispute".

9. **Reply to paragraph 2 of the application** (reliance only on the plaint; no admissions): The Applicant's professed reliance on the plaint is selective: the application ignores the valuation table's own terms, the residual/alternate-property reliefs in Prayers A, C and G, and paragraph 67 of the plaint.

10. **Reply to paragraph 3 of the application** (twin grounds: artificial valuation; commercial disputes): Denied. Ground (i) fails because the Rs. 2,00,00,000/- head corresponds to pleaded reliefs (Prayers A, C-residual and G) and because the aggregate valuation independently exceeds the threshold. Ground (ii) fails because a family partition suit is not a commercial dispute; Section 2(1)(c)(vii) is inapplicable as no "agreement relating to immovable property used exclusively in trade or commerce" is the source of the dispute. The two grounds are, in any event, mutually inconsistent as set out in paragraph 2 above.

11. **Reply to paragraph 4 of the application** (this Court not the proper forum): Denied for the reasons in paragraphs 8–10 above.

12. **Reply to paragraph 5 of the application** (the Rs. 2,00,00,000/- valuation under "compensation/possession by allotment of alternate properties" has no corresponding prayer): Denied as factually incorrect. The corresponding reliefs are expressly pleaded: Prayer A seeks, inter alia, "equitable compensation for alienated properties"; Prayer C concludes with "and/or such other properties forming part of the PPJ Family Corpus as may be found available and suitable for allotment"; Prayer G seeks allotment of balance share in remaining properties with quantum to be determined on the auditor's report. The valuation entry itself records that the value of the alienated properties and equivalent alternate properties is "to be determined upon auditor's report". The head of valuation and the prayers correspond in substance precisely.

13. **Reply to paragraph 6 of the application** (no basis disclosed for the Rs. 2,00,00,000/- figure; no identification of alternate properties): Denied. Where properties of the corpus have been alienated by the Defendants (as pleaded in the plaint), the compensation/alternate-allotment relief is by nature unascertainable at institution; the plaint says so and undertakes quantification on the auditor's report. A bona fide estimated valuation of an unascertainable relief is the accepted mode of valuation; it is not open to a defendant to demand pre-quantification of what only discovery, accounts and the auditor's report can quantify — particularly where the relevant records are in the Defendants' own custody.

14. **Reply to paragraph 7 of the application** (the "only intelligible purpose" of the Rs. 2,00,00,000/- figure is to cross the pecuniary threshold): Denied. The insinuation of forum-manufacturing is baseless: (i) the head corresponds to real reliefs (paragraph 12 above); (ii) the aggregate value of the suit exceeds the threshold independently of this head, the corpus including (on Defendant No. 1's own application) immovable properties of approximately Rs. 81 crores; and (iii) the Plaintiff has claimed mesne profits of 23% of all sales and revenue of the P.P. Jewellers Group from 30.09.2014 (Prayer E) — a claim whose value on any realistic estimate far exceeds Rs. 2,00,00,000/-.

15. **Reply to paragraph 8 of the application** (a plaintiff cannot confer jurisdiction by arbitrary valuation of a non-existent relief): The proposition of law is not disputed in the abstract; its premise — that the relief is "non-existent or unclaimed" — is false as shown above. The paragraph is otherwise denied.

16. **Reply to paragraph 9 of the application** (once the Rs. 2,00,00,000/- head is excluded, the remaining reliefs do not meet the threshold): Denied. First, there is no basis to exclude the head. Secondly, the remaining reliefs — working out of a 23% share in a corpus containing properties the Defendants themselves value at Rs. 81 crores, mesne profits of 23% of the group's sales and revenue since 30.09.2014, rendition of accounts of the entire corpus, and handover of two partnership firms with their stock-in-trade — manifestly exceed Rs. 2,00,00,000/-. The Applicant's arithmetic is possible only by ignoring the plaint. Notably, Defendant No. 1's companion application asserts gross *under*valuation; Defendant No. 5 cannot be heard to assert the opposite.

17. **Reply to paragraph 10 of the application** (suit not maintainable for want of pecuniary jurisdiction; plaint liable to be returned): Denied for the reasons aforesaid.

18. **Reply to paragraph 11 of the application** (substantial reliefs pertain to immovable properties used exclusively in trade and commerce — Karol Bagh 2129–2130, Gali Bhairon Wali 4262–4265 and the Gurugram showroom): Denied. First, the suit is for partition/working out of a family corpus; the three properties named are but items in a corpus comprising numerous assets, firms, companies, jewellery stock and residential and agricultural properties (including 5 acres at Jaffarpur Kalan, 4 acres at Village Daulatpur, a Defence Colony plot, and residential flats) — the Applicant's selective extraction of three allegedly commercial items cannot recharacterise the composite family action. Secondly, Section 2(1)(c)(vii) requires the dispute to arise out of an *agreement relating to immovable property* used exclusively in trade or commerce; the dispute here arises out of a family settlement/partition of a corpus, not out of any commercial agreement relating to any property. Thirdly, whether any particular property is "used exclusively" in trade or commerce, and by whom, is itself a question of fact incapable of resolution on demurrer.

19. **Reply to paragraphs 12 and 13 of the application** (statutory definitions; "used" means actually used — settled law): The statutory provisions are matters of record. The Applicant's own authority-framework (*Ambalal Sarabhai*, supra) mandates strict construction of the Commercial Courts Act precisely so that ordinary civil actions are not forced into the commercial track; it defeats, rather than supports, the application. A partition suit among family members is the paradigm of an ordinary civil action.

20. **Reply to paragraph 14 of the application** (mischaracterising such a suit as ordinary civil action invokes a forum lacking jurisdiction): Denied. There is no mischaracterisation; the suit is what it pleads — a family partition/working-out action. It is the application which mischaracterises the suit by isolating three properties from a composite corpus.

21. **Reply to paragraph 15 of the application** (reliefs concerning these properties involve possession, enforcement of allocation, damages and "consequential commercial benefits", hence commercial disputes of specified value): Denied. The reliefs flow from partition of the family corpus. The expression "consequential commercial benefits" appears nowhere in the plaint. It is further noteworthy that the Applicant here asserts "commercial disputes of specified value" (i.e., value at or above the statutory threshold) while contending in ground I that the suit's valuation cannot even reach Rs. 2,00,00,000/-; the two contentions cannot coexist.

22. **Reply to paragraphs 16 and 17 of the application** (suit ought to have been instituted before the Commercial Court; this Court lacks subject-matter jurisdiction): Denied. No relief in the suit constitutes a "commercial dispute"; there is, accordingly, no question of the mandatory requirements of the Commercial Courts Act (including pre-institution mediation under Section 12A) applying to this family partition action. The Commercial Division and the Ordinary Original Civil Jurisdiction are, in any event, both within this Hon'ble Court; the objection, even if it had substance (which is denied), would raise at best a question of assignment of the suit to the appropriate Bench under this Hon'ble Court's rules rather than "return" of the plaint under Order VII Rule 10 CPC to some other court. The prayer for return is legally misconceived on the Applicant's own case.

23. **Reply to paragraph 18 of the application** (summation — Order VII Rule 10 mandates return): Denied for all the reasons aforesaid. Order VII Rule 10 applies where the Court "finds" it has no jurisdiction; no such finding is possible on the plaint here.

24. **Reply to paragraph 19 of the application** (bona fides and prejudice): Denied. The application is not bona fide: it is one of two simultaneous threshold applications by Defendant No. 5 which, together with Defendant No. 1's application, assert three mutually inconsistent positions — that the suit discloses no cause of action, that it is hopelessly time-barred, that it is undervalued at Rs. 81 crores, and that it is overvalued at Rs. 2 crores — the common object being to prevent adjudication of the working-out suit which the Hon'ble Supreme Court's judgment dated 13.08.2025 (paragraph 25) expressly contemplated. No prejudice is caused to the Applicant by the suit being tried; grave prejudice is caused to the Plaintiff by these obstructive applications.

25. **Reply to the prayer of the application**: The prayer for return of the plaint is unsustainable and deserves dismissal with exemplary costs.

## EARLIER PROCEEDINGS AND CONTRADICTORY STANDS OF DEFENDANT NO. 5 (ISSUE-WISE MAPPING)

26. **Objection (i): "The valuation is artificial; the suit is below the pecuniary threshold" (application paragraphs 5–10).**
    - Earlier stand: Defendant No. 5's group participated in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022 and the Supreme Court proceedings, in which the family corpus and the Plaintiff's 23% share therein — a share whose subject matter the Defendants' own pleadings value in tens of crores (see Defendant No. 1's I.A. No. 16112/2026: approximately Rs. 81 crores for seven properties alone) — were the subject matter; the Kamal Gupta Group's counsel's admission of the Plaintiff's 23% share is recorded in the Plaintiff's pleadings before the Supreme Court *(counsel to verify the precise record — oral arguments of 16.07.2025 as pleaded in the SLP record)*.
    - Analysis: The present objection is irreconcilable both with the record and with the companion application's assertion of Rs. 81 crores; it is approbation and reprobation through split applications. Independent merits answer: the valuation head corresponds to pleaded reliefs; aggregate value exceeds the threshold; bona fide valuation of unascertainable reliefs governs.

27. **Objection (ii): "The disputes are commercial disputes" (application paragraphs 11–17).**
    - Earlier stand: In ARB.P. No. 1010/2022, the recall proceedings (I.A. No. 39500/2024) and the Supreme Court proceedings, the Defendant groups uniformly characterised the disputes as family settlement disputes arising from the FSDs recording the oral family settlement dated 30.09.2014, and procured orders on that basis — including the judgment dated 22.03.2024 (paragraphs 17–18: inter se family disputes not affecting the Plaintiff's rights) and the Supreme Court's judgment dated 13.08.2025 (paragraph 25: rights to be worked out in accordance with the order dated 22.03.2024). At no stage was a "commercial dispute" characterisation advanced.
    - Analysis: The new forum objection is contrary to the consistent earlier characterisation and is barred as approbation and reprobation; it is also an attempt to re-agitate, through a jurisdictional label, the same threshold obstruction rejected by the flow of the earlier proceedings. Independent merits answer: Section 2(1)(c)(vii) is inapplicable to a family partition action (paragraph 18 above; *Ambalal Sarabhai*, supra).
