# REPLY 1

## IN THE HIGH COURT OF DELHI AT NEW DELHI (ORDINARY ORIGINAL CIVIL JURISDICTION)

## REPLY ON BEHALF OF THE PLAINTIFF TO I.A. No. 16112/2026

### (Application of Defendant No. 1 under Order VII Rule 11(b) and (c) CPC read with the Court Fees Act, 1870 — alleged undervaluation / deficient court fee)

**IN:** CS(OS) No. 545 of 2026

**IN THE MATTER OF:** RAHUL GUPTA …Plaintiff **VERSUS** KAMAL GUPTA & ORS. …Defendants

**MOST RESPECTFULLY SHOWETH:**

### A. PRELIMINARY SUBMISSIONS

1. The application is a transparent attempt by Defendant No. 1 to stall the suit and defer the Plaintiff's applications for interim protection (IA No. 16084/2026) and judgment on admissions (IA No. 16089/2026), by re-agitating questions which stand concluded against the Defendants by their own solemn statements and undertakings recorded in judicial orders — in **ARB.P. No. 1010/2022** (judgment dated 22.03.2024 of this Hon'ble Court) and before the Hon'ble Supreme Court in the **two rounds of Special Leave Petitions** arising from the said proceedings, namely (i) **SLP(C) Nos. 4775-4779/2025** (against the order dated 12.11.2024, decided by judgment dated 13.08.2025, 2025 INSC 975) and (ii) **SLP(C) No. 22163/2026 (Diary No. 37707/2026)** (against the order dated 19.06.2026 in this very suit, dismissed as withdrawn on 13.07.2026 with all contentions left open to be decided by this Hon'ble Court).

2. **The foundational facts on which this application turns were already dealt with in ARB.P. 1010/2022.** In the judgment dated 22.03.2024 (Sachin Datta, J.), this Hon'ble Court recorded, as the factual background common to all parties:

   2.1 that *"an Oral Family Settlement was arrived on 30.09.2014; this was recorded in the Family Settlement Deed dated 20.06.2015"*, followed by the Family Settlement Deed dated 09.07.2019 (paragraph 4 of the judgment);

   2.2 that **Annexure 'A' of the FSD itself "identifies and separates the assets and businesses of Mr. Rahul Gupta, in which he has full/partial control, from the performance of the FSD"** (paragraph 4);

   2.3 that *"A categorical statement has been made by learned senior counsel for petitioners and respondents that none of the properties owned by the intervenor companies, though mentioned in annexures of the FSD, will be dealt/divided by the said parties in the arbitration"* (paragraph 17); and

   2.4 that *"The intent of Pawan Gupta Group and Kamal Gupta Group when they executed FSD, was to settle their inter se disputes while at the same time not affecting the rights and interest of Mr. Rahul Gupta"* (paragraph 18).

3. Before the Hon'ble Supreme Court in the appeals arising out of SLP(C) Nos. 4775-4779/2025, the judgment dated 13.08.2025 records (paragraph 10) the submission that the reliefs in the order dated 12.11.2024 *"recognising his [RG's] 23% rights in the family properties were based on the undertaking given by the signatories to the MoU/FSD"* recorded in paragraphs 17 and 18 of the judgment dated 22.03.2024. The Hon'ble Supreme Court set aside the order dated 12.11.2024 **solely on jurisdictional grounds** (the Court having become functus officio after the Section 11(6) appointment, and a non-signatory having no right to remain present in arbitral proceedings), expressly directing that *"the parties are free to work out their rights in accordance with the order dated 22.03.2024"* — an order which itself preserves, in paragraphs 17 and 18, the Defendants' assurance that the Plaintiff's rights and interests are not to be affected. The present suit is the Plaintiff working out those very rights in the only forum left open to him.

4. Defendant No. 1 and his group, having themselves invoked and enforced the family settlement framework in ARB.P. 1010/2022 — where this Hon'ble Court, at their instance, applied *Kale v. Deputy Director of Consolidation*, (1976) 3 SCC 119, holding that it is the duty of courts to uphold and give full effect to family arrangements instead of disturbing them on technical or trivial grounds — cannot now approbate and reprobate by attacking the Plaintiff's suit, founded on the anterior Oral Family Settlement of 30.09.2014 recorded in those same documents, on technical grounds of valuation and court fee.

### B. PARAGRAPH-WISE ANSWER ON MERITS

5. **The application suppresses paragraph 603 of the plaint** — the operative pleading on possession — which expressly avers: *"the Plaintiff submits that he continues to remain a co-owner and co-sharer in the entire PP Jewellers Family Corpus and is deemed to be in joint and constructive possession thereof… The present suit is therefore not a suit by an excluded stranger seeking recovery of possession, but a suit by a co-owner and co-sharer in joint and constructive possession seeking partition by metes and bounds."* An application which is required by its own terms to proceed on the averments of the plaint, yet omits the central averment on the very issue it raises, is misconceived and deserves dismissal with costs on this ground alone.

6. The application proceeds on a deliberate conflation of (i) the Plaintiff's forcible exclusion from the **family residence and physical control of the vault stocks** in November 2015 with (ii) ouster from **co-ownership of the PPJ Family Corpus**. The plaint pleads the former as an incident of the Defendants' highhandedness; it pleads the latter nowhere. On the contrary:

   6.1 the Plaintiff pleads that he **continues in physical possession** of the properties at Village Rawta, New Delhi (paragraph 574 of the plaint);

   6.2 the Plaintiff pleads **constructive possession** of Property Nos. 2129-2130, Gurudwara Road, Karol Bagh and 24A-24B, Mama Parmanand Marg, Mumbai (paragraph 575); and

   6.3 the Plaintiff pleads continuing co-ownership and deemed joint/constructive possession of the entire corpus (paragraph 603).

7. It is settled law that in a suit for partition by a co-owner, possession of one co-owner is possession of all, and exclusion for the purpose of court fee must be a clear, unequivocal ouster pleaded by the plaintiff himself. Where the plaint asserts joint/constructive possession, fixed court fee under Article 17(vi) of Schedule II of the Court Fees Act, 1870 is payable, and the plaint averments must be taken as they stand at the Order VII Rule 11 stage. The Defendants cannot rewrite the plaint to manufacture an "ouster" the Plaintiff has not pleaded.

8. The Defendants' reliance on the ouster-from-residence narrative is also **directly contrary to their own recorded position**: in ARB.P. 1010/2022 the Kamal Gupta Group and Pawan Gupta Group assured this Hon'ble Court (paragraphs 17 and 18 of the judgment dated 22.03.2024) that the Plaintiff's rights and interests in the assets identified in the FSD's annexures were separate, identified, and would not be affected. A party who has judicially acknowledged that the Plaintiff's allocated assets stand identified and protected cannot simultaneously contend that the Plaintiff stands ousted from all such assets so as to attract ad valorem court fee on their full market value.

9. In any event, and without prejudice, even where a court finds the fee deficient, the mandatorily prescribed course under Order VII Rule 11(b) and (c) CPC is to require the plaintiff to correct the valuation or supply the deficit fee **within a time to be fixed by the Court**, and rejection can follow only upon failure to comply. The prayer for outright rejection is contrary to the statute.

10. The question of court fee is a matter primarily between the plaintiff and the State; the Defendant's anxiety on this score, coupled with the simultaneous and mutually contradictory applications of Defendant No. 5 (who says the suit is *overvalued* artificially for pecuniary jurisdiction while Defendant No. 1 says it is *undervalued* for court fee), betrays the true, dilatory purpose of these applications.

### PRAYER

It is therefore most respectfully prayed that this Hon'ble Court may be pleased to dismiss I.A. No. 16112/2026 with exemplary costs, and pass such other order(s) as deemed fit.

# REPLY 2

## REPLY ON BEHALF OF THE PLAINTIFF TO I.A. No. 16113/2026

### (Application of Defendant No. 5 under Order VII Rule 10 CPC — alleged want of pecuniary jurisdiction / commercial dispute)

**MOST RESPECTFULLY SHOWETH:**

### A. PRELIMINARY SUBMISSIONS

1. The Plaintiff repeats and adopts paragraphs 1 to 4 of the Reply to I.A. No. 16112/2026 (the prior proceedings in ARB.P. 1010/2022 and the two SLPs) which are not repeated for brevity. The Plaintiff's 23% allocation, the identification of his assets in the FSD's own annexures, and the Defendants' recorded assurances that his rights would not be affected, were all dealt with in those proceedings; this application ignores that entire judicial record.

2. It is significant that the Defendants' group challenged the order dated 19.06.2026 passed in this very suit before the Hon'ble Supreme Court in **SLP(C) No. 22163/2026 (Diary No. 37707/2026)**, which was **dismissed as withdrawn on 13.07.2026**, with the Hon'ble Supreme Court recording that *"All contentions between the parties are left open to be decided by the High Court."* Jurisdictional objections of the present kind, if bona fide, would have been the first contention urged there; instead, the strategy is to raise seriatim threshold objections in every forum to stall adjudication.

### B. ON THE ALLEGED "ARTIFICIAL" VALUATION

3. The plaintiff is *dominus litis* and is entitled to value his suit; the valuation can be rejected at the threshold only if it is patently absurd or demonstrably dishonest. The suit valuation aggregates **multiple heads** — partition of the PPJ Family Corpus, protection of constructive possession, delivery of allocated properties, compensation qua alienated properties, rendition of accounts, mesne profits and injunction.

4. The attack on the Rs. 2,00,00,000/- head ("compensation / possession by allotment of alternate properties", paragraph 64 of the plaint) is factually wrong: the plaint (paragraph 606) expressly pleads that properties allocated to the Plaintiff have been alienated by the Defendants and reserves the claim to compensation / allotment of equivalent properties; Prayer C itself seeks delivery of allocated properties *"and/or such other properties forming part of the PPJ Family Corpus as may be found available and suitable for allotment"*. The head of valuation thus corresponds to a real, pleaded relief.

5. Even if the impugned head were notionally excluded, the suit on its own showing vastly exceeds the Rs. 2 crore threshold of this Hon'ble Court's ordinary original jurisdiction — the plaint discloses properties aggregating approximately Rs. 81 crores under Prayer C alone, and the corpus of which 23% is claimed runs into several hundreds of crores. The argument that the suit would fall *below* Rs. 2 crores without the impugned head is stated only to be rejected; no computation supporting it is even attempted in the application.

### C. ON THE COMMERCIAL COURTS ACT

6. A partition suit between family members founded on an oral family settlement is not a "commercial dispute" under Section 2(1)(c) of the Commercial Courts Act, 2015. The clause (vii) relied upon — *"agreements relating to immovable property used exclusively in trade or commerce"* — presupposes a commercial agreement as the source of the dispute. The source of the present dispute is a **family settlement**, which the Defendants' own group has characterised, and this Hon'ble Court has in ARB.P. 1010/2022 (applying *Kale*) treated, as an arrangement whose object is family peace, not trade. The nature of the dispute, not the incidental character of some assets, is determinative (*Ambalal Sarabhai Enterprises Ltd. v. K.S. Infraspace LLP*, (2020) 15 SCC 585).

7. The Defendants' applications are, moreover, mutually destructive: Defendant No. 1 (I.A. 16112/2026) treats the suit as a partition suit chargeable ad valorem; Defendant No. 5 in the present application treats it as a commercial suit; and Defendant No. 5's companion application (I.A. 16114/2026) treats it as a suit for enforcement of an agreement. The Defendants may choose any characterisation, but not all three at once — the shifting characterisations demonstrate that the object is delay, not adjudication.

8. Even otherwise, an objection under the Commercial Courts Act, if it had substance (it has none), would lead at best to transfer/re-presentation to the Commercial Division **of this very Court** — not to the return of the plaint for want of jurisdiction as prayed.

### PRAYER

It is therefore most respectfully prayed that this Hon'ble Court may be pleased to dismiss I.A. No. 16113/2026 with exemplary costs, and pass such other order(s) as deemed fit.

# REPLY 3

## REPLY ON BEHALF OF THE PLAINTIFF TO I.A. No. 16114/2026

### (Application of Defendant No. 5 under Order VII Rule 11(a) and (d) CPC — alleged bar of limitation / no cause of action)

**MOST RESPECTFULLY SHOWETH:**

### A. PRELIMINARY SUBMISSIONS — THE ISSUES STAND DEALT WITH IN ARB.P. 1010/2022 AND THE TWO SLPs

1. The entire premise of the application — that the Oral Family Settlement dated 30.09.2014 is a stale "alleged oral agreement" whose breach in 2015 set limitation running — is falsified by the judicial record created **by the Defendants' own group**:

   1.1 In **ARB.P. 1010/2022**, the Pawan Gupta Group and the Kamal Gupta Group themselves petitioned this Hon'ble Court to enforce the family settlement framework — the Oral Family Settlement of 30.09.2014 recorded in the FSD dated 20.06.2015 and the FSD dated 09.07.2019 — and obtained, by the judgment dated 22.03.2024, the appointment of a sole arbitrator to work out that very settlement. It does not lie in the mouth of the same group to contend, two years later, that rights under the identical settlement became unenforceable in 2018.

   1.2 The judgment dated 22.03.2024 records (paragraph 4) that Annexure 'A' of the FSD **identifies and separates the assets and businesses of Mr. Rahul Gupta**; and records (paragraphs 17 and 18) the parties' categorical statements that the intervenors' properties would not be dealt with in the arbitration and that the FSD was executed *"while at the same time not affecting the rights and interest of Mr. Rahul Gupta."* These recorded statements of 22.03.2024 are, at the very least, solemn judicial acknowledgments of the subsistence of the Plaintiff's rights and allocation as on that date.

   1.3 By the judgment dated **13.08.2025** in the appeals arising out of **SLP(C) Nos. 4775-4779/2025** (2025 INSC 975), the Hon'ble Supreme Court, while setting aside the order dated 12.11.2024 on jurisdictional grounds, held that a non-signatory such as the Plaintiff is a stranger to the arbitration whose *"remedy, if any… is available under Section 36 of the Act if such award is sought to be enforced against him"*, and directed that *"the parties are free to work out their rights in accordance with the order dated 22.03.2024."* The Plaintiff — excluded from the arbitral forum at the Defendants' group's own instance — has therefore approached the civil court, the only forum available to him, to work out the rights which the order dated 22.03.2024 preserves. The cause of action for the present suit, in this crystallised form, arose no earlier than 22.03.2024 / 13.08.2025; the suit filed on 17.06.2026 is well within time on any view.

   1.4 In **SLP(C) No. 22163/2026 (Diary No. 37707/2026)** — the Defendants' group's challenge to the order dated 19.06.2026 in this very suit — the petition was dismissed as withdrawn on 13.07.2026 with *"all contentions between the parties… left open to be decided by the High Court."* No plea of limitation was pursued there either.

### B. THE SUIT IS A PARTITION SUIT ON ANTECEDENT TITLE — NOT A SUIT FOR ENFORCEMENT OF CONTRACT

2. The application mischaracterises the suit. Under settled law — *Kale v. Deputy Director of Consolidation*, (1976) 3 SCC 119 — a family arrangement is not an ordinary commercial contract: it recognises and declares **antecedent title** in the members; a memorandum recording a completed oral family arrangement neither requires registration nor operates as a conveyance. The Plaintiff's 23% share vested under the Oral Family Settlement of 30.09.2014; the FSD of 20.06.2015 and the MOU/FSD of 09.07.2019 **record** that allocation (as this Hon'ble Court noted in paragraph 4 of the judgment dated 22.03.2024, Annexure 'A' of the FSD itself identifies and separates the Plaintiff's assets). The suit is for **partition — the working out and delivery of already-vested shares** — not for the creation of rights by enforcement of a contract.

3. This Hon'ble Court (Sachin Datta, J.) has recently reaffirmed the governing principle in *Bhupendra Kumar Sood v. GNCTD*, W.P.(C) 14448/2025 (decided 01.07.2026), relying on *Shub Karan Bubna v. Sita Saran Bubna*, (2009) 9 SCC 689: partition is *"a redistribution or adjustment of pre-existing rights… and delivery thereof to the respective allottees"*; an instrument or suit which works out a division that already stands effectuated does not create rights afresh. Articles 54, 55 and 58 of the Limitation Act, which the application invokes, govern suits for specific performance, breach of contract and declarations — none of which is the frame or substance of this suit.

4. The right of a co-owner to seek partition is a **continuing right**; limitation does not run against it unless there is a clear, hostile and unequivocal ouster brought home to the plaintiff and the plaintiff sues for possession upon such ouster. The plaint pleads continuing co-ownership and joint/constructive possession (paragraph 603), actual physical possession of the Rawta properties (paragraph 574), and constructive possession of the Karol Bagh and Mumbai properties (paragraph 575). Whether there was ouster, when, and with what knowledge, are **mixed questions of law and fact** requiring trial; they cannot be resolved on a demurrer under Order VII Rule 11 (*it is settled that limitation, where it depends on disputed facts, is not a ground for rejection of the plaint*).

5. Without prejudice, even if (contrary to the above) limitation were computed with reference to acknowledgment of the Plaintiff's subsisting rights, the Defendants' group's own conduct provides successive acknowledgments within the meaning of Section 18 of the Limitation Act and successive parts of the cause of action: the FSD of 09.07.2019; the statements and assurances recorded in paragraphs 17 and 18 of the judgment dated 22.03.2024; the stand taken before the Hon'ble Supreme Court (recorded in the judgment dated 13.08.2025) that the recognition of the Plaintiff's 23% rights flowed from the undertakings of the signatories; and the alienations of allocated properties continuing into 2025-2026 (Schedule C, IA No. 16111/2026). The suit filed on 17.06.2026 is within three years of each of the last-mentioned events.

### C. ON "NO CAUSE OF ACTION" AND THE ALLEGED WANT OF A DECLARATORY PRAYER

6. The plea that the suit is bad for want of a prayer declaring the Oral Family Settlement misses the nature of the action: a co-owner suing for partition on antecedent title needs no declaration of the arrangement which merely records his title (*Kale*, supra). In any event, whether the reliefs as framed are adequate is a matter of the merits and, at worst, of amendment — not of rejection under Order VII Rule 11(a). A plaint discloses a cause of action if the facts pleaded, taken as true, entitle the plaintiff to some relief; the plaint here pleads title, allocation, the Defendants' recorded acknowledgments, exclusion from enjoyment, alienations, and the SC-preserved liberty to work out rights — a complete bundle of facts.

7. The application is the third of three mutually destructive threshold attacks (see paragraph 7 of the Reply to I.A. No. 16113/2026) and is, like its companions, a device to defer the hearing of the Plaintiff's interim applications while alienations of the corpus continue. It deserves dismissal with exemplary costs.

### PRAYER

It is therefore most respectfully prayed that this Hon'ble Court may be pleased to dismiss I.A. No. 16114/2026 with exemplary costs, and pass such other order(s) as deemed fit.
