## REPLY ON BEHALF OF THE PLAINTIFF TO THE APPLICATION UNDER ORDER VII RULE 11(a) AND (d) CPC FILED BY DEFENDANT NO. 5

### MOST RESPECTFULLY SHOWETH:

**1.** That the present Reply is being filed on behalf of the Plaintiff in response to the Application filed by Defendant No. 5 (Mr. Mukesh Gupta) under Order VII Rule 11(a) and (d) CPC seeking rejection of the Plaint on the grounds that: (i) the suit is barred by limitation; and (ii) the Plaint does not disclose a cause of action. Both grounds are legally untenable, factually incorrect, and founded upon material suppression of the Plaint's averments and the Defendants' own judicial admissions.

## A. THERE IS NO LIMITATION FOR A SUIT FOR PARTITION

**2.** The Application's limitation argument is founded on the premise that the suit is "in substance" a suit for enforcement of an oral agreement, not a suit for partition. This premise is factually and legally wrong.

**3.** The Plaint expressly characterizes the suit as a suit by a co-owner seeking partition (Para 603):

> *"The present suit is therefore not a suit by an excluded stranger seeking recovery of possession, but a suit by a co-owner and co-sharer in joint and constructive possession seeking partition by metes and bounds, segregation of his admitted 23% share in the PPJ Family Corpus and separate, peaceful and exclusive possession of the assets ultimately found to fall to his share."*

**4.** Under O.VII R.11, the averments in the plaint must be taken at their face value. The Defendant cannot recharacterize the suit as something other than what the plaint expressly states it is.

**5.** It is settled law that **a suit for partition by a co-owner is NOT subject to limitation.** The right to seek partition is an inherent and indefeasible right of every co-owner that subsists as long as co-ownership subsists.

**Case Law:**
- **K. Laxmanan v. Thekkayil Padmini, (2009) 1 SCC 354:** *"The right to claim partition is an inherent right of every co-owner. It is a right which cannot be taken away by limitation or by adverse possession of a co-owner."*
- **Muhammad Hussain v. Kishva Nand, AIR 1937 PC 250:** A co-owner's right to partition cannot be defeated by limitation.

**6.** The Application argues (Para 20) that this is not a suit between coparceners for partition of joint Hindu family property and that properties are held by companies. This is a red herring. A partition need NOT be a Hindu coparcenary partition:

- A **family settlement** creating defined shares among family members is a recognized form of partition
- The Defendants themselves executed the Memorandum 2015 and MOU 2019 dividing assets in specific ratios: Kamal 18%, Pawan 23%, Rahul 23%, Mukesh 18%, Mohit 18%
- This division created co-ownership with defined shares — each co-owner has the inherent right to seek partition

**Case Law:**
- **Kale v. Deputy Director of Consolidation, (1976) 3 SCC 119:** A family arrangement is binding on the parties and creates enforceable rights. It need not conform to any particular statutory framework.

## B. EVEN IF TREATED AS ENFORCEMENT — SECTION 18 LIMITATION ACT RESTARTS LIMITATION

**7.** Without prejudice to the submission that no limitation applies to partition suits, and in the alternative, even assuming (without admitting) that the suit is treated as one for "enforcement of oral agreement" governed by a 3-year limitation, the suit is STILL within limitation by virtue of **Section 18 of the Limitation Act, 1963**, which provides that every acknowledgment of a right restarts the limitation period.

**8.** The Defendants have acknowledged the Plaintiff's 23% share on MULTIPLE occasions, each of which restarts the limitation clock:

| Date | Acknowledgment | By Whom |
|------|---------------|---------|
| 20.06.2015 | Memorandum 2015 — records 23% allocation | All Defendants |
| 09.07.2019 | MOU 2019 — reiterates 23% share | All Defendants |
| **22.03.2024** | **Undertaking recorded in Para 18 of order in Arb.P. 1010/2022** | **D1, D7** |
| 2024 | Written submissions in IA 39500/2024: *"Shri Kamal Gupta undertakes to abide by both the aforesaid undertakings"* | D1 |
| 2024 | Reply before Arbitral Tribunal — acknowledges 23% ratio and Plaintiff's right to vault of choice | D1 |

**9.** The **MOST RECENT acknowledgment** is the undertaking recorded in Para 18 of the order dated 22.03.2024. From this date, even a 3-year limitation period would expire on **22.03.2027**. The present suit was filed on **17.06.2026** — well within 3 years.

**10.** The Application **completely ignores Section 18 of the Limitation Act** and the post-2019 acknowledgments. It restricts its limitation computation to the 2015 ouster and the 2019 MOU, while **suppressing** the 2024 undertaking and the 2024 written submissions. This is material suppression.

**Case Law:**
- **Shapoor Freedom Mazda v. Durga Prasad, (2009) 12 SCC 459:** An acknowledgment under Section 18 of the Limitation Act creates a fresh starting point for limitation.
- **Khan Bahadur Shapoor Fredoom Mazda v. Durga Prasad Chamaria, (2009) 12 SCC 459:** Even a mere affirmation of the existence of the right amounts to an acknowledgment under Section 18.

## C. THE CAUSE OF ACTION IS CONTINUING

**11.** The Application argues that the breach was a "completed refusal" in 2015 and is not continuing (Para 16). This is factually incorrect. The Plaint explicitly pleads a continuing cause of action, with multiple fresh causes of action arising within the last 3 years:

| Date | Fresh Cause of Action |

**12.** Each new alienation of a corpus property, each contradictory stand before a different forum, and each obstruction of the Plaintiff's rights constitutes a fresh and independent cause of action, well within the limitation period.

**Case Law:**
- **State of Bihar v. Deokaran Nenshi, (1972) 2 SCC 890:** Where the cause of action is continuing, the suit is not barred merely because the initial wrong occurred outside the limitation period.

## D. THE PLAINT DISCLOSES A CLEAR CAUSE OF ACTION

**13.** The Application under O.VII R.11(a) is meritless. The Plaint discloses the following bundle of facts constituting a clear cause of action:

   (a) Oral Family Settlement dated 30.09.2014 allocating 23% to the Plaintiff;
   (b) Memorandum 2015 and MOU 2019 recording and acknowledging the allocation;
   (c) Undertaking before the Hon'ble High Court recorded in Para 18 of the order dated 22.03.2024;
   (d) Direction of the Hon'ble Supreme Court (13.08.2025) to work out rights in accordance with 22.03.2024;
   (e) Failure and refusal of the Defendants to implement the allocation;
   (f) Continuing alienation and dissipation of corpus properties;
   (g) Contradictory stands by Defendants before different forums.

**14.** Under O.VII R.11(a), the test is whether, **IF the averments in the plaint are taken as true**, a cause of action is disclosed. Taking the above averments as true, the Plaintiff has a clear and complete cause of action for partition, rendition of accounts, injunction, and delivery of possession.

**Case Law:**
- **Liverpool & London S.P. & I Association Ltd. v. M.V. Sea Success I, (2004) 9 SCC 512:** A plaint discloses a cause of action if it contains every fact necessary for the plaintiff to prove to support his right to the judgment.
- **Dahiben v. Arvindbhai Kalyanji Bhanusali, (2020) 7 SCC 366:** For O.VII R.11, plaint averments must be taken at face value.

**15.** The argument that "no declaration of the oral agreement is sought" (Para 10 of the Application) is irrelevant:
   - Prayer A seeks to "give effect to the Plaintiff's antecedent and crystallised title to a 23% share" — functionally a declaration
   - The 23% share has been **ADMITTED** by the Defendants in multiple proceedings — admitted facts need no declaration
   - The Plaintiff has separately filed an Application under Order XII Rule 6 CPC (judgment on admissions) precisely relying on these admissions

## E. CONTRADICTORY POSITIONS BY THE SAME DEFENDANT

**16.** Defendant No. 5 has filed TWO simultaneous applications:

**17.** These two positions are **mutually contradictory.** If the suit is for enforcement of an oral family settlement (as argued in the limitation application), it cannot simultaneously be a "commercial dispute" (as argued in the jurisdiction application). The Defendant cannot blow hot and cold in the same proceeding.

Defendant No. 5 now argues it is **NOT a partition suit** but enforcement of oral agreement. The Defendants are taking coordinated but contradictory positions through different counsel to maximize delay.
