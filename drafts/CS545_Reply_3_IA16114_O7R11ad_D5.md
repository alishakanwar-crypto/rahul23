## REPLY ON BEHALF OF THE PLAINTIFF TO I.A. No. 16114/2026, BEING THE APPLICATION UNDER ORDER VII RULE 11(a) AND (d) READ WITH SECTION 151 CPC FILED BY DEFENDANT NO. 5 (MR. ## INTRODUCTORY SUBMISSIONS

1. The present Reply is filed on behalf of the Plaintiff to the application of Defendant No. 5 under Order VII Rule 11(a) and (d) CPC, which proceeds on the theory that the suit is "in truth" a time-barred action for enforcement of the oral family settlement dated 30.09.2014 dressed up as a partition suit, with limitation running from 2015 or, at the latest, from 09.07.2019 under Articles 54, 55 or 58 of the Limitation Act, 1963. The theory fails at every step: it mischaracterises the suit, ignores the plaint's pleadings of antecedent title, continuing co-ownership, joint and constructive possession and continuing acknowledgements, suppresses the intervening judicial proceedings (ARB.P. No. 1010/2022; I.A. No. 39500/2024; the Supreme Court proceedings culminating in the judgment dated 13.08.2025), and invites this Hon'ble Court to decide disputed questions of fact on demurrer. The Plaintiff's I.A. No. 16110/2026 (exemplary costs against Defendant No. 5) may be read as part of this Reply.

2. Three verified matters of record destroy the application's foundation:

   2.1. **The suit is a partition/working-out action on antecedent title.** Prayer A seeks effect to "the Plaintiff's antecedent and crystallised title to a 23% share in the PPJ Corpus — which title has always resided in the Plaintiff by virtue of the Oral Family Partition dated 30.09.2014, requires no conveyance to pass, and stands acknowledged in the Defendants' undertaking recorded in Para 18 of the order dated 22.03.2024" and a preliminary decree under Order XX Rule 18 CPC. In law (*Kale v. Deputy Director of Consolidation*, (1976) 3 SCC 119), a family arrangement is not a transfer or a contract creating rights; it recognises antecedent title. A suit to work out such title by partition is not a suit "for enforcement of an agreement" governed by Articles 54/55/58.

   2.2. **The right to seek partition is a continuing right.** A co-owner's entitlement to partition subsists as long as co-ownership subsists; possession of one co-sharer is possession of all; limitation could begin to run against a co-owner only from clear ouster to his knowledge — a fact-intensive plea which the plaint denies and which cannot be assumed on demurrer (*Vidya Devi @ Vidya Vati (Dead) by LRs v. Prem Prakash*, AIR 1995 SC 1789).

   2.3. **The Supreme Court's judgment dated 13.08.2025 expressly left the parties to work out their rights.** Paragraph 25 thereof states: "The parties to the present proceedings are free to work out their rights in accordance with the order dated 22.03.2024." The Plaintiff — whose intervention in the arbitration was successfully opposed by the Defendant groups on the recorded assurance (judgment dated 22.03.2024, paragraphs 17–18) that his rights and interests would not be affected — filed the present working-out suit on 17.06.2026, within a year of the said judgment. The plea that this suit is a "hopelessly time-barred" claim that died in 2018 cannot be reconciled with the Defendants' own procurement of those orders.

## PRELIMINARY OBJECTIONS

3. **Limitation here is a mixed question of law and fact.** Whether the Plaintiff was ousted in 2015; whether the family arrangement was repudiated or remained the acknowledged basis of the parties' dealings (including in the FSD of 20.06.2015, the MOU of 09.07.2019, the Defendants' pleadings in CS(COMM) 1551/2021 and ARB.P. No. 1010/2022, and the statements recorded in the judgment dated 22.03.2024); whether acknowledgements within Section 18 of the Limitation Act, 1963 intervened — are all questions requiring evidence. It is settled that a plaint cannot be rejected under Order VII Rule 11(d) unless the suit is *ex facie* barred on the plaint's own uncontroverted averments; where limitation depends on disputed facts, the application must fail.

4. **The application suppresses the intervening proceedings.** The application makes no mention whatsoever of ARB.P. No. 1010/2022, the judgment dated 22.03.2024, I.A. No. 39500/2024 or the Supreme Court's judgment dated 13.08.2025 — proceedings to which Defendant No. 5's group was party, in which the family settlement and the Plaintiff's 23% share were repeatedly asserted and acknowledged by the Defendant groups, and which culminated in the direction that rights be worked out in accordance with the order dated 22.03.2024. An Order VII Rule 11 application founded on a supposed 2015/2019 accrual, which suppresses a decade of subsequent acknowledgements and judicial orders pleaded in the plaint itself, is misleading.

5. **Approbation and reprobation.** The Defendant groups (including Defendant No. 5) invoked and defended arbitration under the FSD 2019 — which is founded on the very oral family settlement dated 30.09.2014 — as a live, operative arrangement, and are prosecuting arbitral proceedings thereunder. They cannot simultaneously contend, against the Plaintiff, that the same family settlement was dead by repudiation in 2015 and that all rights under it became unenforceable in 2018. A party cannot treat the settlement as subsisting for its own claims and extinct for the Plaintiff's.

6. **Internal inconsistency of Defendant No. 5's own applications.** In I.A. No. 16113/2026 Defendant No. 5 contends that substantial reliefs of this very plaint are "commercial disputes of specified value" requiring trial by a Commercial Court; in the present application he contends the plaint discloses no cause of action at all and is a dead claim. A claim cannot simultaneously be triable-but-in-another-forum and non-existent/dead. The contradictory threshold attacks are an abuse of process (*Future Sector Land Developers LLP v. Bagmane Developers (P) Ltd.*, (2023) 5 SCC 368).

7. **Clarification regarding the order dated 12.11.2024.** The Plaintiff places no reliance on the order dated 12.11.2024, which stands set aside by the Supreme Court's judgment dated 13.08.2025. The Plaintiff relies on the subsisting judgment dated 22.03.2024 (including the statements of the Defendant groups recorded in paragraphs 17–18 thereof) and on the Supreme Court's judgment dated 13.08.2025 (paragraph 25).

## PARAGRAPH-WISE REPLY ON MERITS

8. **Reply to paragraph 1 of the application** (plaint barred by limitation and disclosing no cause of action; a time-barred enforcement claim in the guise of partition): Denied. The suit is a partition/working-out action on antecedent title by a co-owner in joint and constructive possession (plaint paragraphs 66 and Prayer A), instituted in terms of the liberty recognised by the Supreme Court's judgment dated 13.08.2025. It discloses a complete cause of action and is within limitation.

9. **Reply to paragraph 2 of the application** (reliance only on plaint and documents): The standard invoked condemns the application: on the plaint and documents as they stand — including the pleadings of continuing co-ownership, possession, acknowledgements and the judgments dated 22.03.2024 and 13.08.2025 pleaded therein — no ex facie bar appears.

10. **Reply to paragraph 3 of the application** (Rule 11(d) limitation; alternatively Rule 11(a) no cause of action for partition): Denied. Neither clause is attracted, for the reasons developed below.

11. **Reply to paragraph 4 of the application** (grounds set out below): Needs no reply beyond what follows.

12. **Reply to paragraph 5 of the application** (the plaint is "admittedly" one for enforcement of an oral agreement; "partition" is a label to avoid limitation; no relief claimed as coparcener): Denied. The plaint is not a claim for specific performance of a contract; it pleads that title to the 23% share vested antecedently by the family partition of 30.09.2014, needs no conveyance, and stands acknowledged by the Defendants (Prayer A; plaint paragraph 66). Under *Kale* (supra), a family arrangement operates on antecedent title; working it out by metes and bounds is partition, not contract enforcement. That the Plaintiff does not sue "as a coparcener" under classical Mitakshara law is irrelevant: co-ownership arising from a family arrangement equally sustains a partition action, with the same continuing character.

13. **Reply to paragraph 6 of the application** (plaint paragraphs 1, 7, 10, 11, 12, 15, 17, 46, 49, 51, 59–62, 66 and Prayer A rely on the settlement dated 30.09.2014; most properties held by companies): The plaint's reliance on the family settlement as the *source of antecedent title* is admitted; the inference of "contract enforcement" is denied. As to properties held through companies/entities: the plaint claims the Plaintiff's share in the family corpus including shareholdings and beneficial interests as allocated; the objection that particular assets are held by companies is a defence on merits (and on frame), not a ground under Order VII Rule 11(a)/(d). It is also noteworthy that paragraph 66 — which the Applicant himself cites — is the paragraph pleading continuing co-ownership and joint and constructive possession, which negates the accrual theory of the application.

14. **Reply to paragraph 7 of the application** (not a partition suit of a co-owner or coparcener under Hindu law): Denied. A partition action lies among co-owners whatever the source of co-ownership — inheritance, purchase or family arrangement. The Applicant cites no principle confining partition to coparcenary property, and there is none.

15. **Reply to paragraph 8 of the application** (suit cannot survive without enforcement of the oral agreement; Order XX Rule 18 invocation cannot alter the character of the action): Denied. The action's character is determined by the plaint as a whole: antecedent vested title + continuing co-ownership + joint/constructive possession + partition and consequential reliefs. It is the application which seeks to alter that character by relabelling. In any event, whether the settlement requires "enforcement" or merely "working out" is itself a triable issue unfit for demurrer.

16. **Reply to paragraph 9 of the application** (a party cannot evade limitation by drafting an enforcement suit as a partition suit; limitation runs from breach/refusal): Denied as inapplicable. The premise assumes what it must prove — that the suit is an enforcement action. On the plaint's case (antecedent title; continuing co-ownership), limitation for partition does not run at all until ouster is established; and the alleged breach/refusal is disputed and overlaid by a documented chain of subsequent acknowledgements: the FSD dated 20.06.2015, the MOU dated 09.07.2019 (both of which record the family arrangement and the Plaintiff's 23% share), the Defendants' pleadings in CS(COMM) 1551/2021 and in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022, and the statements recorded in the judgment dated 22.03.2024 *(instances as catalogued in I.A. No. 16110/2026 — counsel to verify each instance against certified copies)*. Successive acknowledgements within Section 18 of the Limitation Act, 1963 furnish fresh starting points even on the Applicant's own (misconceived) contractual theory.

17. **Reply to paragraph 10 of the application** (no declaration sought as to the oral agreement; consequential reliefs not maintainable): Denied. No declaration of the family arrangement is required in law: under *Kale* (supra), a family arrangement — even oral — operates of its own force on antecedent title, and courts lean in favour of upholding it rather than defeating it on technical grounds. A co-owner suing for partition on the basis of an arrangement acknowledged by the Defendants (including in paragraph 18 of the judgment dated 22.03.2024) need not first sue to declare it. The paragraph, moreover, contradicts the application's limitation theory: had the Plaintiff sought a declaration, the Applicant would have invoked Article 58; because he has not, the Applicant says the reliefs fail for want of declaration — an argument designed to be unanswerable either way, which is the hallmark of an unsound objection.

18. **Reply to paragraph 11 of the application** (the plaint itself discloses non-implementation and refusal in 2015; knowledge of breach in 2015): Denied as a mischaracterisation. The plaint pleads dishonest non-implementation of specific components and hostile incidents of November 2015, alongside continuing co-ownership, continuing possession/control of allocated assets, and the Defendants' continuing acknowledgements of the settlement and the 23% share through 2015, 2019, 2021–2024 (paragraph 16 above). Non-implementation of components of a family arrangement does not extinguish the co-ownership it recognises, nor start limitation for partition.

19. **Reply to paragraph 12 of the application** (plaint paragraphs 12, 15, 17–19 and 62 show breach/repudiation known by 06.11.2015, at latest by the MOU dated 09.07.2019): Denied. The cited paragraphs must be read with the whole plaint, including paragraph 62 itself, which pleads a cause of action arising and continuing thereafter — including the Supreme Court proceedings culminating on 13.08.2025, the Defendants' recent obstruction of registration of the Rawta lands sale deed before the SDM Kapashera, and continuing dissipation of corpus assets — and pleads that the cause of action "continues to subsist on a day-to-day basis". Strikingly, the MOU dated 09.07.2019 which the Applicant fixes as the latest accrual date is itself an *acknowledgement* of the family arrangement and the Plaintiff's share — it defeats, not supports, the limitation plea (Section 18, Limitation Act).

20. **Reply to paragraph 13 of the application** (Articles 54, 55 and 58 prescribe three years): The Articles are matters of statute; their applicability is denied. Article 54 governs specific performance of contracts; Article 55 compensation for breach of contract; Article 58 declarations. A partition/working-out suit by a co-owner on antecedent title is governed by none of them; partition being a continuing right, no Article runs until ouster (paragraph 2.2 above).

## ADDITIONAL RESEARCH — NO LIMITATION FOR PARTITION SUITS (IN ANSWER TO THE 3-YEAR OBJECTION)

35. The Applicant's invocation of a 3-year limitation (Articles 54/55/58) is answered by the settled body of law on partition:
35.1. The right to claim partition is a continuing/recurring right which subsists as long as the co-ownership subsists; a co-owner may sue for partition at any time, and no limitation runs against such a right until there is a clear and proven ouster. (Vidya Devi @ Vidya Vati (Dead) by LRs v. Prem Prakash, AIR 1995 SC 1789.)
35.2. In law, the possession of one co-sharer is the possession of all co-sharers; exclusion becomes adverse only upon open assertion of hostile title to the knowledge of the excluded co-owner — a fact which must be pleaded and proved by the party asserting ouster, and which can never be assumed on demurrer. (Vidya Devi, supra; see also the principles under Article 110 of the Limitation Act, 1963 — suit by a person excluded from joint family property to enforce a right to share therein: twelve years from when the exclusion becomes known to the plaintiff — even the Applicant's own ouster theory, if accepted, attracts Article 110's twelve years, not any 3-year Article.)
35.3. A family arrangement does not create title; it recognises antecedent title. A suit to work out shares under a family arrangement is therefore a partition suit on antecedent title, not a suit for enforcement of a contract governed by Articles 54/55/58. (Kale v. Deputy Director of Consolidation, (1976) 3 SCC 119.)
35.4. Partition is a continuing process of working out shares in which the court retains jurisdiction until the final decree; the cause of action of a co-owner for partition is recurring. (Shub Karan Bubna @ Shub Karan Prasad Bubna v. Sita Saran Bubna, (2009) 9 SCC 689.)
35.5. Applying the above: (i) if the suit is what it pleads — partition by a co-owner on antecedent title — no limitation Article bars it absent proven ouster; (ii) if the Applicant's own ouster/exclusion theory is assumed, Article 110 (12 years from knowledge of exclusion) applies, and even on the Applicant's dates (November 2015 / 09.07.2019) the suit of 17.06.2026 is within twelve years; (iii) the 3-year Articles 54/55/58 have no application on any view; and (iv) the chain of acknowledgements through 2015-2025 and the Supreme Court's working-out liberty of 13.08.2025 independently defeat the plea.
