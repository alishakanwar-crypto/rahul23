# PART A — IMPROVEMENTS TO OUR OWN APPLICATIONS

## A-1. Application dated 20.06.2026 (S.151 CPC — updated Schedule-C) [File 05]

**Strengths:** short, focused; anchors the ₹238.50 cr figure to the Defendants' own admitted 2014 partition values; correctly pleads the Bahadurgarh sale (Item 16, ₹8.50 cr) as a fact within the Defendants' exclusive knowledge.

**Improvements:**
1. **Title–prayer mismatch.** The heading promises an "updated Schedule-C", but the prayer does not expressly ask the Court to *take the revised Schedule-C on record*. Add as Prayer (A):
   > "A. Take on record the revised/updated Schedule-C annexed hereto as Annexure-A, reflecting the shifting of Item No. 16 (Sector-16, Bahadurgarh property) from Schedule-B to Schedule-C, and treat the same as part of the plaint in substitution of the earlier Schedule-C;"
2. **Add a disclosure/discovery prayer.** Since the sale particulars lie in the Defendants' exclusive knowledge, add:
   > "B. Direct the Defendants to disclose on affidavit the date of sale, identity of the purchaser(s), actual sale consideration received, and utilisation of the sale proceeds in respect of each property listed in the revised Schedule-C;"
   This converts the application from a bookkeeping exercise into a fact-finding weapon and directly supports IA 16084 and IA 16089.
3. **Tie it expressly to IA 16084 (status quo).** Add a paragraph:
   > "The continuing alienations pleaded herein demonstrate precisely the irreparable injury against which the Plaintiff seeks protection in IA 16084/2026. The present application may therefore be considered along with IA 16084/2026."
4. **Invoke lis pendens (S.52 TPA).** Add a paragraph stating that any transfer of Schedule-B/C properties pendente lite is hit by Section 52, Transfer of Property Act, and seek liberty to implead purchasers/transferees as parties — this deters further sales and puts buyers on notice.
5. **Consistency check:** the entity name for Item 16 appears in one place as "Shree Vishnu Apparels Pvt. Ltd." — verify against the sale record whether it is *Apparels* or *Jewellers*; the L.R. Builders session taught us that entity mix-ups are seized on by the other side.

## A-2. S.35A/35B Application against D-1 Kamal [File 10]

**Strengths:** the *Marg Ltd.*-style two-step reading of O7R11(b)/(c); the Kale special-equity section; the killer approbate/reprobate extraction of Kamal's own Arb.P. 1010/2022 Reply (paras 11, 29, 32: Rahul "has taken control… admittedly controls" the companies).

**Improvements (in priority order):**
1. **CRITICAL — stop calling Para 18 an "undertaking of Defendant No.1."** Para 35(a) says the ouster plea is "irreconcilable with *the undertaking recorded* … in Paragraph 18". D-7/8's O7R11 [File 13, Ground III] attacks exactly this characterisation as a "false statement on oath" — Para 18 records the Court's finding on the *intent* of the FSD, while the party statement is in Para 13/17. Recast every such reference as:
   > "…irreconcilable with (i) the statement of Defendant No.1's own group recorded in Para 13 of the judgment dated 22.03.2024 that the Plaintiff shall not be bound by the award, (ii) the finding in Para 18 thereof that the intent of the FSD was to settle inter se disputes 'while at the same time not affecting the rights and interest of Mr. Rahul Gupta', and (iii) Defendant No.1's own Written Submissions in I.A. 39500/2024 (para 2.7), where Shri Kamal Gupta expressly 'undertakes to abide by both the aforesaid undertakings.'"
   Point (iii) is the shield: it is **Kamal's own document** that calls them "undertakings" — so the label comes from him, not from us. This one change neutralises the opponents' entire "fabricated undertaking" ground before it is ever argued.
2. **Citation corrections:**
   - Para 1/9 references to "SLP 4775/2026" → uniformly "SLP(C) 4775-4779/2025, decided 13.08.2025, 2025 INSC 975".
   - The footnote "Anu v. Suresh Verma [(2011) SCC OnLine Del 2831" has an unclosed bracket — verify and complete the citation before reliance.
3. **Add the two-step authority now available.** File 11 cites *Marg Ltd. v. Sushil Lalwani, 2026 SCC OnLine SC 647* (O7R11(b)/(c) defects are curable; opportunity mandatory); File 10 makes the same argument without the authority. Insert the Marg Ltd. extract into File 10 as well — both applications should run on identical authority.
4. **Strengthen the joint-possession answer to Kamal's ₹81 cr point.** Kamal's O7R11 leans entirely on "ouster pleaded = ad valorem". Add:
   - *Neelavathi v. N. Natarajan, (1980) 2 SCC 247*: possession of one co-owner is in law possession of all; exclusion must be *unequivocal ouster*, and the question of court fee is decided on the plaint's averments taken as a whole;
   - Express cross-reference to plaint paras 95–96 (constructive possession of Karol Bagh and Mumbai properties) and to physical possession of the Rawta lands — i.e. the Plaintiff *is* in possession of part of the corpus, so Article 17(vi), Schedule II, Court Fees Act fixed fee applies to a partition of the composite corpus.
5. **Repair the costs foundation.** S.35A compensatory costs are capped at ₹3,000 (see *Ashok Kumar Mittal v. Ram Kumar Gupta, (2009) 2 SCC 656*) — an application seeking only 35A/35B invites the answer "maximum ₹3,000". Add Section 35 CPC (costs follow the event; actual/realistic costs), the Court's inherent power, and:
   - *Uflex Ltd. v. Govt. of Tamil Nadu, (2022) 1 SCC 165* (actual-costs regime, deterring luxury litigation);
   - *Dnyandeo Sabaji Naik v. Pradnya Prakash Khadekar, (2017) 5 SCC 496* (exemplary costs for abuse of process).
   Amend Prayer B to: "Impose actual and exemplary costs under Sections 35, 35A and 35B CPC read with Section 151 CPC and the inherent powers of this Hon'ble Court, quantified at not less than ₹__ lakh."
6. **Language/OCR-visible drafting fixes** (verify in the Word original): para 30 "now it is merely to restrainment of defendants" — rewrite: "the allocation having already been made, the relief now sought is restraint of the Defendants from interfering with it"; para 33 is garbled ("application acknowledgements and suppresses seeks to create the aforesaid a misleading impression") — rewrite: "The present application suppresses the aforesaid acknowledgements and seeks to create a misleading impression…".

## A-3. S.35A/35B Application against D-5 Mukesh [File 11]

**Strengths:** the "can't have it three ways" inconsistency matrix (D-1: it *is* a partition suit; D-5: it is *not* a partition suit; D-5 again: it is a *commercial* suit); the Marg Ltd. two-step point; *Vidya Devi* (no limitation for partition — running cause of action); S.18 Limitation Act acknowledgements; the composite-corpus answer to the commercial-courts objection.

**Improvements:**
1. **Same Para 18 fix as A-2 (critical).** Para 18 of File 11 says D-5 "gave an undertaking in paragraph 18 of the judgment" — factually Para 18 records the *Court's* finding, and the undertaking language comes from the group's own WS in IA 39500/2024 (para 2.7, already quoted at para 19). Rewrite para 18 to rest on (i) Para 13 statement, (ii) Para 18 finding, (iii) the group's own WS calling them "undertakings". Never plead that D-5 personally "gave an undertaking in paragraph 18" — he was one of Respondents 1–6, and precision here defuses the opponents' perjury/false-statement counter-narrative.
2. **Meet the limitation ground on the correct Article.** D-5 argues Arts. 54/55/58 (3 years). Add the affirmative answer:
   - The suit being one for partition/possession of a share by a co-owner, the governing provision is **Article 110** (exclusion from joint family property — 12 years from when exclusion becomes known) or **Article 65** (possession based on title — 12 years), not Arts. 54/55/58;
   - *Shakti Bhog Food Industries Ltd. v. Central Bank of India, (2020) 17 SCC 260*: limitation is a mixed question of law and fact and ordinarily cannot be decided under O7R11 where the plaint pleads a continuing/renewed cause of action;
   - *Salim D. Agboatwala v. Shamalji Oddhavji Thakkar, (2021) SCC OnLine SC 735*: plaint must be read as a whole; O7R11(d) only where the bar is apparent ex facie.
   - Keep §18 Limitation Act but *list the specific acknowledgements with dates* (FSD 20.06.2015 cl.8; MOU 09.07.2019; Reply in Arb.P. 1010/2022 paras 11/29/32; WS in IA 39500/2024 para 2.7; SC judgment 13.08.2025 para 25) — a dated chain, each within 3/12 years of the previous, is far more powerful than a general averment.
3. **Commercial-courts rebuttal — add the governing authority.** Add *Ambalal Sarabhai Enterprises Ltd. v. K.S. Infraspace LLP, (2020) 15 SCC 585*: "used exclusively in trade or commerce" is to be **strictly construed**; the dispute must *arise out of* the commercial use. A partition claim to an undivided 23% share in a composite family corpus does not "arise out of" the commercial use of three properties within it. This is the exact proposition para 56 gestures at, now with the citation.
4. **Answer the ₹2 crore pecuniary point head-on.** File 08's best point is that the ₹2 cr "alternate properties" valuation has no matching prayer. Add a paragraph expressly mapping plaint para 64's valuation heads to the prayer clauses (in particular the prayer seeking compensation/allotment of equivalent properties from the remaining corpus in lieu of alienated assets), and stating that in any event the aggregate value of reliefs A–C alone (partition of a corpus the Defendants' own 2014 values place in hundreds of crores; possession reliefs Kamal himself values at ₹81 cr) is far above ₹2 cr — D-1's own O7R11 asserts the reliefs are worth ₹81 cr+, which demolishes D-5's "below threshold" plea. **Use each defendant's application against the other.**
5. **Verify the prayer-D citation.** Prayer D cites "Future Sector Land Developers v. Bagmane Developers, (2023) 5 SCC 368" for the proposition that O7R10 and O7R11 applications are a contradiction in terms — verify this citation (name/report) before the next hearing; if unverifiable, rest the point on first principles (rejection presupposes jurisdiction to adjudicate; return presupposes no jurisdiction).
6. **Numbering/typo fixes:** stray "33." embedded inside para 61; Notice of Motion says filed "21-06-2026" while the application is dated 22.06.2026 — harmonise; same costs-quantum upgrade as A-2 point 5 (add S.35 CPC, Uflex, Dnyandeo Naik, and a quantified figure).

## A-4. S.340 CrPC / S.379 BNSS Application in SLP 22163 [File 12]

**Strengths:** the approbate/reprobate proof (their own para-by-para HC applications extract plaint paras 53–55, 66 and prayers A/C/D/E — impossible without having the plaint); service to their own court-disclosed email pawan@ppj.co.in (DHC cause lists 29.01.2024 and 22.07.2024); materiality (non-service is the SLP's foundational ground).

**Improvements:**
1. **CRITICAL — use BNS 2023 sections, not IPC.** The false affidavits were sworn in June 2026, i.e. after 01.07.2024, so the offences are under the **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023**, not the IPC. Prayer (C) currently seeks a complaint under "Sections 191, 192, 193, 199, 200, 209 IPC". Substitute/add the BNS equivalents: **S.227** (giving false evidence), **S.228** (fabricating), **S.229** (punishment), **S.236** (false declaration on oath), **S.237** (using declaration known false), **S.379 BNSS** (procedure). Retain the IPC references only as parenthetical cross-references. A prayer citing repealed provisions is an invitation for a technical objection.
2. **Add the SC's own materiality authorities:** *Sciemed Overseas Inc. v. BOC India Ltd., (2016) 3 SCC 70* (costs for false statement on affidavit in SC); *ABCD v. Union of India, (2020) 2 SCC 52* (scope of S.340 — deliberate falsehood on a matter of substance; expediency of inquiry); *K.D. Sharma v. SAIL, (2008) 12 SCC 481* (suppression → petition dismissed at threshold, uberrima fides in Art. 136). K.D. Sharma also independently supports Prayer (D) (dismissal of the SLP itself).
3. **Fix the index typo** "CS(OS) 545/2025" → "CS(OS) 545/2026" (affidavit-of-service entry, p.22-45) before any further reliance.
4. **Attach the email delivery proofs as distinct annexures** (delivery/read receipts, absence of bounce-backs) rather than relying on the averment "never returned undelivered" — the strongest version of the service case is documentary.
5. **Pre-empt the "we meant formal service" escape.** Add one paragraph: even if D-7/8 contend they meant absence of *formal* service, their sworn statement was "not supplied" and "no opportunity" — which is falsified by their own detailed HC applications; a half-truth in an Art. 136 petition is itself suppression (*K.D. Sharma*).

# PART B — WEAKNESS MAP OF THE OPPONENTS' APPLICATIONS (for our replies)

## B-1. Kamal's O7R11(b)&(c) [File 07]
- **Fatal structural flaw:** O7R11(b)/(c) require the Court to *first* fix a time to correct valuation/pay fee; rejection only on non-compliance (*Marg Ltd., 2026 SCC OnLine SC 647*). The application seeks outright rejection — legally impossible relief.
- **Ouster premise contradicted by his own pleadings** in Arb.P. 1010/2022 (paras 11/29/32: Rahul "admittedly controls" companies) — approbate/reprobate.
- **Ignores plaint paras 95–96** (constructive possession, Karol Bagh/Mumbai) and physical possession of Rawta lands — joint possession of even part of the corpus attracts Art. 17(vi) fixed fee (*Neelavathi*).
- The ₹81 cr Prayer-C computation is *his own admission* of the value of what the group withholds — usable against D-5's pecuniary-jurisdiction plea and in support of exemplary costs.

## B-2. Mukesh's O7R10 [File 08]
- **Internally inconsistent with his own O7R11:** a court without jurisdiction (O7R10) cannot simultaneously be asked to reject the plaint (O7R11) — invoking both is a contradiction in terms.
- **Pecuniary point ignores the aggregate:** valuation is tested on the plaint as a whole; even excluding the contested ₹2 cr head, partition/possession reliefs (which Kamal values at ₹81 cr+) far exceed the DHC threshold. The two defendants' applications refute each other.
- **Commercial-courts point fails Ambalal Sarabhai:** strict construction; a partition of a composite family corpus does not "arise out of" the commercial use of 3 of its properties; the plaint pleads residential, agricultural and corporate assets too.
- No supporting affidavit deficiencies noted; but the application cites **no authority at all** for either ground — reply should highlight this.

## B-3. Mukesh's O7R11(a)&(d) — limitation [File 09]
- Wrong Articles (54/55/58): partition/exclusion claims are governed by Arts. 110/65 (12 years), and *Vidya Devi* holds partition has a running cause of action.
- Ignores S.18 acknowledgements chain (2015 FSD → 2019 MOU → 2022 Arb.P. pleadings → 2024 WS → 13.08.2025 SC) — each re-starts limitation; the SC's 13.08.2025 direction that parties "work out their rights in accordance with the order dated 22.03.2024" freshly enables this suit (plaint para 62).
- "No declaration sought" point is misconceived: a co-owner suing for partition need not seek a declaration of the family arrangement's validity (*Kale* — family arrangements need no registration/formality and can be oral); the share is pleaded as antecedent title, not a contractual right.
- Limitation here is a mixed question (*Shakti Bhog*) — outside O7R11.

## B-4. Pawan & Renu's O7R11 (4 grounds) [File 13]
- Grounds I–II (valuation/limitation) fail for the same reasons as B-1/B-3.
- **Ground III ("Para 18 records no undertaking") is their most dangerous point** — it is answered not by defending the label but by the three-layer chain (Para 13 statement + Para 18 finding + their group's own WS in IA 39500/2024 calling them "undertakings"). Our amended Files 10/11 (Part A) implement exactly this.
- Ground IV (abuse/res judicata via 13.08.2025) is self-defeating: para 25 of 2025 INSC 975 expressly *preserves* the parties' liberty to work out rights per 22.03.2024 — the very foundation of this suit.
- Their standing to seek plaint rejection is undermined by the perjury application [File 12]: parties who swore they never received the plaint filed a para-by-para O7R11 on it.
