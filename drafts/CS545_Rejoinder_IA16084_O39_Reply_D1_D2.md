## REJOINDER ON BEHALF OF THE PLAINTIFF TO THE REPLY DATED 31.07.2026 FILED BY DEFENDANT NOS. 1 AND 2 TO THE APPLICATION UNDER ORDER XXXIX RULES 1 & 2 READ WITH SECTION 151 CPC (I.A. No. 16084/2026)

MOST RESPECTFULLY SHOWETH:

### I. PRELIMINARY SUBMISSIONS

1. That the present Rejoinder is being filed on behalf of the Plaintiff to the Reply dated 31.07.2026 filed by Defendant Nos. 1 and 2 ("the answering Defendants") to the Plaintiff's application under Order XXXIX Rules 1 and 2 read with Section 151 CPC. The contents of the Reply are denied in their entirety, save and except what is specifically admitted hereinafter. The contents of the application under reply (I.A. No. 16084/2026) and of the plaint are reiterated and reaffirmed, and nothing contained herein may be construed as a departure therefrom.

2. **The Reply itself concedes the only fact that matters at the interim stage — an admitted, continuing pattern of alienation of the suit corpus.** The answering Defendants do not deny that properties forming part of the PP Jewellers Family Corpus have been sold, and continue to be sold, right up to the eve of the present suit: Bahadurgarh (13.10.2021), Lajpat Nagar (13.04.2022), Daulatpur (13.04.2023) and Green Park (16.01.2026). Their only justification is that these were "commercial decisions based on necessity taken by the lawful owners" (Reply, Section L). Whether these alienations were bona fide debt repayment or dissipation is quintessentially a triable issue; what stands admitted is that the corpus of the suit is being progressively diminished pendente lite. On this admission alone, the subject-matter of the partition suit requires preservation, and the classical trinity of prima facie case, balance of convenience and irreparable injury stands satisfied. A restraint on further alienation causes no prejudice whatsoever to a Defendant with a bona fide defence; refusal of restraint renders the suit infructuous property by property.

3. **The present suit is the very "working out of rights" contemplated by the Hon'ble Supreme Court.** By the judgment dated 13.08.2025 in SLP (C) Nos. 4775-4779/2025, the Hon'ble Supreme Court, while setting aside the order dated 12.11.2024, expressly left the parties free to work out their rights in accordance with law. The Plaintiff was excluded from the arbitral proceedings arising from ARB.P. No. 1010/2022 precisely because he is a non-signatory to the arbitration agreement; a civil suit for partition is accordingly his only remedy. The plea of res judicata / abuse of process (Reply, Sections G and H, and paras 20–26 of the underlying O7R11 narrative repeated in the Reply) is therefore misconceived: no Court has ever adjudicated, on merits, the Plaintiff's 23% share or the oral family settlement dated 30.09.2014.

4. **The answering Defendants themselves approbate and reprobate within the four corners of their own Reply.** In Section C they contend that no concluded family settlement — oral or written — ever came into existence, and that the FSD 2015 and MOU 2019 remained mere drafts. Yet:
   (a) in Section E they compute the Plaintiff's alleged 23% share of liabilities (₹160.67 crore out of ₹698.54 crore) — a computation possible only if the 23% allocation under the family arrangement is accepted;
   (b) they assert that the Rawta land stood "allotted to the Plaintiff" under the FSD — again invoking the very documents they disown;
   (c) in Section L they justify the sales as acts of "lawful owners", while simultaneously pleading that the allocation of assets "was inextricably linked with the corresponding discharge of liabilities" under "the draft FSD 2015 … and MOU 2019" (Reply, para 47).
   A party cannot blow hot and cold: either the family arrangement exists (in which case the Plaintiff's 23% share and the linked liabilities both fall for accounting at the final decree), or it does not (in which case the entire Section E liabilities edifice collapses). On either footing, the interim preservation of the corpus is warranted.

5. **The "contradictory stands" chart (Reply, Section A/B) misreads the Plaintiff's earlier pleadings.** The extracts relied upon — including I.A. No. 13282/2023 in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022 ("there is no question of any family settlement") — are statements made in the context of the non-implementation and repudiation of the family arrangement by the Defendants, in proceedings where the Defendants' group was seeking to enforce selective portions of the very same arrangement against third parties. A co-sharer who complains that a family settlement has been dishonoured does not thereby abandon his antecedent title. In law, a family settlement does not create title; it recognises and works upon antecedent title — *Kale v. Deputy Director of Consolidation*, (1976) 3 SCC 119. The Plaintiff's status as a co-owner/co-sharer of the family corpus is independent of, and antecedent to, the settlement whose implementation is in dispute.

6. **Limitation (Reply, Section F) is answered by the continuing right to partition.** The right of a co-sharer to seek partition is a continuing right; mere non-participation or exclusion does not extinguish it unless ouster — open, hostile and to the knowledge of the excluded co-sharer — is pleaded and proved, which is a pure question of fact incapable of resolution at the interlocutory stage. The plaint (para 66) expressly pleads that the Plaintiff continues in joint and constructive possession of the corpus; the possession of one co-owner is in law the possession of all. In any event, the plaint pleads repeated acknowledgments and assurances between 2022 and 2025, furnishing a fresh and continuing cause of action. The limitation objection is, moreover, sub judice in the Defendants' own applications under Order VII Rule 11 (I.A. Nos. 16112 and 16114 of 2026) and cannot be a ground to deny interim protection in the meanwhile — indeed, if the Defendants were confident of their limitation plea, the grant of status quo pending its adjudication would prejudice them not at all.

7. **The "assets-with-liabilities" argument (Reply, Section E) is an argument for partition, not against interim relief.** In a partition action, a co-sharer takes his share subject to accounts and equities, which are worked out at the stage of the preliminary and final decrees under Order XX Rule 18 CPC. If Defendant Nos. 1 to 6 have in fact discharged group liabilities of ₹698.54 crore (which is denied and not admitted, and which figure is unsupported by any primary record annexed to the Reply), their remedy is to claim adjustment in the accounting — not to alienate the corpus pendente lite. The Section E table is, in any event, a matter of evidence requiring proof at trial.

8. **The threshold-applications objection (Reply, Sections J/K) is untenable.** The pendency of the answering parties' applications under Order VII Rules 10 and 11 (I.A. Nos. 16112, 16113 and 16114 of 2026) is no bar to the grant of interim protection; it is settled that an application under Order XXXIX can and ought to be considered notwithstanding the pendency of an application under Order VII Rule 11, since the corpus must be preserved so long as the suit is alive. To withhold protection until the threshold applications are decided would hand the Defendants the very licence to alienate that the injunction seeks to prevent.

9. **Urgency (Reply, Section M) is assessed from the standpoint of the Plaintiff and from the Defendants' escalating conduct.** The sale of the Green Park property on 16.01.2026 — mere months before the institution of the suit — coupled with the earlier chain of alienations, demonstrates a live and accelerating threat to the corpus. Urgency is not negated by the fact that earlier sales were not separately challenged; each fresh alienation furnishes a fresh cause and heightens, rather than diminishes, the need for protection.

10. **The allegations concerning M/s Glow Jewellery Pvt. Ltd. and the Rawta land (Reply, Sections H and I) are extraneous to I.A. 16084/2026 and, in any event, distorted.** [COUNSEL VERIFICATION: exact status of the Sale Deed dated 08.06.2026 qua the Rawta land, the status quo orders dated 01.09.2025/11.09.2025 in CP 133/2025, and the correct sequence — to be confirmed from the certified record before this paragraph is finalised.] The proceedings before the NCLT in CP 133/2025 are between different parties and concern the affairs of the company; they neither adjudicate nor extinguish the Plaintiff's share in the family corpus. No suppression arises: the plaint itself discloses the Rawta land and the disputes concerning it.

11. That the answering Defendants have placed reliance on the order dated 22.03.2024 in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022 (Reply, Section G) while suppressing its true effect: the said order records the undertakings/assurances of the Defendants' group (paras 17–18) on the basis whereof interim protection was declined elsewhere, and the Defendants' own group has pleaded in reply to I.A. No. 39500/2024 that "the assurances given in paras 17 and 18 … have been fully adhered to." The Defendants cannot rely upon the order when convenient and disclaim its recorded assurances when inconvenient.

12. That the Reply, running to over a hundred pages, is in substance a written statement on the merits. The multitude of disputed factual questions it raises — the existence and terms of the settlement, the liabilities, the bona fides of the sales, ouster, accounts — are all questions for trial. At the interlocutory stage the only question is whether the subject-matter of the suit should be preserved; on the Defendants' own showing, it should.

### II. PARA-WISE REJOINDER

13. **Re Paras 1–2 (introduction; denial of concluded settlement):** Denied. The oral family settlement of 30.09.2014 is a concluded arrangement acted upon by the parties, as pleaded in the plaint; the FSD 2015 and MOU 2019 are subsequent documents recording/working out the same. The answering Defendants' own invocation of the 23% figure and the FSD allocations (Reply, Sections E, H) belies the denial.

14. **Re Section A/B — "contradictory stands" chart (paras 5–6 and the chart):** Denied as pleaded, and answered in Preliminary Submission 5 above. Each extract relied upon is torn from a context of the Defendants' non-implementation of the settlement. The chart conspicuously omits the Defendants' own contrary pleadings, including their reliance on the FSD/MOU allocations in these very proceedings and before other fora.

15. **Re Section C — no concluded settlement:** Denied. Concluded-ness is the central triable issue; it cannot be resolved in an O.XXXIX reply. The partial performance pleaded in the plaint (allotments acted upon, businesses divided, properties dealt with in accordance with the arrangement) is itself evidence of a concluded arrangement — *Kale* (supra).

16. **Re Section D — "selective corpus" / Intervenor Companies:** Denied. The scheduling of the corpus is a matter of pleading and proof; the Defendants remain free to assert at trial that further assets be included. An alleged under-inclusion of the corpus is no ground to permit alienation of the assets admittedly within it.

17. **Re Section E — ₹698.54 crore liabilities:** Denied and not admitted; the table is unproved and unsupported by primary banking records. Without prejudice, the argument concedes the 23% allocation and is, at its highest, a claim to adjustment in accounts at the final decree (Preliminary Submission 7).

18. **Re Section F — limitation:** Denied; answered in Preliminary Submission 6. The extracts from the application (paras 90, 102, 104) plead deprivation of enjoyment, not extinguishment of title; deprivation of enjoyment by co-sharers gives rise to a continuing cause.

19. **Re Section G — order dated 22.03.2024 and judgment dated 13.08.2025:** Denied as pleaded; answered in Preliminary Submissions 3 and 11. The judgment dated 13.08.2025 set aside the order dated 12.11.2024 and left rights open; it decided nothing against the Plaintiff's share. The costs of ₹3,00,000 pertained to applications in a disposed-of Section 11 petition and are irrelevant to the present suit.

20. **Re Sections H and I — Glow Jewellery / Rawta / 113 Park Street:** Denied as pleaded; answered in Preliminary Submission 10. [COUNSEL VERIFICATION as noted above.]

21. **Re Sections J and K — threshold applications:** Denied; answered in Preliminary Submission 8.

22. **Re Section L — "commercial necessity" sales:** Denied that the sales were bona fide or in the ordinary course; in any event the plea admits the alienations and is a triable defence (Preliminary Submission 2). It is specifically denied that the sale proceeds have been demonstrably applied to group liabilities; no trail is annexed.

23. **Re Section M — urgency:** Denied; answered in Preliminary Submission 9.

24. **Re Section N — prima facie case / irreparable injury / balance of convenience:** Denied. The Plaintiff has a strong prima facie case founded on antecedent co-ownership, the settlement of 30.09.2014, the Defendants' own part-performance and their recorded assurances. Irreparable injury is self-evident where immovable properties of a family corpus are being alienated seriatim; a share in specific immovable property is not adequately compensable in money. The balance of convenience overwhelmingly favours preservation: an order of status quo occasions no loss to owners acting bona fide, whereas its refusal destroys the subject-matter of the suit.

25. **Re the para-wise reply (paras 1–105 of the Reply):** Each paragraph of the para-wise reply is denied save admissions of record expressly noted above; the corresponding paragraphs of the application under reply are reiterated. Specific traverses follow the sectional answers above and are not repeated for the sake of brevity. [COUNSEL NOTE: if the Court requires a paragraph-by-paragraph traverse of all 105 paragraphs, the same can be expanded — instructions awaited.]

26. **Re the Reply to Prayer:** Denied that the reliefs sought amount to final relief. A restraint on alienation and creation of third-party rights pendente lite is the standard protective order in partition actions and decides nothing finally.
