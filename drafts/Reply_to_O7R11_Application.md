Accordingly, the Plaintiff is deemed in law to be in joint possession of the entire PP Jewellers Family Corpus comprising all movable and immovable assets, businesses, entities, jewellery stocks, gold bullion and properties forming part thereof. The present suit is therefore not a suit by an excluded stranger seeking recovery of possession, but a suit by a co-owner and co-sharer in joint and constructive possession seeking partition by metes and bounds, segregation of his admitted 23% share in the PPJ Family Corpus and separate, peaceful and exclusive possession of the assets ultimately found to fall to his share."*

**4.** It is further submitted that the Defendant No. 1, in his affidavit in support of the Application, has stated that *"nothing material has been concealed"* therefrom. ## ON THE MERITS: THE APPLICATION IS LEGALLY UNTENABLE

### A. The Plaint Expressly Pleads Constructive Joint Possession

**5.** Under Order VII Rule 11, the Court is required to look at the **averments in the Plaint alone**. The Plaint, when read as a whole, unambiguously and categorically asserts:

   (a) The Plaintiff continues to remain a co-owner and co-sharer in the PPJ Family Corpus (Para 603);

   (b) The Plaintiff is deemed to be in joint and constructive possession of the entire PPJ Family Corpus (Para 603);

   (c) The Plaintiff is in **physical possession** of properties situated at Village Rawta, New Delhi, owned by Glow Jewellery Pvt Ltd (Para 574 of the Plaint);

   (d) The properties bearing Nos. 2129-2130, Gurudwara Road, Karol Bagh, New Delhi and Mama Parmanand Road, Mumbai are under the Plaintiff's **constructive possession and beneficial enjoyment** (Para 575 of the Plaint);

   (e) The present suit is "not a suit by an excluded stranger seeking recovery of possession, but a suit by a co-owner and co-sharer in joint and constructive possession seeking partition by metes and bounds" (Para 603).

**6.** The Application cannot overcome these express pleadings by citing paragraphs describing the Plaintiff's departure from the family residence in November 2015. The **distinction between being forced to leave the family HOME after a physical assault and being "ousted" from one's co-ownership rights** is fundamental and well-recognised in law. A co-owner's constructive possession of joint estate subsists regardless of whether he physically resides at one of the properties.

### B. "Ouster from Family Residence" ≠ "Loss of Co-ownership"

**7.** The Application's central fallacy lies in conflating two legally distinct concepts:

   (a) **Physical exclusion from the family residence** (C-19, Rana Pratap Bagh) following violent assault — which the Plaint narrates as factual background; and

   (b) **Ouster from co-ownership position** in the PPJ Family Corpus — which the Plaint **does not** assert and **expressly negates** in Para 603.

**8.** In law, a co-owner's title and constructive possession in joint property subsists until there is a formal partition or a decree declaring otherwise. The Plaintiff being compelled to leave one residential property after a physical assault does not extinguish his rights in a corpus comprising dozens of properties, businesses, jewellery stocks and assets. It is submitted that:

   - **Suraj Bhan v. Financial Commissioner, (2007) 6 SCC 186:** A co-sharer's rights in joint family property are not extinguished by exclusion from physical possession; constructive possession continues.

   - **Dorab Cawasji Warden v. Coomi Sorab Warden, (1990) 2 SCC 117:** Even where a co-owner is out of physical possession of a specific property, his undivided interest and constructive possession in the joint estate subsists.

### C. Fixed Court Fee in Partition Suit — Settled Law

**9.** In a suit for partition where the Plaintiff claims as a co-owner and seeks partition and separate possession, only **fixed court fee** is payable under Article 17(vi) of Schedule II of the Court Fees Act, 1870. The mere fact that the Plaintiff also seeks delivery of separate possession **does not convert a partition suit into a "suit for possession" requiring ad valorem court fee**. Every partition suit, by its very nature, seeks separate possession — that is the essential relief in partition.

   - **Satheedevi v. Prasanna Kumar, (2010) 5 SCC 601:** In a suit for partition, the plaintiff who claims as a co-owner is required to pay only a fixed court fee. The mere fact that the plaintiff also seeks delivery of separate possession does not make it a suit for "possession" requiring ad valorem court fee.

   - **Meenakshisundaram v. Venkatachalam, (1979) 3 SCC 481:** Where the plaintiff sues for partition as a co-owner, court fee is payable under Schedule II, Article 17(vi) regardless of whether separate possession is also sought.

**10.** The Application's reliance on Prayer C (which seeks delivery of 7 identified properties valued at ~Rs. 81 Crores) is misconceived. Prayer A — which is the **dominant and primary relief** — seeks a preliminary decree for partition under Order XX Rule 18 CPC. Prayer C is consequential and incidental to the partition decree and does not independently attract ad valorem court fee. The Plaintiff's own framing in Prayer C makes this abundantly clear:

> *"the Plaintiff's title thereto having always resided in him and no conveyance being required to pass the same; Since title always resided in Plaintiff, this is enforcement of vested possession, not fresh acquisition"*

### D. Even if Deficiency Exists — Section 149 CPC, Not Rejection

**11.** Without prejudice to the foregoing submissions that no court fee deficiency exists, it is submitted that **even assuming arguendo** that this Hon'ble Court forms a view that additional court fee ought to be paid, the proper remedy is:

   (a) Under **Section 149 CPC**, which empowers the court to allow deficient court fee to be made good;

   (b) The Plaintiff has **already filed an Application under Section 149 CPC** (IA 16085/2026) seeking enlargement of time for court fee, which was **allowed by this Hon'ble Court on 19.06.2026**;

   (c) The Plaintiff **undertakes** to pay any additional court fee as may be determined and directed by this Hon'ble Court;

   (d) Under Order VII Rule 11(c) itself, the court **returns** the plaint for amendment — it does not reject the suit with finality.

**Case Law:**
   - **Noor Mohammad v. Jethanand, (2013) 5 SCC 202:** Where court fee is found insufficient, the court should ordinarily permit the plaintiff to make good the deficiency rather than rejecting the plaint.
   - **Shah Babulal Khimji v. Jayaben D. Kania, (1981) 4 SCC 8:** The power under O.VII R.11 is a drastic one and should be exercised sparingly.

### E. O.VII R.11 is a Drastic Power — Cannot Be Used for Delay

**12.** It is respectfully submitted that rejection of a plaint under Order VII Rule 11 is a **drastic and exceptional remedy** that shuts out the Plaintiff from access to justice. The settled legal position is:

   - **T. Arivandandam v. T.V. Satyapal, (1977) 4 SCC 467:** The power under O.VII R.11 should be exercised with circumspection and caution.
   - **Dahiben v. Arvindbhai Kalyanji Bhanusali, (2020) 7 SCC 366:** For O.VII R.11, averments in plaint must be taken at face value; the court cannot dissect plaint allegations to arrive at a conclusion contrary to what the plaint itself states.
   - **Sopan Sukhdeo Sable v. Assistant Charity Commissioner, (2004) 3 SCC 137:** The plaint has to be read as a whole; the court cannot cull out individual sentences in isolation to determine the suit's character.

**13.** The timing of the Application (filed 20.06.2026, just one day after notice was issued on 19.06.2026 and before any hearing on the Plaintiff's urgent interim applications) reveals its true purpose — to **delay and obstruct** the hearing of IA 16084 (status quo), IA 16087 (auditor), IA 16088 (disclosure) and IA 16089 (maintenance). While the Defendant files court fee objections, the PPJ Corpus continues to be dissipated — one more property (Plot No. 15, Sector-16, Bahadurgarh) has already been sold, as discovered from the 2024 Balance Sheet of Shree Vishnu Apparels Pvt. Ltd.

### F. The Defendants' Own Admissions Destroy Their Court Fee Argument

**14.** The Application's argument that the Plaintiff should be treated as an "excluded stranger" is belied by the **Defendants' own conduct and admissions**:

   (a) The Defendants acknowledged the Plaintiff's 23% share in the Oral Family Settlement dated 30.09.2014;

   (b) The Memorandum dated 20.06.2015 records the 23% allocation;

   (e) The Hon'ble Supreme Court in SLP(C) 4775/2025 directed the parties to work out their rights in accordance with the order dated 22.03.2024;

   (f) Defendant No. 1 in his written submissions in IA 39500/2024 expressly stated: *"Shri Kamal Gupta undertakes to abide by both the aforesaid undertakings"*;

   (g) Defendant No. 1 before the Arbitral Tribunal has acknowledged the 23% share and the right to vault of choice.

A party who has repeatedly admitted the Plaintiff's co-ownership and allocated share cannot now contend that the same Plaintiff is an "excluded stranger" for court fee purposes.
