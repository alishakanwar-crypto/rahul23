11B. #### E. The 2014 Oral Family Arrangement, the 2015 Memorandum and the 2019 MOU — the *Kale* principle as applied by this Hon'ble Court in ARB.P. 1010/2022

14. The factual sequence concerning the PPJ family arrangement, which forms the foundation of the present suit, is as follows:

   (i) **2014** — On 30.09.2014, the PPJ family arrived at an **oral family arrangement/partition**, whereunder, inter alia, a 23% share in the PPJ family corpus stood allocated to the Plaintiff. The arrangement stood concluded and acted upon;

   (ii) **2015** — On 20.06.2015, the family/Defendants prepared an alleged **memorandum** recording / purporting to record the said already-concluded oral arrangement;

   (iii) **2019** — On 09.07.2019, the family entered into a **Memorandum of Understanding (MOU)** which, significantly, contained **modifications** to the earlier concluded arrangement.

15. It is respectfully submitted that this very sequence must be examined in light of the law laid down in ***Kale & Ors. v. Deputy Director of Consolidation & Ors.*, (1976) 3 SCC 119**, as cited and applied by this Hon'ble Court in its judgment dated 22.03.2024 in ARB.P. 1010/2022 — proceedings between the same family — wherein it was held (paragraph 23):

   > "Further, it is well settled that unlike commercial contracts which focus on business interests, family settlements aim to preserve peace and goodwill within the family. This social objective is recognized by courts, and it has been held in umpteen judgments that it is the duty of the courts to uphold and give full effect to such an arrangement instead of disturbing the same on technical or trivial grounds. In *Kale v. Dy. Director of Consolidation* (supra), it has been held as under:
   >
   > '9. ... By virtue of a family settlement or arrangement members of a family descending from a common ancestor or a near relation seek to sink their differences and disputes, settle and resolve their conflicting claims or disputed titles **once for all** in order to buy peace of mind and bring about complete harmony and goodwill in the family. The family arrangements are governed by a special equity peculiar to themselves and would be enforced if honestly made. ... The courts have, therefore, leaned in favour of upholding a family arrangement instead of disturbing the same on technical or trivial grounds. Where the courts find that the family arrangement suffers from a legal lacuna or a formal defect **the rule of estoppel is pressed into service and is applied to shut out plea of the person who being a party to family arrangement seeks to unsettle a settled dispute** and claims to revoke the family arrangement under which he has himself enjoyed some material benefits. ...
   >
   > 19. Thus it would appear from a review of the decisions analysed above that the courts have taken a very liberal and broad view of the validity of the family settlement and have always tried to uphold it and maintain it. **The central idea in the approach made by the courts is that if by consent of parties a matter has been settled, it should not be allowed to be reopened by the parties to the agreement on frivolous or untenable grounds.**'"

    The same order further records (paragraph 24) that in *K.K. Modi v. K.N. Modi*, a family settlement regarding division of assets *"should not be lightly interfered with by a Court especially when it has been substantially acted upon by the parties."*

16. The ratio of *Kale*, as cited in this Hon'ble Court's own order dated 22.03.2024, is therefore that a family arrangement, once concluded, settles the rights of the members **"once for all"**; it is **final and binding**; a party who has taken benefit under it is **estopped** from resiling from or unsettling it; and a settled family arrangement **cannot be reopened**. It necessarily follows that no unilateral amendments or modifications are permissible to a concluded family arrangement. Consequently:

   (i) the Plaintiff's 23% share stood crystallised under the concluded oral family arrangement of 30.09.2014;

   (ii) the 2015 memorandum, being merely a record of the anterior concluded arrangement, neither created nor could vary those rights; and

   (iii) the **modifications** sought to be introduced by the 2019 MOU must be examined against the *Kale* principle — the Defendants, having taken benefit under the concluded 2014 arrangement, could not, by a subsequent MOU, modify or whittle down the Plaintiff's crystallised share; any such modification to the Plaintiff's prejudice is impermissible in law and does not bind him, the Plaintiff not even being a signatory to the said MOU.

17. The facts set out above are subsequent events and material facts necessary for the just and effective adjudication of the pending applications. This Hon'ble Court has ample power under Section 151 CPC to take notice of subsequent events in order to shorten litigation and do complete justice between the parties.

18. This application is bona fide and in the interest of justice. No prejudice shall be caused to the Defendants if it is allowed.

**Part C — No reply to IA 16084 despite notice:**

- If D-7 seeks time to reply on Monday, press that any time be granted only **subject to interim protection** (status quo on Schedule-B + stock restraint/disclosure) operating in the meanwhile — otherwise the SLP-and-withdraw device will have succeeded in buying yet another cycle.

**Part E — 2014 arrangement / 2015 memorandum / 2019 MOU modifications (Kale):**

GNCTD*, W.P.(C) 14448/2025, decided 01.07.2026 by the same Bench: a document recording a prior settlement is not itself a partition).
- Caution: do not invite a merits adjudication of the 2019 MOU's validity at the interim stage — deploy Part E only for the *prima facie* threshold and to blunt the maintainability attack.
