## REPLY ON BEHALF OF THE PLAINTIFF TO I.A. No. 16112/2026, BEING THE APPLICATION UNDER ORDER VII RULE 11 READ WITH SECTION 151 CPC FILED BY DEFENDANT NO. 1 (MR. KAMAL GUPTA) SEEKING REJECTION OF THE PLAINT FOR ALLEGED UNDERVALUATION AND INSUFFICIENCY OF COURT FEE [ORDER VII RULE 11(b) AND (c) CPC]

MOST RESPECTFULLY SHOWETH:

## INTRODUCTORY SUBMISSIONS

1. The present Reply is filed on behalf of the Plaintiff to the application of Defendant No. 1 under Order VII Rule 11(b) and (c) CPC read with the Court Fees Act, 1870, seeking rejection of the plaint on the ground of alleged undervaluation and insufficiency of court fee. The application is misconceived, is founded upon a selective and misleading reading of the plaint, suppresses the Applicant's own prior pleadings and the undertakings/statements recorded in judicial orders binding on the Applicant, and deserves dismissal with exemplary costs. The Plaintiff has separately filed I.A. No. 16111/2026 under Sections 35A, 35B and 151 CPC seeking exemplary costs against Defendant No. 1 for filing the present misleading application, and the contents thereof may be read as part of this Reply.

2. The application rests on a single foundational premise — that the plaint pleads "complete ouster" and does not plead joint or constructive possession — and that premise is demonstrably false on the face of the plaint. Paragraph 66 of the plaint expressly pleads:

   > "Without prejudice to the disputes regarding implementation of the Oral Family Settlement, the Plaintiff submits that he continues to remain a co-owner and co-sharer in the entire PP Jewellers Family Corpus and is deemed to be in joint and constructive possession thereof... the Plaintiff has at all material times remained in possession, control and management of certain assets and businesses forming part of his allocated share... The present suit is therefore not a suit by an excluded stranger seeking recovery of possession, but a suit by a co-owner and co-sharer in joint and constructive possession seeking partition by metes and bounds..."

   The application nowhere refers to paragraph 66. An application under Order VII Rule 11, which is required by settled law to be tested on a reading of the plaint as a whole, cannot be built by suppressing the very paragraph of the plaint which pleads the fact the Applicant says is absent.

3. Even otherwise, and without prejudice, the law under Order VII Rule 11(b) and (c) CPC is that rejection is not the first step: the Court must first form an opinion on valuation/court fee, then require the Plaintiff to correct the valuation or supply the requisite stamp paper within a time fixed by the Court, and rejection can follow only upon failure to comply. The Hon'ble Supreme Court has reiterated this two-step statutory scheme in *Marg Ltd. v. Sushil Lalwani*, 2026 SCC OnLine SC 647. The plaint itself, in paragraph 65, contains the Plaintiff's undertaking "to make good any deficient court fee if this Hon'ble Court directs". The prayer for outright rejection is therefore contrary to the statute itself.

## PRELIMINARY OBJECTIONS

4. **Suppression of paragraph 66 and connected possession pleadings.** The Applicant's entire case (paragraphs 7, 10, 11, 15, 16, 17 and 19 of the application) is that the plaint contains no plea of joint/constructive possession. This is falsified by paragraph 66 of the plaint (quoted above), by the plaint's pleadings regarding the Plaintiff's physical possession of the Rawta lands and constructive possession/beneficial enjoyment of the Karol Bagh and Mumbai properties *(plaint paragraphs 95–96 — counsel to verify exact paragraph numbers from the filed plaint)*, and by Prayer B itself. The application is founded on suppression and mischaracterisation of material pleadings.

5. **The Applicant's own prior pleadings contradict the "complete ouster" premise.** In his Reply/Objections filed in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022, Defendant No. 1's own group pleaded that the Plaintiff had *"taken control of the assets of the Joint Family Group"* and was *"in control of multiple businesses mentioned in Annexure A to the 2019 MOU"* (paragraph 11), that the Plaintiff was in *"admitted control of the companies mentioned in Annexure A to the MOU and that of other Commercial Entities"* (paragraph 29), and that the Plaintiff *"admittedly controls these companies"* (paragraph 32) *(paragraph references as extracted in I.A. No. 16111/2026; counsel to verify against the certified copy of the said Reply)*. A party who has pleaded on oath in earlier judicial proceedings that the Plaintiff controls substantial assets of the family corpus cannot, in a later proceeding, assert that the Plaintiff is completely ousted from possession of the entire corpus — least of all for the collateral purpose of a court-fee objection. The application offends the rule against approbation and reprobation.

6. **The issues concerning the Plaintiff's rights in the family corpus have already been dealt with in earlier proceedings.** The Applicant participated in (i) the intervention proceedings in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022, decided by judgment dated 22.03.2024, in which this Hon'ble Court recorded (paragraph 17) the categorical statement of senior counsel for both groups that none of the properties owned by the intervenor companies would be dealt with/divided in the arbitration, and held (paragraph 18) that "the intent of Pawan Gupta Group and Kamal Gupta Group when they executed FSD, was to settle their inter se disputes while at the same time not affecting the rights and interest of Mr. Rahul Gupta"; (ii) the recall proceedings in I.A. No. 39500/2024; and (iii) the proceedings before the Hon'ble Supreme Court culminating in the judgment dated 13.08.2025 in SLP(C) No. 4775/2025 and connected matters, wherein the Hon'ble Supreme Court, while setting aside the order dated 12.11.2024, expressly held (paragraph 25): "The parties to the present proceedings are free to work out their rights in accordance with the order dated 22.03.2024." The present suit is that very working-out of rights. Having invited and obtained that disposition, the Applicant cannot obstruct the working-out suit at the threshold on a contrived court-fee objection.

7. **Clarification regarding the order dated 12.11.2024.** The Plaintiff does not rely upon the order dated 12.11.2024 as a subsisting order; it stands set aside by the judgment of the Hon'ble Supreme Court dated 13.08.2025. What the Plaintiff relies upon is (i) the judgment dated 22.03.2024 in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022, which remains fully operative; (ii) the statements/undertakings of the Defendant groups recorded therein; and (iii) the Hon'ble Supreme Court's direction of 13.08.2025 that parties are free to work out their rights in accordance with the order dated 22.03.2024.

8. **Questions of ouster are questions of fact.** Whether the Plaintiff stands ousted in the legal sense — hostile animus, exclusive possession to his knowledge and exclusion from co-ownership rights (the very test the Applicant sets out in paragraph 15 of the application) — is a mixed question of law and fact requiring evidence; it cannot be conclusively decided at the Order VII Rule 11 stage against the tenor of the plaint, which pleads continuing co-ownership, joint and constructive possession and continuing acknowledgements by the Defendants.

## PARAGRAPH-WISE REPLY ON MERITS

9. **Reply to paragraph 1 of the application** (application filed under Order VII Rule 11(b) and (c) CPC alleging the suit is not properly valued and insufficiently stamped): Denied. The suit is properly valued and the court fee paid is in accordance with law. The plaint (paragraph 64) sets out a relief-wise valuation table; the principal relief of partition/working out of the Plaintiff's 23% share is by a co-owner in joint and constructive possession (plaint paragraph 66) and is valued under Article 17(vi) of Schedule II of the Court Fees Act, 1870 with fixed court fee, as is settled for partition suits by co-owners in joint/constructive possession. In any event, rejection under clauses (b) and (c) can arise only upon failure to comply with a court-fixed time for correction — no such opinion has been formed nor time fixed.

10. **Reply to paragraph 2 of the application** (application stated to be based on the pleadings in the plaint, reserving rights): The Applicant's professed reliance "on the averments contained in the Plaint" is belied by his omission of paragraph 66 and the possession pleadings of the plaint. An application which invokes the plaint-only standard while suppressing the decisive paragraphs of the plaint is an abuse of process.

11. **Reply to paragraph 3 of the application** (suit described as one for partition and enforcement of the oral family arrangement dated 30.09.2014, seeking separate possession, compensation and consequential reliefs): The description is incomplete and misleading. The suit is a suit for partition and working out of the Plaintiff's antecedent and crystallised 23% share in the PPJ Family Corpus by way of a preliminary decree under Order XX Rule 18 CPC (Prayer A), in terms of the Hon'ble Supreme Court's direction dated 13.08.2025 that parties work out their rights in accordance with the order dated 22.03.2024. The reliefs of possession/compensation are consequential to partition, not freestanding recovery-of-possession claims.

12. **Reply to paragraphs 4 to 6 of the application** (recital of the alleged 2014 arrangement, the 2015 incidents, the Memorandum dated 20.06.2015 and the Plaintiff's leaving the family residence): The contents of the plaint speak for themselves. It is however denied that the plaint pleads that the Plaintiff "has ever since been kept out and denied access" to the entire estate. The plaint pleads dishonest non-implementation by the Defendants of specific components of the settlement and physical exclusion from specific premises, *while simultaneously pleading* (paragraph 66) that the Plaintiff has at all material times remained in possession, control and management of certain assets and businesses forming part of his allocated share and remains in joint and constructive possession of the corpus. Exclusion from a residence or from particular business premises is not, in law, ouster from co-ownership of the family corpus.

13. **Reply to paragraph 7 of the application** (assertion that the Plaintiff proceeds on the footing of ouster and exclusion, so cannot claim constructive joint possession for court-fee purposes): Denied. The premise is contrary to the plaint read as a whole. It is settled that for court fee, the plaint averments alone are to be seen (*Saroj Salkan v. Capt. Sanjeev Singh*, 2008 SCC OnLine Del 1278); the plaint expressly pleads joint and constructive possession (paragraph 66) and actual/constructive possession of specific properties. Moreover, in law the possession of one co-sharer is the possession of all co-sharers, and exclusion must amount to a clear and hostile ouster before the fiction of constructive possession ceases; whether it does is a matter of evidence, not demurrer.

14. **Reply to paragraph 8 of the application** (statement of law on Article 17(vi) Schedule II versus Section 7 of the Court Fees Act): The legal propositions, in the abstract, are not disputed, but their application is: the plaint pleads joint and constructive possession, and hence fixed court fee under Article 17(vi) is properly paid. The Applicant's own authority-framework thus supports the Plaintiff once the suppressed paragraph 66 is restored to the analysis.

15. **Reply to paragraph 9 of the application** (reference must be confined to the plaint averments alone): Admitted as a proposition of law — and it is precisely on this test that the application fails, because the plaint averments include paragraph 66, the possession pleadings and Prayer B, which the Applicant has withheld from the Court.

16. **Reply to paragraph 10 of the application** (assertion that "the plaint does not state that the Plaintiff is in joint possession of any of the property"): Emphatically denied as factually false. Paragraph 66 of the plaint states in terms that the Plaintiff "is deemed to be in joint and constructive possession" of the entire PPJ Family Corpus and "has at all material times remained in possession, control and management of certain assets and businesses forming part of his allocated share". The plaint also pleads the Plaintiff's physical possession of the Rawta lands and constructive possession of the Karol Bagh and Mumbai properties *(plaint paragraphs 95–96 — counsel to verify)*. Paragraph 10 of the application is a direct misstatement of the record, made to a Court of law, and is itself a ground for exemplary costs (see I.A. No. 16111/2026).

17. **Reply to paragraph 11 of the application** (constructive possession cannot be invoked once there is a clear plea of exclusion and ouster): Denied as an incorrect and incomplete statement of law as applied here. There is no plea of ouster in the legal sense in the plaint; there are pleas of non-implementation and physical exclusion from particular premises coupled with express pleas of continuing co-ownership and joint/constructive possession. Whether the Defendants' conduct amounts to ouster is their defence, to be established at trial; a defendant's defence cannot be the basis of rejection of a plaint under Order VII Rule 11.

18. **Reply to paragraph 12 of the application** (Prayer B — Karol Bagh and Mumbai properties — should have been separately valued ad valorem): Denied. Prayer B seeks confirmation and protection of the Plaintiff's *existing constructive possession* with a permanent injunction — it does not seek recovery of possession from the Defendants. A relief of injunction protecting existing possession is valued as pleaded and does not attract ad valorem court fee on the market value of the properties. The plaint's valuation table (paragraph 64) separately values this injunctive relief and court fee has been paid thereon. The Applicant's argument again rests on converting the plaint's pleas of constructive possession into their opposite.

19. **Reply to paragraph 13 of the application** (Prayer C — seven properties aggregating approximately Rs. 81 crores — must be valued ad valorem): Denied. Prayer C is expressly consequential upon the partition/working-out relief: the prayer itself recites that the Plaintiff's "title thereto having always resided in him and no conveyance being required to pass the same; Since title always resided in Plaintiff, this is enforcement of vested possession, not fresh acquisition", and delivery of separate possession of allocated properties is the ordinary consequence of a preliminary and final decree of partition under Order XX Rule 18 CPC. In a co-owner's partition suit with pleaded joint/constructive possession, the relief of separate possession of the share on partition does not convert the suit into one for recovery of possession requiring ad valorem fees on the entire value of the properties. The disclosure of approximate values in Prayer C reflects candour in pleading, not an admission of ouster. Without prejudice, even if this Hon'ble Court were ultimately to take a different view on any head of valuation, the plaint contains the Plaintiff's undertaking (paragraph 65) to make good any deficiency as directed, and the statutory consequence would be an opportunity to pay, not rejection (*Marg Ltd.*, supra).

20. **Reply to paragraph 14 of the application** (Plaintiff bound to value possession relief at market value under Section 7 and cannot avoid it by styling the suit as partition): Denied. It is not the Plaintiff's nomenclature but the substance of the plaint — antecedent title, continuing co-ownership, joint and constructive possession, partition and working out under Order XX Rule 18 CPC — which attracts fixed court fee under Article 17(vi) of Schedule II. The Applicant's submission inverts the settled rule that the plaint must be read as a whole and as it stands.

21. **Reply to paragraphs 15 and 16 of the application** (ingredients of ouster said to be satisfied on the plaint's own averments): Denied. The ingredients of ouster set out by the Applicant himself — declaration of hostile animus, long uninterrupted exclusive possession, open exercise of exclusive ownership to the Plaintiff's knowledge — are questions of fact par excellence, incapable of determination on demurrer. The plaint does not concede these ingredients; it pleads the contrary (paragraph 66). Moreover, the Applicant's own pleadings in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022 (paragraphs 11, 29 and 32 of his group's Reply, asserting the Plaintiff's control of group assets and companies) contradict the very ouster he now asks this Hon'ble Court to find. The Applicant cannot be permitted to plead possession and control by the Plaintiff when it suited him (to defeat the Plaintiff's intervention in the arbitration) and complete ouster of the Plaintiff when that suits him (to defeat this suit at the threshold). This is approbation and reprobation and is barred.

22. **Reply to paragraph 17 of the application** (no ad valorem fee paid on the 23% share despite the Plaintiff "treating himself as ousted"): Denied. The Plaintiff does not treat himself as ousted; the plaint pleads joint and constructive possession. The valuation and court fee are in accordance with law for a co-owner's partition suit. The balance of the paragraph is repetitive of paragraphs 7–16 and is denied for the reasons already stated.

23. **Reply to paragraph 18 of the application** (Order VII Rule 11(b) and (c) mandate rejection): Denied as an incomplete statement of law. Clauses (b) and (c) themselves provide that rejection follows only where the plaintiff, "on being required by the Court" to correct the valuation or supply the requisite stamp-paper "within a time to be fixed by the Court", fails to do so. No such requirement or time-fixing exists here; the plaint moreover contains an express undertaking (paragraph 65) to make good any deficiency. The prayer for outright rejection is contrary to the statutory text and to *Marg Ltd. v. Sushil Lalwani*, 2026 SCC OnLine SC 647.

24. **Reply to paragraph 19 of the application** (Plaintiff "admits lack of possession"): Emphatically denied. There is no such admission anywhere in the plaint; the assertion is an invention. The plaint pleads the opposite (paragraph 66; possession pleadings; Prayers A–C read together).

25. **Reply to paragraph 20 of the application** (plaint not in consonance with the Court Fees Act and liable to be rejected): Denied for all the reasons aforesaid.

26. **Reply to paragraph 21 of the application** (application bona fide; prejudice to the Applicant): Denied. The application is mala fide; it suppresses paragraph 66 of the plaint and the Applicant's own prior pleadings, misstates the record (paragraph 10 of the application), and is part of a coordinated set of threshold applications by the Defendant groups (I.A. Nos. 16112, 16113 and 16114 of 2026) aimed at stalling the working-out suit which the Hon'ble Supreme Court's judgment dated 13.08.2025 expressly left the Plaintiff free to pursue. No prejudice is caused to the Applicant by the suit proceeding; grave prejudice is caused to the Plaintiff by threshold obstruction of his suit.

27. **Reply to the prayer of the application**: For the reasons aforesaid, the prayer for rejection of the plaint is unsustainable and deserves to be dismissed with exemplary costs.

## EARLIER PROCEEDINGS AND CONTRADICTORY STANDS OF DEFENDANT NO. 1 (ISSUE-WISE MAPPING)

28. **Objection (i): "The Plaintiff is completely ousted and not in possession of any property" (application paragraphs 7, 10, 11, 15–17, 19).**
    - Earlier stand: Defendant No. 1's group's Reply in ARB.P. No. 1010/2022 pleaded that the Plaintiff had "taken control of the assets of the Joint Family Group", was "in control of multiple businesses mentioned in Annexure A to the 2019 MOU", was in "admitted control of the companies mentioned in Annexure A to the MOU and that of other Commercial Entities", and "admittedly controls these companies" (paragraphs 11, 29 and 32 of the said Reply, *as extracted in I.A. No. 16111/2026 — counsel to verify against the certified copy*).
    - Proceeding: ARB.P. No. 1010/2022 (intervention proceedings), decided 22.03.2024.
    - Analysis: The present objection is directly contrary to the earlier sworn stand; the Applicant approbates and reprobates; he is estopped from asserting complete ouster. Independent merits answer: the plaint pleads joint/constructive possession (paragraph 66), and ouster is a triable issue of fact.

29. **Objection (ii): "The suit is merely one for enforcement of an allocation, requiring ad valorem fees" (application paragraphs 3, 14, 19).**
    - Earlier stand/proceeding: In ARB.P. No. 1010/2022 the Kamal Gupta Group procured disposal of the petition on the recorded statement (judgment dated 22.03.2024, paragraphs 17–18) that the arbitration would not affect the rights and interest of Mr. Rahul Gupta; in the recall proceedings (I.A. No. 39500/2024) the Kamal Gupta Group's written submissions reiterated that the order dated 22.03.2024 was based on the submission that Rahul Gupta would not be bound by the award and that his rights would not be affected *(written submissions, paragraph 2.7, as extracted in I.A. No. 16110/2026 — counsel to verify)*; and before the Hon'ble Supreme Court the Applicant's group obtained the judgment dated 13.08.2025 whose paragraph 25 leaves the parties "free to work out their rights in accordance with the order dated 22.03.2024".
    - Analysis: Having excluded the Plaintiff from the arbitration on the assurance that his rights would be worked out separately, and having obtained from the Hon'ble Supreme Court a disposition that rights be worked out in accordance with the 22.03.2024 order, Defendant No. 1 cannot now recharacterise the working-out suit as a mere money/possession claim to impose a prohibitive court-fee burden at the threshold. Independent merits answer: the suit is a partition/working-out action on antecedent title (Prayer A; Order XX Rule 18 CPC), attracting fixed fee under Article 17(vi) Schedule II.

30. **Objection (iii): "Prayer C properties worth ~Rs. 81 crores require ad valorem fees" (application paragraph 13).**
    - Earlier stand/proceeding: The allocation of specific properties to the Plaintiff's share stands acknowledged in the Defendants' own statements recorded in paragraph 18 of the judgment dated 22.03.2024 (as pleaded in Prayer C itself).
    - Analysis: Delivery of allocated properties is consequential partition relief; the acknowledgment of allocation by the Defendant groups is inconsistent with the assertion that the Plaintiff is an ousted stranger who must sue for recovery of possession ad valorem. Independent merits answer: as in paragraph 19 above; without prejudice, any deficiency is curable under the two-step scheme (*Marg Ltd.*) and stands covered by the plaint's paragraph 65 undertaking.
